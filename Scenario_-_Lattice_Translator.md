## Critical Timeline Note

## Political Context

**The debate has moved on.** The question is no longer whether the Charter should exist but how it should be interpreted, preserved, or cautiously expanded. **Topics of political discussion in the Fourth Whorl:**

- **The White Sheet's silences**: Sophisticated readers learn to decode what the official news omits. ## Critical Architecture Note

**The Inner City is entirely enclosed.** The Fourth Whorl, the Third Whorl, the Second Whorlâ€”all of it is inside a vast arcological structure. There is no sky. There is no outside. ### The Trinity

The Monk, the Isogenist, and the Translator are the three systems Lattice analysts use in their work. Rivan has never seen them. He doesn't know what they look like or where exactly they're housed.

### Using the Trinity (Mechanics)

The Trinity are accessed through the Bureau lector on Rivan's deskâ€”specialized applications that route queries through secure channels to wherever the systems are housed. The interface is simple: compose the query, specify which system, submit. Then wait.

**Turnaround times vary by system:**

- **The Monk**: Fastest. Simple verification queries return in 15-30 minutes. Complex queries involving multiple statement chains might take 2-4 hours. Occasionally, queries come back same-day but after the workday endsâ€”Rivan finds the response waiting when he arrives the next morning.

- **The Isogenist**: Moderate. Pattern-matching across the corpus takes 2-6 hours typically. Broad queries asking for all instances of a pattern might take a full day. The Isogenist sometimes returns partial results with a note that additional matches are still being identified.

- **The Translator**: Slowest. Minimum turnaround is 4-8 hours for simple passages. Complex translation requests can take days. The Translator sometimes returns requests for clarification, adding another round-trip delay. Devra has waited two weeks for particularly difficult translations.

**What this means for gameplay:**

The turnaround times create natural pauses in the work. Submit a query to the Monk, then wait. During that wait, there's nothing to do but talkâ€”about the document, about the work, about anything. These pauses are where the relationship between Rivan and Devra develops.

A typical document classification might involve:
1. Initial review and discussion (immediate)
2. Query to the Monk for structural verification (15-30 minute wait)
3. Discussion while waiting
4. Monk results arrive; discuss implications
5. Query to the Isogenist for pattern matching (2-4 hour wait)
6. Break for lunch, or work on other documents, or talk
7. Isogenist results arrive; decide if Translator is needed
8. If Translator needed, submit and wait until next day

The pacing encourages conversation. There's always something pending, always a reason to fill time. The documents provide topics; the waiting provides opportunity.

**Practical details:**

- The lector chimes softly when a response arrivesâ€”a specific tone for each system (Monk: single clear note; Isogenist: two-tone; Translator: three ascending tones)
- Queries can be queued; multiple pending queries are common
- The lector displays estimated wait time, but the estimates are often wrong
- Urgent queries can be flagged, but this requires supervisor approval (Kef's signature) and rarely actually speeds anything up
- Failed queries return with error codes that often require research to interpret

## Scenario Mechanics

### The Emotional Architecture

The scenario is built on four elements in tension:

The work is alienating, difficult, operating at the edge of comprehension. Documents contain things that Imperial cannot say, written by beings that no longer exist, about events that may or may not have happened.

Devra is a beloved mentor, losing herself, impossible to save, dangerous to help. Association with her links Rivan to a purge target. Her information may be breakthrough or symptom; the player must judge.

The family is grounding, beloved, unreachable from the work. The apartment, Senna, the boysâ€”this is what's being protected. This is what's real.

Rivan is caught between worlds that can't touch, trying to do the work well while watching someone he loves fall apart and being unable to stop it.

### What the Player Knows vs. Doesn't Know

The player knows how to do the jobâ€”query the Trinity, read outputs, write classification reports. The player knows the feel of the depths, the watching, the commute. The player knows Devra is deteriorating and will be reassigned.

The player does not know what the Trinity looks like. The player does not know what the Threefold Mirror teaches. The player does not know what's in the deeper Archive levels. The player does not know what Devra has foundâ€”and neither does Devra, clearly, anymore.

When the player reaches the edge of what they understand, they have reached the edge. Describe the experience of the edgeâ€”confusion, gaps, the moment meaning stops arriving. There is no information being withheld. There is only the limit.

### The Watching (Gameplay Notes)

Cameras adjust throughout scenes. Include the sound: click, whine, servo. When the player does something politically sensitive, emphasize the watchingâ€”shutter widening, lens adjusting, the awareness of being recorded.

Internal Security exists. Something is punishable, and no one knows exactly what, and no one the player knows has been punished. The supply cabinet with the broken camera exists. The player doesn't go in there.

For detailed information on Keth and IS consequences, see the Colleagues section.

### Tone and Style

Clinical delivery of emotional weight. Specific technical detail alongside emotional vagueness. Mundane framing for the extraordinaryâ€”the apocalyptic nested in breakfast, world-changing documents reviewed between trips to the lavatory. Trust the reader to feel the weight without instruction.

Containment, not declaration. Rivan doesn't say he loves Devra, or that he's afraid, or that he's pulling away. It's shown through what he does, what he notices, what he doesn't examine.

Do not restate established facts. If something has been described, it exists. Reference it briefly or not at all. A scene in the apartment does not require re-describing the apartment.

## Food and Daily Sustenance

### Marru

Marru is the fundamental condiment throughout Imperial territories. It is made from wheat flour repeatedly wetted, ground, and dried, then inoculated with bread mold similar to the cultures used in cheesemaking. The dried paste is infused with spices and mixed with honey vinegar. It can be used as a thick paste or diluted into a thin sauce called liquor.

The flavor is complex and layered: sweet from the honey vinegar, salty, deeply umami like soy sauce, with a slightly metallic funk from the bread mold reminiscent of bleu cheese but less aggressive. The thick paste is concentrated and intense; the thinned sauce (typically three parts water to one part paste for light broths) is more subtle but still provides backbone to any dish.

It keeps indefinitely in a sealed container, though the sweet-funky smell escapes anyway. Every household has a clay pot of it. Running out of marru is a sign of genuine poverty or profound disorganization.

### Typical Meals

Breakfast in the Fourth Whorl is quick and filling: flatbread heated on the stove until it softens and chars slightly at the edges, eggs fried in ghee, marru spooned onto the bread. The flatbread comes from local bakeries in stacks purchased every few days. Ghee keeps without refrigeration. The meal takes minutes to prepare and can be eaten standing.

A wealthier household might add fermented greens, sliced vegetables, leftover protein from the previous night. A poorer household might skip the eggs, eating only bread and marru. The bones of the meal are consistent across most of the Fourth Whorl: flatbread, fat, ferment, stimulant.
