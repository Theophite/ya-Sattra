# The Highborn

> **Status:** Ruling caste
> **Key feature:** Polyphonic voices triggering Compulsion
> **Phenotypes:** Elongated, Optimized Proportions, Integrated Technology
> **Descendants without Compulsion:** Völërath (Puget Sound)

The Highborn were engineered to rule. Their polyphonic voices produce layered harmonics that trigger Compulsion—an involuntary obedience response—in all other castes. This is not a strength in rulers but a designed weakness in the ruled; every non-Highborn brain contains receptors keyed to these frequencies.

## Compulsion Ethics

**Theological position:** Commanding beyond capacity damages the subject; improper use is considered worse than rape.

**Practical constraint:** Most Highborn learn restraint through witnessing consequences—watching a servant's mind destroyed through careless overuse, living thirty more years unable to form new memories.

## Phenotype: Elongated Lineage

**Type specimen:** Caenis es-Mahoun, 43, landholder

**Physical characteristics:**
- Over eight feet tall, neck extended like a fluted column
- Skull enlarged at back for modifications no living engineer understands
- Bones fragile and light—optimized for length rather than density
- Can break arm from stepping wrong on uneven ground

**Lifestyle adaptations:**
- Estates built for elongated frames (12-foot doorways)
- Palanquins for outdoor movement—falls could shatter bone
- Constant servants compensating for physical limitations
- Never reaches for anything, never carries anything

**Reproduction:** Restricted breeding pools produce genetic conditions. Caenis has outlived two husbands from cadet branches; both died of accumulated genetic problems. Her son shows early signs of the same bone fragility.

## Phenotype: Optimized Proportions

**Type specimen:** Vorath es-Kellam, Scale inspector

**Physical characteristics:**
- Six feet tall, unremarkable height
- Features in symmetry so precise it triggers unease
- Proportions correct, spacing ideal—something wrong anyway

**Reproduction:** His lineage reproduces primarily through cloning. Vorath does not have a mother. The last mother in his line was four generations back—still alive, preserved by antiagathics.

**Voice suppression:** He learned to suppress the undertones marking his voice as Highborn. The suppression has rendered his affect peculiarly flat—expressions seem performed rather than felt.

## Phenotype: Integrated Technology

**Type specimen:** Mira es-Thalen, 27, patent holder

**Born incomplete:** For the first six days of her life, she was dying. Heart could not regulate, nervous system could not propagate signals correctly. On day seven, surgeons installed components making her body functional.

**Integration:**
- Interface ports along forearms
- Cardiac regulator beneath sternum
- Circuitry threading through peripheral nervous system (visible as copper traces beneath translucent skin)

**Authentication:** Her presence serves as authentication key for family patent holdings. She does not understand the technology she enables—she simply carries it.

**Compulsion aversion:** Despite completing standard training proving the capacity exists, she has never used Compulsion since. The aversion does not reach articulation—it simply is.

## Descendants: The Völërath

**Location:** Puget Sound caste-villages

**History:** Ten thousand years ago, ancestors fled ya-Tsovez during slave revolt. Retained physical markers but lost Compulsion entirely within centuries—genes diluted through necessary intermarriage.

**Type specimen:** Erikva Stenn, 58, fisherwoman

**Physical characteristics:**
- Six foot four, notably tall locally
- Neck extends two inches longer than baseline
- Fingers thin and overlong (extra knuckle's worth)
- Skull extends slightly at back
- Bloodless pallor purpling in cold

**Degeneration:** Hearing began deteriorating at forty (common among Völërath). Relies on elaborate gesture-language developed across millennia of progressive deafness.

**Lifespan:** Expects perhaps another eighty years. Good death: old enough to see great-great-grandchildren, quick enough to avoid years of immobility.
