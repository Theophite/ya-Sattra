## Core Principle: Competitive Philanthropy

The Republic merges economic and political units through the kilit system. Your workplace votes; your workplace holds Senate seats; your workplace provides collective services. The Khen-Masot Textile Enterprise employs workers, generates trade, subsidizes electricity for 15,000 households, and holds a Senator's seat.

Works-based atonement theology creates obligations capitalism doesn't recognize. Accumulation is legitimate only if demonstrated to serve collective capacity. The monthly subsidy plaques—"This month's collective service: 2,847 dinars below cost"—are theological documentation, not advertising.

Universal electrification matters enormously. Every Taho worker experiences the revolution's material achievement through subsidized power. This isn't a welfare state bolted onto capitalism; the subsidy is constitutive of system legitimacy.

**Historical parallel:** Yugoslav self-management socialism combined with Protestant work ethic inverted—accumulation is spiritually dangerous unless demonstrated to benefit others.

## Wages in Taho (1 dinar = 10 obols)

| Occupation | Monthly Income |
|------------|----------------|
| Dock worker | 18-22 dinars (180-220 obols) |
| Warehouse worker | 16-20 dinars (160-200 obols) |
| Workshop laborer | 18-24 dinars (180-240 obols) |
| Factory foreman | 32-45 dinars (320-450 obols) |
| Merchant factor | 60+ dinars (600+ obols) |
| Major merchant (personal draw) | 400-600 dinars (4,000-6,000 obols) |

**Cost of living:** Higher than ya-Sattra due to altitude. Food imports make meals 20-30% more expensive. Subsidized power (1.5-2 dinars monthly) partially offsets these costs.

## Major Institutions

### Khen-Masot Textile Enterprise (Representative Merchant Kilit)

**What it does:** Maguey fiber processing, textile production, finished goods for Republican and Imperial markets. Employs 300 workers; provides subsidized power to 15,000 households.

**Cash flow in:**
- Textile sales: 400,000-600,000 obols annually
- Imperial export premium: 20-30% on Ganati maguey-silk
- Hydroelectric revenue: sold at cost (creates dependency, not profit)

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 500,000-700,000 obols equivalent.

**Cash flow out:**
- Raw materials: ~80,000 obols/year
- Worker wages: ~648,000 obols/year
- Power generation maintenance: ~20,000 obols/year
- Power subsidies: ~35,000 obols/year
- Family compound maintenance: ~15,000 obols/year

**Note:** The enterprise operates near break-even on direct operations. Profitability comes from capital appreciation, political capital from subsidies, Imperial trade margins, and accumulated reserves.

**Family Draw:** Factor Yula personally draws 400-500 dinars/month (4,000-5,000 obols).

**Decision-making:** The kilit is technically democratic. In practice, the Khen-Masot family has controlled operations for three generations. The enterprise's Senate seat belongs to the kilit—currently held by Factor Yula.

**Distinctive feature:** Competitive philanthropy as business strategy. The monthly subsidy plaques compete with other merchant families for reputation as the most generous contributors to collective capacity.

### Workingmens' Kilits Caucus

**What it does:** Coalition of seven labor kilits (21,000 workers and families) that elects three Senators collectively and bargains with merchant kilits as a bloc.

**Cash flow in:**
- Member dues: 500,000-1,000,000 obols/year from participating kilits

**Annual revenue to Caucus central:** 200,000-300,000 obols.

**Cash flow out:**
- Streetlight maintenance: ~50,000 obols/year
- Academy sponsorships: ~15,000 obols/year
- Strike fund: accumulates
- Negotiators and representatives: ~50,000 obols/year

**Decision-making:** Each participating kilit sends representatives to Caucus council. Major decisions require approval from kilits representing majority of membership. The three Senate seats rotate among participating kilits according to negotiated schedule.

**Distinctive feature:** The Caucus votes as a bloc. When merchants negotiate with one labor kilit, they're negotiating with all seven.

---

### The Academy

**What it does:** Higher education producing engineers, lawyers, administrators, technical specialists. Perhaps 2,000 students; 200 faculty; 300 support staff.

**Cash flow in:**
- Tuition: ~400,000 obols/year
- Kilit sponsorships: ~200,000 obols/year
- Consulting revenue: ~300,000 obols/year
- Surplus power sales

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 900,000-1,200,000 obols.

**Cash flow out:**
- Faculty salaries: ~960,000 obols/year
- Staff: ~650,000 obols/year
- Facilities: ~300,000 obols/year
- Scholarships: variable

**Decision-making:** Faculty senate governs academic matters. Administrative council (faculty plus kilit-appointed trustees) governs finances. The Rector is elected by faculty but serves at administrative council's pleasure.

**Distinctive feature:** The Academy is supposed to enable mobility based on merit. In practice, merchant children can afford tutors and three years without wages; dock workers' children rarely can. But some do attend and succeed, maintaining the ideology.

## Household Profiles

### Household 9: Torin and Sala Dresh, Dock Worker Family (Working Poor)

**Profile:** Serrulata male, 35, dock worker (Dockers' Fraternal Kilit). Serrulata female, 33, market vendor. Three children (10, 7, 4). Port District tenement, Taho.

**Monthly Income:** 24-28 dinars (240-280 obols)

| Category | Amount (dinars) | % |
|----------|-----------------|---|
| Housing | 5 | 19% |
| Food | 10 | 38% |
| Fuel/light (subsidized) | 1.5 | 6% |
| Clothing | 2 | 8% |
| Kilit dues | 1.5 | 6% |
| Children's education | 1 | 4% |
| Miscellaneous | 3 | 12% |

**Budget Balance:** Roughly neutral. Good vendor months mean meat; bad months mean grain and beans.

**Possessions:**
- Two-room apartment (rented)
- Cooking equipment, vendor cart
- Clothing for family (maguey-silk; Serrulata standards)
- Children's school materials
- No lector

**Economic Anxieties:**
- Altitude costs—everything food-related is expensive
- Children's futures—can they afford Academy?
- Kilit politics—if Dockers' Fraternal loses influence, wages follow
- Power subsidy maintaining their barely-affordable lifestyle

| Charitable donations | 60 | 12% |
| Children's education | 30 | 6% |
| Entertainment | 30 | 6% |
| Reserve/investment | 50 | 10% |

**Budget Balance:** Positive, but competitive philanthropy and social obligations consume most surplus.
