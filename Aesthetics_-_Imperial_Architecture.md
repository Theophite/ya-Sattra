# Imperial Architecture (Current)

**Core intention:** "I belong here. I have access. I maintain what I was given."

Current Imperial material culture speaks of position within systems no one made. The message is stewardship, not creation.

---

## Relationship to Inherited Structure

Current Imperial construction happens in the interstices of Antediluvian architecture. Three levels of execution:

**Aureate buildings:**
- Refinement through precision and maintenance
- Carved copper plates with patterns worked an inch deep, grooves still sharp after years
- Welds nearly disappear
- Paint in layered gradations
- Geometric patterns following consistent logic
- Surfaces maintained—no corrosion, no algae, no faded paint
- Where they occupy Antediluvian structures, they modify as little as possible

**Guild buildings:**
- Competent execution
- Bold patterns, visible brush marks
- Minor imperfections accepted between maintenance cycles

**Limited resources:**
- Decoration concentrated at visible points
- Paint around doorways while the rest shows bare metal or faded color

---

## Interstitial Construction

**Buildings between columns:** Stone, brick, wood—3-5 stories, never touching the ancient columns.

**The five-foot gap:** Buildings never touch the ancient columns. The gap maintained for reasons no one remembers. You don't question the gap.

**The Medina's commercial architecture:** Human-scale construction fitting between monumental Antediluvian elements.

**The Ridge's workshops:** Built inside collapsed arcology sections—new construction in the ruins of the incomprehensible.

## Philosophy

**"I belong here."** Credential architecture. Living closer to functioning pre-Interdict systems. The apartments near the Second Whorl say: "My family has clearance. This proximity is earned." Not built, not made—permitted.

**"I maintain what I was given."** Stewardship, not creation. "I didn't make this, but I keep it working. I'm a good heir."

**"Adaptation, not transformation."** The Medina's buildings fit between ancient columns. The Ridge's workshops occupy collapsed arcology. Ganati re-carving would be sacrilege. You adapt the inherited, not transform it.

**"Position is meaning."** The statement is in where you live within the inherited structure, not in what you build. The Fourth Whorl apartment says everything; the furniture inside says almost nothing.

---

## Key Examples

*The Keloreth Block apartments.* "You cannot sell your apartment. The grant doesn't permit transfer. You cannot be evicted. The apartment belongs to your line the way your skeleton belongs to your body." The credential is the address.

*The Orvanneth Atrium.* Three-hundred-foot atrium with diamond windows looking at actual sky. The family lives in a salvage cottage in its shadow. "The clearance is a fortune and a cage." Position is wealth; the cottage is irrelevant.

*Maintenance as status display.* An Aureate family's corridor section gleams. Bronze polished to mirror brightness. Adjacent sections show normal wear. The message isn't "we built this" but "we maintain this better than you."

---

## Generating New Details

*Example: An artisan's workshop.* She occupies a corner where ancient walls meet. She hasn't modified them—permits would be required. Instead, portable equipment: workbenches on wheels, tool racks that don't touch the bronze. Everything could be removed in an hour. The impermanence is the point.

*Example: A family shrine.* The shrine occupies a niche in the ancient wall—one of those alcoves whose purpose is unknown. The family has placed devotional objects there. No construction. Just use. "We occupy this niche by credential, not by right."

*Example: A nouveau-riche miscalculation.* They've added construction to their apartment—new walls, decorative elements. Neighbors are scandalized. You don't BUILD in inherited space. The additions scream "we don't understand how this works." Established families have modest interiors, because the address makes the statement.
