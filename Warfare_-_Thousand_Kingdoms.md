## Command Structure

### Traditional Warband Model

A khan and whoever follows them. Size varies from a dozen warriors to several hundred. There's no clear rank structure—authority flows from the khan's personal judgment about who commands respect. A warrior who's proven himself leads; a warrior who hasn't follows.

Traditional warbands pay through shares of plunder rather than salaries. This creates incentive structures: warriors want raids producing loot, not campaigns producing strategic advantage. Division of spoils causes fights. Unsuccessful khans lose followers to more successful rivals.

### Professional Reorganization

Some khans have adopted Imperial-style organization. Khan Tsoka's warband (643 soldiers) is organized into seven companies: three destrier cavalry, two mixed infantry with rifles, one scouts and skirmishers, one logistics and support.

Written orders, salary payments (8 obols monthly for cavalry, 6 for infantry), merit-based promotion. His officers find this amusing but follow the system because it works: his supply trains don't get lost, his forces arrive when promised, his men don't die of preventable disease.

### The Junta

The Junta in Ogon represents another organizational model—urban governance by military oligarchy. Token-holders control city functions, maintain forces for enforcement, balance against each other through the Tama-managed redistribution systems.

## Who Fights

### Destrier Cavalry

The core of Akama military power. Warriors bond with their mounts—the clicking, carnivorous destriers that won't accept just anyone. A warrior without a destrier fights on foot; a warrior whose destrier dies may never bond with another.

The mongrel genetics of Akama warriors—Redback muscle density mixing with Springheel explosiveness, diluted Warborn size without metabolic collapse—emerged from exile populations breeding without oversight. They are what happens when engineered humanity breeds without Creche control.

### Infantry

Those whose destriers won't accept them, or who never bonded. They hold ground, man fortifications, do the unglamorous work that makes cavalry raids possible. Less prestigious, steady work.

### Scouts

Young warriors learning fieldcraft, mapping territory, reporting movement. Not warriors yet, not really.

## Characteristic Equipment and Procurement

### Sources

**Ganati trade** is the primary source for modern firearms. Avouvar factors supply barrel alloys, firing mechanisms, precision components that Akama smiths cannot produce. Rifles, ammunition, optical sights—anything requiring industrial precision comes north through trade networks.

**Imperial capture** provides equipment and prestige. Every border action produces salvage. Some warbands specialize in raiding supply convoys specifically for equipment. The Salvage Consortium in Ogon evaluates captured weapons, determines what can be made to work, performs authentication bypass on functional equipment.

**Village production** is sustainable and infinite. A village smith can produce bows, arrows, melee weapons, armor components—anything Akama hands have always made. Adequate for most infantry work and can continue indefinitely.

**Thavek district in Ogon** represents emerging local production. The smithies there have accumulated machine tools, some with Mutterer access. Imek Stone-Foot has purchased a coil-winder enabling light-bore rail weapon production—for the first time, Akama warriors can carry rail weapons that Akama technicians can repair.

### Destrier Cavalry Weapons

The **gouge** is the primary weapon: a sickle-curved blade on a long handle, point-first, designed for striking down during the mount's descent. The point enters, then the curve follows through—both outer edges sharpened so the widening blade carves outward. The curve routes around bone and armor rather than catching on it. Destriers pick at formation edges rather than charging into them; the gouge hooks soldiers from the periphery, dragging them out of position.

Secondary weapons include braces of high-caliber breach-loading chemical pistols (or imported Ganati weapons), sabers (single-edge, moderate curve), and lances. Dragoons carry heavy-barreled chemical rifles with bayonets that double as lances, alongside grenades, smoke bombs, and fireworks for psychological effect.

### Named Akama Smiths and Salvagers

*Dzoragh Ironhand* (mobile forge, Malpais): Traditional smithing. Destrier tack, gouges with distinctive blood-channel fuller, sabers, spearheads. Third generation—travels between villages.

*Grandmother Vurda Eleven-Sage* (Southern Malpais): Legendary blades. Master-quality gouges (months-long wait), pattern-welded sabers, ceremonial daggers, lance heads for khans only. Seventy-three years old; no apprentice has matched her work. Her gouges are heirlooms.

*Khorat the Deserter* (Thavek district, Ogon): Chemical-propellant rifles. Hunting rifle (.38 caliber, accurate to 400 yards), carbine, heavy rifle (.50 caliber). Ganati Republican Guard deserter using captured Imperial tooling. Quality approaches Republican standards. Doesn't talk about his past.

*Imek Stone-Foot* (Thavek district, Ogon): Electromagnetic weapons (emerging). Light rail carbine (Imek-pattern), portable capacitor packs, coil assemblies. Purchased a coil-winder from Avouvar traders—first Akama-repairable rail weapons.

*Old Tseng's boys* (Ogon outskirts): Infantry equipment. Shields, spears, leather armor, bulk arrows. Tseng is dead; his sons run the forge. Everyone still calls it Old Tseng's. Reliable but not exceptional.

*Gorvan Salvage* (Ogon harbor): Captured equipment evaluation and bypass. Gorvan himself handles neural-lock bypass; his apprentices do the rest. If it was captured, they can tell you if it works.

## What Military Life Is Like

### Standing in the Rain

The Akama have inverted conventional morality's relationship to fortune. Where most cultures treat luck as morally neutral, the Akama treat luck as the primary mechanism of cosmic justice. If fate assigns you a burden, that assignment itself is just. Your response determines your virtue.

Unlucky people who endure well are more virtuous than lucky people who endure easily. Attempting to avoid your assigned burden is moral cowardice. This creates a culture where fortune-as-justice prevents revenge cycles. Bad luck killed him, not enemies. No courts needed, no bureaucracy required.

### Ordinary Warband Life

Most warriors aren't philosophical about standing in the rain. They follow their khan because the khan pays, because their fathers followed, because alternatives are worse. They send money to mothers in Malpais villages three weeks' ride south. They bond with destriers at fourteen or fifteen, learning to read the clicking rhythms that signal hunting, feeding, threat. The ones whose mounts won't accept them fight on foot.

Older warriors survive through knowing when to fight, when to run, when to hide. Never charging unless certain. This makes them unpopular with young warriors who think courage means dying gloriously. It makes them invaluable to khans who need soldiers still alive next year.

In professionally organized warbands like Tsoka's, warriors follow paperwork requirements without understanding why they matter. Written duty rosters, scheduled patrols, proper latrines. The older warriors find this amusing but comply because the supply trains don't get lost.

### The Tama Strategy

Some families survive by being useful to whoever's in charge rather than fighting. The Tama family has outlasted every warband that ever controlled Ogon—four centuries of Khans who seemed invincible, now dead or displaced. Be useful to whoever's in charge. Never threaten power.

## Exemplar Characters

**Gorin Three-Hawk** — Age 31, destrier rider under Khan Tsoka. The astrological surname indicates traditional Malpais family. Bonded with his mount at fourteen. His kit is functional: destrier-leather armor with ceramic inserts, Dzoragh Ironhand gouge (notched from use, edge recently touched up), composite bow, brace of pistols. Sends money to his mother three weeks' ride south. Follows Tsoka's paperwork requirements without understanding why they matter.

**Krom Kindling** — Age 44, infantry under Khan Ataru. The epithet came from a fire he should have died in—he was left for dead in a burning granary, walked out three hours later. Never bonded with a destrier—the mounts won't accept him. Fights on foot: holding ground, manning fortifications. One of Ataru's most reliable soldiers because he doesn't care about glory.

**Ghom** — Age 52, veteran under Khan Ataru. No epithet—remarkable after thirty years. Survived through knowing when to fight, when to run, when to hide. Never charges unless certain. Unpopular with young warriors. Invaluable to Ataru, who needs soldiers still alive next year.

**Vremin Five-Elk** — Age 24, scout under Khan Tsoka. The front-vowel personal name marks possible Kma-Dhol ancestry; the astrological surname is traditional Malpais. Learning fieldcraft, mapping territory, reporting movement. Not a warrior yet—she's killed one person, a wounded Imperial soldier, and still dreams about his face.

**Khan Tsoka Red Deer** — Age 43, controls the southeast approach to Ogon, commands 643 soldiers. Fostered by Colonel-Hereditary es-Valent for twelve years. His warband operates with Imperial discipline while maintaining Akama identity. His tribute protection contracts create structural conflict with Ataru's salmon empire. They've fought three duels; Tsoka won all three.

**Khan Ataru Blackfish** — Age 47, controls western canal districts, commands approximately 800 warriors. Commercial empire built on salmon runs. Seventy-odd vassals pay fixed tribute regardless of fortune. Religious in Akama way, venerates largely fictional ancestors. Collects Ganati glass sculpture.
