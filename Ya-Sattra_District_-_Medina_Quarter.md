## **Guild Neighborhoods and Guild Streets**

The guild system in the Medina operates at two scales: neighborhoods where large guilds claim entire districts, and streets where small guilds control individual thoroughfares. Both operate under ancient charters granting rights that predate Bureau authorityâ€”legal grants from before current record-keeping, etched on materials whose alloy compositions baffle modern metallurgists, written in scripts that require specialist translation. ### **The Permit Raj**

Understanding guild territory requires understanding what exists outside it. The Imperial bureaucracy has accumulated millennia of regulations governing commercial activityâ€”permits from the Scale Bureau for premises, certifications from the Rod Bureau for equipment, registrations with the Coin Bureau for tax purposes, safety compliance through channels no one can fully identify. These requirements contradict each other. A vendor selling food needs health certification from a Creche sub-office, commercial licensing from Scale, tax registration with Coin, and equipment permits from Rod if using any heating apparatus. The fees, waiting periods, and documentary requirements for each permit assume the others don't exist. Obtaining full legal compliance would take years and cost more than most businesses earn.

This isn't deliberate oppression. It's accumulated bureaucratic sediment that no one has authority to clear. The Bureaus cannot repeal regulations their predecessors issued; they can only add new ones. The result is a system where operating legally outside guild protection is effectively impossible. Even if a merchant somehow obtained all required permits, they would be operating in technical violation of something, because the regulations are self-contradictory.

Guild membership cuts through this entirely. A charter from before the Bureaus existed cannot be invalidated by Bureau regulations issued after. When a Scale inspector demands to see a craftsman's commercial premises license, guild credentials are sufficient response. The guild charter grants all members the right to practice their craft. The Bureau can take complaints to the guild masters, not to individual members. The guild handles its own standards, its own quality control, its own internal discipline. Bureau authority stops at the guild's boundaries.

### **The Original Design**

The guilds were not designed for medieval-style artisanal production. The Antediluvians conceived guild members as independent operators of automated production facilitiesâ€”small versions of ya-Don's Furnaces, standardized equipment producing standardized goods. Guild structure was supposed to ensure quality standards and prevent monopolies in an economy of individual automated producers competing on programming quality rather than manual skill.

The automation is gone. The Furnaces of ya-Don are the only remaining example of what every guild was supposed to operate. But the charters remain, creating legal frameworks for automated production that now protect manual craftwork. A charter granting rights to "operate automated thermal processing units within specifications established by Bureau of Rod certification" now protects a blacksmith heating metal over a coal forge, hammering it by hand, creating bespoke products that violate every specification because the automated unit broke down eight centuries ago.

This fossilization creates the Empire's economic stagnation. The charters assume automation that doesn't exist. New automated production would require new charters, which would require Imperial authority no Bureau possessesâ€”the Emperor granted the original rights, and the Emperor is in occultation. Manual production under old charters creates non-standard goods maintained by Mutterer choirs who repair each piece of equipment into unique configurations. The whole system is locked into handcraft production using legal frameworks designed for automated manufacturing.

The Medina represents normal life under impossible conditions. Characters here navigate systems that officially function while actually operating through accumulated workarounds. Scenes should feel comprehensibleâ€”commerce, housing, daily routineâ€”while revealing through specific details the absurdities inhabitants have learned to accept.
