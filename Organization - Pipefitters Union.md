# The Pipefitters Union

> **Type:** Labor union
> **Location:** Iron Yards (offices in The Pipe and Gauge on Torchline Row)
> **Function:** Controls labor, represents workers, maintains infrastructure
> **Membership:** Most Iron Yards workers regardless of caste

The Pipefitters Union holds legitimacy predating Imperial memory. Its charter, etched on steel plates so ancient the alloy composition baffles modern metallurgists, grants rights to "all who labor in the conveyance of vital fluids through the body of the City."

No one can say definitively when those words were first inscribed, but their weight carries forward—authority older than the Bureaux themselves dare challenge.

## Evolution Beyond Original Mandate

What began as a guild of specialized pipe-workers now encompasses nearly every form of industrial labor in the Rotten Quarter that doesn't explicitly fall under another charter:

- Stevedores who've never touched a wrench carry union cards
- Cable-splicers whose work involves no fluids pay their dues
- Salvage-cutters whose only relationship to pipes involves removing them for scrap

**The Union takes them all**, asking only solidarity in return.

## Leadership

### Marlo Glen

Leads from a position that shouldn't exist—a near-baseline at the head of an organization whose membership is overwhelmingly Labor Caste.

**His success rests on understanding that dignity requires no augmentation.**

When negotiating with Salvage Guild officials, he brings his lieutenants:

**Old Crek** — Ironbone whose joints have begun their final calcification but whose presence still commands respect.

**Lira Three-Stone** — Springheel woman whose legend grows with each retelling.

### The Three-Stone Story

Union mythology. The old boss, **Havek Otelmar**, had grown comfortable in corruption—taking bribes from the Guild to suppress wage demands while skimming dues for personal use.

During a heated meeting about dock safety (three men had died that week from preventable cable failures), Otelmar made the mistake of calling the Labor Caste workers "meat that thinks it's men."

Lira happened to be adjusting the tension on a cargo sling—one of those fist-sized lead weights Springheels use for ballast calculations.

**The distance was forty meters. The throw was perfect.**

The Union elected her to leadership that same night, but she declined, suggesting Marlo Glen instead. A Springheel leading might divide membership along caste lines. A near-baseline leading with Labor Caste lieutenants sent a different message—**merit and loyalty matter more than the shape of your bones.**

## Power and Leverage

**The willingness to strike** gives the Union leverage even the Salvage Guild respects.

**Infrastructure competence:** The Union maintains systems more competently than the Bureau of the Rod could manage. The Rod has nominal jurisdiction but rarely intervenes.

## Cross-Caste Solidarity

The Union's unusual composition—near-baseline leadership, Labor Caste membership, all working together—creates something the Empire's caste system isn't designed to produce: solidarity across engineered divisions.

This makes the Union both essential (the economy depends on them) and threatening (the ideology contradicts caste hierarchy).
