# Sump-Gate

> **Type:** Tenement / Pumping station
> **Population:** ~1,500 residents
> **Function:** Water processing, religious center, deep access
> **Notable feature:** The dome (sub-basement level -9)

Sump-Gate exists because ya-Sattra needs its water pumped somewhere, and the Middens provided convenient foundations. The pumping station occupies nine levels below ground and four above, processing millions of gallons daily through machinery predating current Imperial records.

## The Dome: Level -9

The deepest level houses the dome. Workers report consistent experiences when assigned here:
- Sensation of understanding mathematical principles just beyond conscious grasp
- Conviction that the dome responds to presence rather than action
- Temporary improvements in spatial reasoning that fade after returning to surface

The dome shows no visible connections to pump infrastructure above, yet the correlation is undeniable: when the dome is accessible, pumps work. During the one recorded incident where the sub-basement flooded completely, pumps failed until water was cleared.

### Mutterer Activity

Mutterers congregate near the dome in groups shifting composition according to logic no Companion has decoded. They apply modifications to its surface:
- Geometric patterns in conductive materials
- Components serving no obvious purpose
- Arrangements created and destroyed according to internal imperatives

**These modifications correlate with pump efficiency improvements persisting for weeks after application.** No one understands the mechanism. Everyone accepts the results.

Curate Aram rings disruption chimes hourly to prevent cascading vocalizations that once resulted in three Mutterers attempting to rebuild a pressure valve while congregants were still arriving for service.

## Building Structure

### Middle Basements (-1 to -8): Pump Mechanisms

Massive constructions moving water through the building and out to sea. Each pump is maintained by specific families who've developed generational knowledge of their particular machines.

The Mutterers nest in unused sections, their presence tolerated because their obsessive optimization occasionally prevents failures that scheduled maintenance would miss.

### Sea Level: The Valve-Saint Parish

The original overflow chamber—a vast space extending into the harbor from the building's base. The floor sits below high tide, with grated sections allowing controlled water entry. Seawater seeps through during spring tides and storms, never more than ankle-deep but enough that congregants learn appropriate footwear.

**The Wheel Altar:** Four meters in diameter, corroded surface bearing generations of carved prayers.

According to tradition, when catastrophic flooding threatened the building, a Redback stevedore whose name is lost turned this wheel that hadn't moved in centuries, activating the pumps through pure physical determination. His heart failed from the strain, but the water receded.

*The wheel probably never controlled the pumps—any engineer can see the lack of mechanical connection—but the story provides meaning that technical accuracy cannot replace.*

### Upper Floors (1-4): Residential and Technical

**Waterwheel generators** in outflow pipes convert water pressure to modest electricity—untaxed because no one has figured out how to meter it, distributed through informal agreements.

**Short-wave relay station** on third floor provides communication links to maritime traffic, operators timing transmissions for atmospheric conditions rather than pump cycles.

**Roof cisterns** store processed water from osmotic membranes that pump pressure drives, producing freshwater with slight residual salinity meeting basic safety standards.

## The Valve-Saint Parish

Curate Aram es-Thabit conducts services acknowledging the building's nature without surrendering to it:
- Sound of pumps as background rather than impediment
- Tide as rhythm rather than threat
- Mutterers above as witnesses rather than intrusions

The parish serves fishers, pump workers, and anyone seeking meaning in mechanical systems exceeding human understanding.

**The grain dole** operates from the adjacent kitchen, where Deacon Mer Hasket manages supplies with obsessive documentation that two years ago proved the parish wasn't selling donated food. The inspector left. The documentation habit remained.

## Clan Sarn Presence

Clan Sarn maintains minimal surface presence at Sump-Gate. Their real clan life exists in flooded tunnels below.

Patriarch Vennas Sarn rarely surfaces, spending months at depths where pressure changes human perception in ways he no longer tries to explain. When he does appear:
- Skin shows lichen crusts that don't quite look terrestrial
- Eyes carry bioluminescent glow persisting even in bright light

What the Sarns trade for, what they trade with—these questions remain unanswered because the answers might be worse than uncertainty.
