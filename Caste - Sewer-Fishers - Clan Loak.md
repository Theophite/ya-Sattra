# Clan Loak: The Vertical Operators

> **Location:** Upper three levels of Hollow-Egress, Middens
> **Operation:** Lift-3 vertical transport
> **Power source:** Sewer-gas engine (methane from Sarn gas-harvesting)
> **Secondary business:** Merra's Kitchen - communal feeding operation

The Loaks control Lift-3, the only Middens lift capable of hauling industrial-scale structural elements.

## Important Members

**Yuli Loak** (Age 33): Current council chair and Lift-3 operations manager. Inherited both political position and technical expertise from her mother Merra. Her burn-scarred face came from hot lubricant spraying from the Lift-3 engine's feedline. Manages the salvage trade with sub-percent error rates while navigating council politics.

**"Aunt" Merra Loak** (Age 57): Former council chair, now runs "Merra's kitchen," which has expanded from her personal kitchen to the mess for every salvage crew dependent on Lift-3. Her position behind the counter, serving miso-barley-mussel stews to hundreds daily, makes her an information nexus. Workers discuss business over meals, providing intelligence she filters to Yuli.

**Korr Loak** (Age 48): Valve tender and apiarist. Lost his leg in a lift accident, now uses a metal peg. Manages both the rooftop hives and the clan's water negotiations with the Hanged Men. His ability to read pre-Interdict maintenance glyphs suggests deeper technical knowledge than typical valve work requires.

**Bos Loak** (Age 17): Companion candidate representing generational investment. His training fees consumed years of clan savings. Sapphire-glass knapper and knifemaker. Sketch artist. Success would provide medical connections and legal employment pathway; failure would still leave valuable Mutterer-handling skills.

**Jak Loak** (Age 19): Orphaned cousin residing with Merra after Chain-Men killed his parents in a toll dispute. Apprenticed to Den Vesh's operation, he's acquired limited Black Calculus knowledge—enough to induce selective forgetting in observers. The clan recognizes this skill's utility without fully understanding its mechanisms.

**Jossa Loak-Benru** (Age 29): Marriage-bridge between clans, managing documentation through both networks. Her incomplete Companion training provides unofficial medical knowledge, particularly useful given the hazards of lift work.

**Elim Loak** (Age 15, Stevedore Orphan): Handles counterweight adjustments requiring significant strength. Still processing his family's death in a pier collapse, he's developed fierce protective loyalty.

**Rua Loak** (Age 13, Stevedore Orphan): Despite stuttering speech, demonstrates mechanical aptitude with lift systems. Can diagnose brake problems through sound alone.

**Tam Loak** (Age 11, Stevedore Orphan): Serves as lookout and messenger, small enough for sewer-fisher passages but robust enough for surface work. Maintains mental maps of surveillance patterns.

## Family Business and Relationships

The Loaks operate Lift-3 through monthly-renewed "maintenance contracts" with the Salvage Guild, a documentary fiction persisting over a century. The lift's capacity for structural elements makes it irreplaceable—no other Middens lift can haul industrial beams or large machinery components.

The sewer-gas engine powering Lift-3 represents the clan's greatest technical achievement. Built half a century ago, it uses methane from the Sarns' gas-harvesting operation to drive massive drum wheels. The engine's construction required intuitive understanding of engineering which only the peculiar sewer-fisher facility for mathematics could provide, but its operation is unstable—it also depends on constant micro-adjustments.

Daily operations involve coordinating the six pulley crews for the smaller, hand-pulled lifts, calculating counterweight distributions, and maintaining rope systems supplied primarily by the Venns. The lift operates on unwritten priority systems: Guild shipments receive optimal scheduling, Benru-brokered loads get reliable placement, independents wait for available capacity. This arrangement converts political relationships into vertical transportation access.

The kitchen operation creates secondary power through food distribution. Merra feeds the entire salvage workforce, making her temporarily untouchable—no one attacks the person who provides tomorrow's meal. This protection extends partially to the clan, as disrupting the Loaks means disrupting food supply for hundreds of workers.

Jak's Black Calculus abilities provide unacknowledged protection. Bureau inspectors develop memory gaps about regulatory violations. Chain-Men forget specific toll disputes. The clan doesn't discuss this openly but ensures Jak maintains legitimate lift work during sensitive periods.

## Living Situation and Idiosyncrasies

The clan occupies Hollow-Egress's three uppermost levels, with the top floor split between Merra's kitchen and the engine room. The kitchen's proximity to the engine provides year-round warmth. The smoke from trash fires and the canal's perpetual sewage smell don't make it this high. It's almost homey.

The middle level, converted from mechanical space when the engine simplified support requirements, houses living quarters accessible only through narrow hatches. These openings, easily navigated by sewer-fishers but challenging for larger castes, provide security through architecture. Eight small chambers surround a central meeting space where the clan conducts private business away from the kitchen's public nature.

The rooftop gardens transform available surface into agricultural production. Korr's hives occupy the southeast corner, producing honey with antimicrobial properties from urban-adapted bees. Hydroponic potato tanks, repurposed from industrial use, provide staple starches. Every remaining surface hosts vegetables—beans climbing salvaged trellises, tomatoes in hanging systems, hardy greens in shallow beds.

Water management shapes daily activity. The Lockworks provides minimal flow this high, forcing reliance on elaborate rainwater collection. Overlapping tarpaulins channel precipitation into cisterns built throughout the structure. During storms, the clan mobilizes to adjust collection systems, treating water capture as seriously as lift operations. Their roof ownership—technically leased from the Hanged Men but practically hereditary—makes collected water legally theirs.

The engine room serves as practical classroom. Children learn mathematics through mechanical reality: engine rhythms teach counting, counterweights introduce algebra, rope tension demonstrates geometry. This education produces operators who intuitively understand vertical dynamics but might struggle with abstract concepts unrelated to lift work.

The clan maintains three parallel accounting systems. Official Guild documentation shows minimal activity. Internal written ledgers track actual flows. Mental accounts, never documented, record the favor economy determining real access. Children learn all three, understanding that survival requires maintaining these separate realities simultaneously.

The stevedore orphans have developed their own internal culture, maintaining birth-caste strength training while learning sewer-fisher technical knowledge. They provide physical deterrent—Chain-Men hesitate before confronting a clan with stevedore protection. This arrangement benefits both groups: orphans gain family structure and economic inclusion, sewer-fishers gain physical security.

The family shrine, besides standard icons, maintains components from fatal lift accidents—not as morbid trophies but as recognition of responsibility. Each piece bears a name, reminding operators that vertical transport kills. This memorial aspect reinforces the clan's careful approach to maintenance and operation.
