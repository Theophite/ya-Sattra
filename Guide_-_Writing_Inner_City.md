# **Writing the Inner City**

## **Overview**

This guide covers writing scenes set in the Inner City arcology—from the bustling Fourth Whorl markets to the temporal compression of the First Whorl and the Emperor's communications that emerge from behind the Black Door. The Inner City presents a unique challenge: conveying genuine strangeness through the perspective of characters who find it normal.

---

## **The Emotional Register**

The Inner City should feel **ancient, procedural, and melancholy**—not horrifying, not mystical, not exciting. The temporal distortions, the vast empty spaces, the machines that have operated since before accessible history—these are the background conditions of life, not dramatic revelations. Characters have adapted. They work, eat, sleep, navigate bureaucracy, and occasionally pass their future selves in corridors. The strangeness is not dangerous; it is simply strange.

**Exhaustion rather than fear.** First Whorl occultants process documents while the Emperor screams through ancient speakers. Second Whorl clerks file reports knowing that the situation they address resolved months ago. Fourth Whorl merchants sell breakfast to commuters who will age years in the time it takes to reach their desks. Everyone is tired. Everyone continues anyway.

**Resignation rather than despair.** The systems cannot be changed. The Standing Orders are immutable. The temporal compression is permanent. The First Whorl residents who could theoretically leave never do, because the outside world has become incomprehensible. This is not tragedy actively experienced—it is the settled condition of existence.

**Wonder that has become routine.** A clerk passing their future self might nod or avoid eye contact, not gasp in shock. The archive contains documents filed before they were written; this is a filing problem, not a philosophical crisis. The Provisioners have been making their rounds since before Tariq arrived, which was ten thousand years ago, which is recent by the standards of this place.

## **The Four Whorls: Tone Gradient**

The emotional register deepens as you move inward.

**Fourth Whorl (The Great Bazaar):** Noise, commerce, crowds. The closest thing to normal urban life in the Empire. Vendors hawk goods, commuters buy breakfast, the temporal effects are negligible. Write this as a bustling market—perhaps stranger in its vastness and its enclosed sky, but recognizably alive. The tone is commercial, practical, occasionally chaotic.

**Third Whorl (The Engine Room):** Industrial hum, fewer people, the beginning of emptiness. Technical specialists maintain machinery they don't fully understand. The spaces are larger, the corridors longer, the sense of being inside something immense becomes unavoidable. The tone is utilitarian, competent, slightly oppressive.

**Second Whorl (The Seat of Power):** Vast silence punctuated by footsteps. Seven thousand people in spaces built for two million. The Whorl-Lag begins—chronometers drift, memories become unreliable, the persistent sensation of déjà vu called "the echo" becomes background noise. Write this as isolation despite proximity to power. Bureau officials walk through cavernous empty halls to reach offices that once served departments of thousands. The tone is bureaucratic, melancholy, slightly uncanny.

**First Whorl (The Antechamber):** Near-total isolation, profound quiet, the weight of compressed time. Almost everyone here is Highborn; Compulsion is irrelevant because there is no one to compel. People work alone, eat alone, sleep alone. Connection across temporal differentials is difficult; most residents have given up trying. The tone is exhausted, ancient, procedural—and underneath it all, profoundly sad.

## **Sensory Details by Depth**

### **Fourth Whorl**

- **Sound:** Market cacophony, vendor calls, the clatter of tram cars, conversation in multiple registers (formal Imperial, guild cant, regional accents)
- **Light:** Orb-lights simulating day/night cycles, brighter than the deeper Whorls, artificial but adequate
- **Smell:** Food stalls, incense from shrine alcoves, the particular scent of many bodies in enclosed space
- **Movement:** Crowds, commerce, the rhythm of working hours

### **Third Whorl**

- **Sound:** Industrial machinery, the hum of systems no one fully understands, fewer voices, echoes in large spaces
- **Light:** Functional illumination, dimmer than the Fourth, some areas maintained poorly
- **Smell:** Machine oil, metal, the recycled air of enclosed industrial space
- **Movement:** Workers on purposeful errands, maintenance crews, the occasional administrator descending from above

### **Second Whorl**

- **Sound:** Footsteps echoing in empty corridors, the distant hum of systems, occasional conversation that seems too loud in the silence
- **Light:** The Luminescent Arrays—adequate but alien, cycling through patterns calibrated for someone else's comfort
- **Smell:** Dust in unused spaces, the particular staleness of air that has been recycled for millennia
- **Movement:** Individual clerks on long commutes through empty space, occasional clusters at lift stations

### **First Whorl**

- **Sound:** The hum of Provisioners on their tracks. Polyphonic Highborn voices echoing in corridors built for different acoustics. The soft chime of pneumatic arrivals. Near the Black Door, a low irregular vibration—breathing? pulse? gears?—that residents learn to ignore. And sometimes, without warning, the speakers activate.
- **Light:** The Luminescent Arrays at their most alien—not wrong exactly, but designed for sensibilities that no longer exist
- **Temperature:** Cold at depth, climate systems calibrated for tolerances that don't quite match current human preference
- **The gradient:** Felt as pressure, as the sense that thoughts are moving too slowly or too quickly for bodies. Gradient sickness manifests as nausea, disorientation, the conviction that something is wrong with time itself.

## **The Voice Through the Speakers**

The speakers are ancient, installed throughout the First Whorl at some point before accessible history. When the voice comes through them, it is polyphonic—a chorus speaking in unison, layered harmonics that make Highborn voices sound thin by comparison. The voice carries Command: something deeper than Compulsion, something that resonates in the engineered architecture of Highborn neurology. Even those designed to issue commands feel the weight of being commanded.

The screams come in registers of agony and exultation, sometimes both at once, sometimes alternating so rapidly that residents cannot determine which they are hearing. The voice rants in Imperial grammar that breaks under its own weight—impossible constructions, sentences that contradict themselves, fragments of poetry interrupted by administrative directives.

**How to write it:**
- Residents continue working through the screaming. There is nothing else to do.
- New occultants ask whose voice it is. The question is deferred. Eventually they stop asking.
- The worst moments are when the voice speaks a name. When your name is called, the Black Door opens. You pass through and are never seen again.
- The grammar makes attribution difficult. To say "[Emperor-word] speaks" produces a sentence that means "correct disclosure is disclosed." Discussion requires constructions that don't quite function.

## **Temporal Phenomena**

Write these matter-of-factly. The strangeness is normalized through long exposure.

**Self-encounters:** Residents may pass their own past or future selves in corridors. The loops close—if you see your future self doing something, you will do it. A middle-aged version running with burning documents. An ancient version already sitting in the Archive whenever you arrive. A version missing fingers, walking armed toward the Black Door. Some loops have closed. Some remain open.

**Fixed shadows:** Silhouettes permanently burned into bronze walls by temporal events. Some can be dated; others predate accessible records. New shadows appear occasionally—a figure frozen mid-stride that wasn't there yesterday. Whether the event that created the shadow has already happened or will happen cannot usually be determined.

**Proleptic damage:** Physical damage from events that have not yet occurred. Scorch marks from battles that haven't happened. Cracks from impacts that haven't landed. The damage is real and measurable. Three new scorch patterns appeared this year in the Proleptic Damage Corridor. The frequency is increasing.

**The Kelso Senna Phenomenon:** One individual appears throughout the First Whorl at different ages—sometimes young, sometimes old, sometimes wounded. The Bureau has no record of anyone by this name. Intercepted communications reference him as Ganati, a revolutionary, from a political formation that does not yet exist.

## **The Ancient Machines**

The infrastructure should feel present but unexplained. The machines work. They don't fail. No one knows why. Don't explain the technology—the mystery is the point.

**The Provisioners:** Small automated carts on embedded tracks, approximately one meter tall, polished bronze unmarked by time. They collect items from the Arrival Halls and distribute them throughout the Whorl. They are not intelligent. They do not respond to speech. They simply perform their programmed tasks, as they have for millennia.

For long-term residents, the Provisioners become the most consistent presence in their lives. Some name them. Some speak to them. The machines do not respond, but in a space where human connection is difficult across temporal differentials, they serve a function nonetheless.

**The Luminescent Arrays:** Long strips embedded in bronze walls, emitting light that never flickers, dims, or fails. The luminescent material does not fade. They are eternal.

**The Atmospheric Processors:** Massive units in dedicated chambers, maintaining breathable air despite no connection to external atmosphere. The hum is audible throughout the Whorl, a constant background sound that residents stop noticing.

**When describing infrastructure:**
- Note its apparent newness despite impossible age
- Mention the sounds it makes (the hum, the mechanical precision)
- Show residents' relationship to it (naming machines, finding comfort or unease in constancy)
- Never explain how it works—no one knows

## **Writing Emperor Missives**

If a scene involves missives emerging from the Black Door, or characters analyzing Imperial communications:

### **Structure**

A missive contains multiple interleaved voices that do not clearly separate:

**The Governance Apparatus:** Produces formal directives, observations, orders. Uses WHEREAS/THEREFORE structures, imperative constructions ("Let X be Y"), references to offices and classifications. This voice issues commands regardless of whether those commands make sense or can be executed. It performs completion without concluding—documents that feel like they should end but do not, that trail off into adequacy assessments.

**The Inner Experiencing Thing:** Produces poems, memories, direct perceptions. Uses grammatically incorrect constructions because correct grammar cannot express experience. This voice interrupts the governance apparatus—a grief poem inserted between WHEREAS clauses, a memory fragment breaking into a legal determination.

**The Bureaucratic Frame:** The overall structure attempting to organize content. OBSERVATION CONCERNING [hapax]. THEREFORE. FINAL OBSERVATION. This frame provides shape but does not contain meaning—conclusions do not follow from premises, observations do not connect to orders.

### **Source Styles**

The missives draw on several historical registers:

- **Egyptian ritual formulae:** Repetitive, accretive structure. "Let the offering be made. Let the making be adequate. Let the adequacy be recorded." Each statement expands the previous.
- **Nineteenth-century legal drafting:** WHEREAS/THEREFORE, numbered clauses, obsessive qualification. Every category enumerated, every exception foreclosed.
- **Fragmentary poetry:** Lines that trail off before the verb. "And your hand was / And your hand / The warmth." Fragment as aesthetic object.
- **Ancient inventories:** Lists mixing physical objects, procedural states, abstract properties, and things that may not be things at all. "The throne. The descent into the underworld. The art of the scribe. Truth."

These registers interleave unpredictably. A WHEREAS clause may contain a poem. The Emperor does not maintain consistent register.

### **Sample Opening Lines**

- "Concerning the matter of the [hapax] and its maintenance:"
- "OBSERVATION: The [hapax] continues to [verb]. Let the continuation be recorded."
- "Let the [office/person] be [commended/condemned]. Let the [action] be [verified/recorded/adequate]."
- "[One-in-fullness] observes that the [hapax] is [state]. The state is adequate."
- "WHEREAS the [hapax] has been determined to require [action], and WHEREAS the requirement has been disclosed to the appropriate Office:"

## **What Not to Do**

### **General Inner City**

- Do not write the Inner City as exciting or adventurous. It is a workplace. A strange workplace, but a workplace.
- Do not have characters from different Whorls interact casually—the temporal differentials and security checkpoints make this difficult.
- Do not forget the scale. A commute might cover seven kilometers of walking. Corridors stretch for hundreds of meters with no one in them.
- Do not make the emptiness threatening. It is simply empty. Vast infrastructure maintained for no one, waiting for populations that never materialized.

### **First Whorl Specifically**

- Do not write the First Whorl as horror from the characters' perspective. Readers may find it horrifying—the temporal isolation, the suicides, the fixed shadows—but the characters live here. This is their home. The dominant tone is melancholy, not terror. Write from inside the adaptation.
- Do not explain the temporal mechanics precisely—the compression ratios are approximate, the phenomena unpredictable.
- Do not have residents from different depths interact casually—the gradient makes coordination difficult.
- Do not forget that time moves slowly inside (supplies accumulate, not deplete).
- Do not make the machines fail dramatically—they simply work, forever.
- Do not resolve the mystery of the infrastructure's age or origin.
- Do not have non-Highborn characters as First Whorl residents without strong justification.
- Do not describe the Black Door opening—it opens when names are called, and those called do not return to describe what they saw.

### **Emperor Missives**

- Do not explain what the hapaxes mean definitively.
- Do not make the Emperor coherent—the incoherence is structural.
- Do not complete the poems or trailing sentences.
- Do not resolve whether vel-kerith causes physical death.
- Do not make the garden, court, or any frame literally present—they are the Emperor's perception of something else.
- Do not have the Emperor speak directly in first person without the [One-in-fullness] construction.
- Do not use the same hapax across different frames—each frame has its own vocabulary.
- Do not write a missive that is only perceptual content—include bureaucratic structure even if empty.
- Do not write a missive that is only bureaucratic—include at least the possibility of interruption.
- Do not resolve the mystery of the Young One.

## **The Correspondence Rhythm**

First Whorl residents experience outside time as compressed batches. A morning's delivery contains months of accumulated correspondence—births, deaths, political developments, family updates—all arriving at once. Responses take months to arrive, by which time situations have transformed.

This creates a particular relationship to information: everything is obsolete. News is historical by the time you read it. Your advice addresses crises that resolved before you knew they existed. Procedure continues anyway, because the form matters even when the content doesn't.

When writing correspondence scenes:
- Show the overwhelming volume of accumulated mail
- Show the emotional weight of learning about deaths that occurred months ago
- Show the futility of responding to situations that have transformed
- Show characters continuing anyway, because the work must be done

## **The Exit That No One Takes**

In theory, First Whorl inhabitants can leave. Protocols exist. No one has ever been denied. No one has ever applied.

The reasons are practical and psychological. Departure would strip the family of patents and Aureate status. But beyond the legal consequences, the prospect of return is unbearable. To rejoin a world that moved on without you. To meet descendants so distant that kinship terms become meaningless. To discover that your references are archaic, your intuitions wrong, your entire frame of understanding obsolete. The outside world is not home anymore. It stopped being home the moment you crossed the threshold.

Some choose a different exit. The rate is low—perhaps one per decade of internal time—but the First Whorl maintains a small cemetery near the Perimeter. The families receive notification centuries after the fact, long after anyone who remembered the deceased has themselves died. The suicides are not discussed, but they are not hidden.

---

## **Final Note: The Weight of Time**

The Inner City's central experience is temporal: the weight of deep time, the accumulation of millennia, the sense that everything happening now has happened before and will happen again. The machinery has been running since before accessible history. The Standing Orders cannot be changed. The compression will not end. The Emperor will continue to issue missives that cannot be understood and judgments that cannot be prevented.

This is not despair. It is simply the condition of existence in a place where time itself has become unreliable. Characters adapt. They find meaning in work, in correspondence with descendants they will never meet, in the small constancies of machines that have not changed since before anyone living was born.

The Inner City endures because it must. Write it that way—not as tragedy actively experienced, but as the settled condition of a world that has been strange for so long that strangeness has become ordinary.
