# The Ledger of Slights

- Cannot Compel but remember that they once could

## Central Question

What does it mean to hold authority you can no longer enforce?

---

## The Gun

**The Founding Ledger** - The oldest of the Völërath's grievance records, kept in the Archive Hall of Brennholm. This ledger does not merely record slights against the Völërath—it contains the original compact between the three refugee castes when they fled ya-Tsovez 10,000 years ago. The compact specifies that the Völërath would rule *until such time as their voice returned*. For millennia, everyone has assumed this clause is metaphorical. But the ledger also contains marginalia in a different hand—notes suggesting the voice *can* return, and describing the mechanism.

---

## Overview

In the caste-village of Brennholm on Puget Sound's eastern shore, the Völërath have maintained their position as harbor masters and record-keepers for ten thousand years through bureaucratic inertia and the weight of tradition. They cannot Compel—that power was lost within centuries of their flight from ya-Tsovez—but they conduct themselves as if the memory of Compulsion confers authority. The Szkovërin work the forges on their toxic promontory. The Draëthen weave in their synchronized compounds. And the Völërath count, record, and remember.

Now a ship has arrived from the north carrying an Avouvar trader with an unusual cargo: a Highborn from the Empire proper, unconscious and bound, being transported for reasons the trader will not disclose. The Völërath harbormaster who processed the ship's manifest has recognized something in the Highborn's features—a resemblance to the portraits in the Archive Hall. And she has begun to wonder if the clause in the Founding Ledger might not be metaphorical after all.

---

## Narrative Shape

The scenario explores the tension between inherited authority and earned legitimacy. The Völërath have ruled Brennholm not because they can enforce their will, but because the other castes have permitted it—partly from habit, partly because the Völërath's meticulous record-keeping serves everyone's interests. But permission can be withdrawn.

The arrival of a true Highborn—one who can Compel—threatens to expose the fiction at the heart of Brennholm's social order. If the Founding Ledger's clause is literal, and if a Highborn's voice could somehow "return" to the Völërath, what would that mean? Would they become rulers in truth rather than merely in name? And if so, would the Szkovërin and Draëthen accept it?

The forces in motion: the Avouvar trader wants to complete her delivery and leave before complications arise. The bound Highborn, when conscious, will want freedom—and may offer it in exchange for assistance. The Draëthen, who model systems with perfect fidelity, have already begun calculating what the Highborn's presence means for the village's power structure. And the Szkovërin, whose forges provide Brennholm's only valuable export, may decide that ten thousand years of Völërath "leadership" is quite enough.

## The Protagonist

### Keth val-Morren

**Physical Description**: Keth stands six feet seven inches—tall for baseline humans, middling for a Völërath. She is 67 years old, past youth but not yet into the long decline that takes her caste in their final decades. Her neck extends three inches longer than baseline, her fingers possess an extra knuckle's worth of length, and her skull extends slightly at the back in the manner of her kind. Her skin holds the bloodless pallor common to Völërath, going purple unevenly in the cold. Her eyes are pale grey, almost colorless in certain light. Her hair, ash-colored and fine, she keeps short—unusual among Völërath women, a practical concession to the salt wind at the harbor.

**Background**: Keth has served as assistant harbormaster for thirty-one years, processing manifests, calculating tariffs, and recording every transaction in the great ledgers that are her caste's pride and obsession. She was passed over for the chief harbormaster position twice—once for a man who died before assuming the role, once for her current superior, Theven val-Oss, who is now 118 and beginning to forget things. She has no children, no partner, no close friends. Her life is the harbor and the ledgers.

**What She Wants**: To be recognized. Not praised, not celebrated—recognized. Acknowledged as competent, as having contributed, as mattering. Thirty-one years of perfect records, and Theven still checks her arithmetic.

**What She Fears**: That she is exactly as insignificant as she suspects. That when she dies, no one will notice except as an administrative inconvenience. That the Völërath's authority is as hollow as she sometimes fears her own life to be.

**What She Can Do**: Keth can read and write in four scripts, including the archaic forms used in the oldest ledgers. She can calculate tariffs in her head faster than most can with counting boards. She knows the harbor's rhythms—which ships arrive when, which captains can be trusted, which cargoes require special handling. She has access to the Archive Hall, where the oldest records are kept.

**What She Cannot Do**: Keth cannot fight, cannot run, cannot endure physical hardship. She has never left Brennholm. She has no allies among the Szkovërin or Draëthen—her caste's habitual condescension has seen to that. She cannot Compel, though like all Völërath she sometimes catches herself speaking as if she expects obedience.

**What She Knows**: She knows that the ship from the north carried something unusual—she saw the bound figure being unloaded. She knows that Theven has been summoned to meet with the village council. She knows where the Founding Ledger is kept, and she knows that no one has read it in living memory.

**What She Doesn't Know**: She doesn't know why the Highborn was bound, or where they're being taken, or what the Avouvar trader's true purpose is. She doesn't know that the Draëthen have already begun their calculations. She doesn't know that the marginalia in the Founding Ledger was written by a Szkovërin smith who died four thousand years ago.

**Emotional Architecture**: Keth suppresses. She has spent sixty-seven years learning that her feelings do not matter, that her opinions are not wanted, that her role is to record and calculate and be silent. When she feels anger, she records it mentally—adds it to the ledger of slights she carries in her head. When she feels hope, she crushes it before it can disappoint her. But the arrival of the Highborn has stirred something she thought long dead: the possibility that things might change.

**Playing Keth**: Speak in precise, clipped sentences. Record everything. Notice details others miss. When someone dismisses you, don't react—but remember. The tension in playing Keth is between her habitual passivity and the growing realization that she might, for once, be in a position to act.

## Key Characters

### Theven val-Oss (Völërath, 118 years old)

**Physical Description**: Theven was once imposing even by Völërath standards—nearly seven feet tall, broad-shouldered, with a voice that carried across the harbor. Now he is stooped, his height reduced by the curvature of his spine, his voice a thin rasp. His eyes, once sharp, have clouded to the point where he can barely read. He uses the elaborate gesture-language that elderly Völërath develop for when their hearing fails—though his hearing remains, his patience for speech does not.

**Situation**: Theven has been chief harbormaster for sixty-two years. He knows he is failing—knows that Keth does most of the real work—but cannot bring himself to acknowledge it. The arrival of the Highborn has thrown him into crisis: he recognizes what this could mean, and he is terrified.

**What He Wants**: To die before everything collapses. To not be the harbormaster who lost Völërath authority. To be remembered as competent.

**How He Operates**: Theven hoards information. He attends meetings, nods sagely, and shares nothing with Keth that he doesn't have to. When forced to make decisions, he delays until circumstances decide for him.

**Speech Patterns**: Long pauses. Incomplete sentences. Gestures that substitute for words. "The matter will... we shall see. In time."

**Relationship to Keth**: He relies on her completely and resents her for it. He promoted her twice and passed her over twice, and he no longer remembers which decisions were which.

**Theme Connection**: Theven embodies blocked achievement—he achieved his position, but too late, and now he clings to it past the point of usefulness. His authority is real on paper and hollow in practice.

**Introduction Scene**: The morning after the ship arrives, Theven summons Keth to his office and asks her to describe the cargo manifest. He has already forgotten what she told him yesterday.

### Sivet Kalna (Avouvar Trader, 34 years old)

**Physical Description**: The Avouvar are radiation-adapted colonists with ink-black skin that seems to absorb rather than reflect light. Sivet is shorter than most of her kind—five feet four—with the characteristic hairlessness and slightly too-large eyes. She wears layers of treated leather against the Sound's damp cold, and she moves with the careful economy of someone who has learned that energy is precious.

**Situation**: Sivet was hired in the northern territories to transport the Highborn south. She was not told why, only that the cargo must arrive alive and that she would be paid enough to retire. She is now trapped in Brennholm—her crew refused to continue once they realized what they were carrying, and the village council has impounded her ship pending "tariff review."

**What She Wants**: To complete the delivery and get paid. Failing that, to escape with her ship and her life. She has no loyalty to her cargo.

**How She Operates**: Sivet lies fluently and often, but she also keeps her word once given. She assesses everyone she meets for usefulness and threat level. She has survived thirty-four years in a hostile world by being smarter than the people trying to exploit her.

**Speech Patterns**: Quick, clipped, with a northern accent that makes her vowels sharp. Avoids direct statements when indirect ones will serve. "One might wonder. One might also profit from wondering."

**Relationship to Keth**: Sivet sees Keth as a potential ally—someone with access to records and authority who might be persuaded to release the ship. She is cultivating Keth without being obvious about it.

**Theme Connection**: Sivet represents external disruption—the outside force that exposes the internal contradictions of Brennholm's social order.

**Introduction Scene**: Sivet finds Keth at the harbor the evening of the ship's arrival and asks, with studied casualness, how long tariff reviews typically take.

### The Highborn (Name Unknown, Age Unknown)

**Physical Description**: Even unconscious and bound, the Highborn commands attention. They are of the elongated lineage—over eight feet tall, with a neck that extends a full foot above the shoulders and a skull that sweeps backward in an elegant curve. Their skin is pale, faintly luminescent in the dark, and their fingers are so long and thin they seem to have too many joints. They wear a silver filigree headpiece that has been damaged, bent out of shape by rough handling.

**Situation**: The Highborn was taken from somewhere in the Empire proper—Sivet doesn't know where—and was sedated for the journey. The sedation is wearing off. When they wake, they will find themselves in a place where no Highborn has been seen for ten thousand years, surrounded by castes that their ancestors once ruled absolutely.

**What They Want**: Unknown. When they wake, they will want freedom first, and then answers.

**How They Operate**: Unknown until they wake. But their voice works. If they speak with Compulsion in their tone, every non-Highborn in the room will obey.

**Theme Connection**: The Highborn is the Wands card made manifest—pure potential, pure power, but bound and blocked. Their presence forces the question: what is authority without the capacity to enforce it, and what is power without the wisdom to use it?

**Introduction Scene**: The Highborn is being kept in a warehouse near the harbor, guarded by Draëthen who have volunteered for the duty. They will wake during the scenario's second act.

### Brennek Grey-Patch (Draëthen, 41 years old)

**Physical Description**: Brennek looks almost baseline—average height, average build, coloring within normal ranges. What marks him as Draëthen is the intensity of his gaze when focusing, his eyes set slightly too close together, and the mechanical precision of his movements. His stride matches itself step to step with unsettling regularity. He is called Grey-Patch for the birthmark on his left temple.

**Situation**: Brennek leads the Draëthen work-collective responsible for sorting the autofactory's output. When the village council asked for guards for the Highborn, he volunteered immediately. He has been standing outside the warehouse for sixteen hours, motionless, watching.

**What He Wants**: To understand. The Draëthen model systems with such fidelity that separate individuals reach identical conclusions—but the Highborn's arrival has introduced a variable they cannot model. Brennek needs data.

**How He Operates**: Brennek does not negotiate or discuss. He observes, calculates, and acts. When the Draëthen reach consensus on a course of action, they execute it without hesitation or mercy.

**Speech Patterns**: Flat, declarative statements. No hedging, no uncertainty. "The Highborn will wake within six hours. When they speak, we will know their purpose. Then we will decide."

**Relationship to Keth**: Brennek has never spoken to Keth individually—Draëthen do not engage with the Völërath except through official channels. But he is watching her now.

**Theme Connection**: Brennek and the Draëthen represent the question of legitimate authority. They follow the Völërath's administrative decisions because it is efficient, not because they acknowledge Völërath rule. If the calculus changes, so will their compliance.

**Introduction Scene**: Keth passes the warehouse on her way home from the harbor and sees Brennek standing at the door, motionless, his too-close eyes tracking her movements.

**Relationship to Keth**: Mava views Keth as slightly less insufferable than other Völërath—at least Keth works. But she has no interest in friendship.

## Key Locations

### The Archive Hall

The Archive Hall stands at the center of Brennholm's Völërath district—a stone building with doorways eight feet tall and ceilings higher still, built to Highborn proportions by ancestors who remembered what Highborn proportions meant. The interior is cool, dry, and dark; the windows are narrow slits designed to admit light without moisture. Shelves line every wall, floor to ceiling, holding ten thousand years of records.

The Founding Ledger rests in a locked cabinet at the hall's center, on a reading stand that has not been moved in living memory. The cabinet's key hangs around the neck of Lenna val-Kess, the Archive Keeper, who is 132 years old and can no longer see well enough to read what she guards.

This is where resolution will occur. Whatever Keth decides about the Highborn, about her caste, about her own life—she will decide it here, with the weight of ten millennia of records pressing down on her.

### The Harbor Office

A wooden building at the end of the main pier, salt-stained and perpetually damp. Two desks, one for Theven (empty more often than not) and one for Keth (perpetually buried in papers). A window overlooking the harbor. The manifest from the Northern Star sits on Keth's desk, its irregular entry circled in red ink: CARGO: SEALED. PASSENGER: SECURED. NO INSPECTION.

### The Warehouse

A converted fish-processing building near the harbor, now emptied and cleaned as best as possible. The Highborn lies on a makeshift bed in the center of the main floor, bound hand and foot with rope, guarded by Draëthen who stand at the walls and watch. The sedation is wearing off.

### The Forge Compound

On the promontory east of the village, where the soil is grey and nothing grows. The Szkovërin live and work here, in buildings that run hot enough to be uncomfortable for outsiders. The forges burn day and night. The smoke rises in columns that can be seen for miles.

## The Introduction

The ship came with the morning tide.

Keth watched it from the harbor office window, a northern vessel with black sails and the Avouvar trading sigil painted on its hull. Unusual, but not unprecedented—Avouvar traders arrived monthly, their ships loaded with goods from the contaminated north. This one was off-schedule by two weeks and riding low in the water, which meant heavy cargo or an overfull hold.

She was at the pier before the gangplank dropped, manifest ledger in hand, stylus ready. The Avouvar captain—short, dark-skinned, moving with the careful economy of her kind—came down first, followed by four sailors who looked like they hadn't slept in days.

"Tariff review," Keth said. Standard opening. "Cargo manifest and passenger list."

The captain handed over a single sheet. Keth scanned it, her stylus tapping the entries. Metals, yes—the north had metals. Preserved fish, spices, rendered fat. Standard trade goods, properly taxed.

Then she reached the final line.

CARGO: SEALED. PASSENGER: SECURED. NO INSPECTION.

"What is this?" Keth asked.

The captain's too-large eyes met hers. "Exactly what it says."

"I need to inspect all cargo. That's the regulation."

"Then you'll need to change the regulation."

Keth felt the familiar tightness in her chest—the sensation of being dismissed, of her authority being weighed and found wanting. She recorded the slight automatically, adding it to the mental ledger she'd carried since childhood.

"The harbormaster will—"

"The harbormaster will do what he's told," the captain said. "As will you. As will everyone in this village when they understand what I've brought."

Behind the captain, two of the sailors were carrying something down the gangplank. Something wrapped in canvas, something that was too long and too thin and shaped wrong for any cargo Keth had ever seen.

Something that moved.

Keth watched them carry it past, her stylus frozen over the manifest. Through a gap in the canvas, she caught a glimpse of pale skin, of fingers that seemed to have too many joints, of a face that echoed the portraits in the Archive Hall—portraits of the ancestors who had founded Brennholm ten thousand years ago, when the Völërath could still command obedience with a word.

"What have you brought here?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

The captain smiled. It was not a kind smile.

"The future," she said. "Or possibly the past. I've never been entirely sure which."

## The Mystery

The truth hidden in the Founding Ledger is this: the compact was never about Compulsion returning. The Szkovërin smith who wrote the marginalia—a woman named Veth Ironhand, ancestor to Mava—understood the document's original intent. The phrase "until such time as their voice returned" referred not to the supernatural power of Compulsion, but to the Völërath learning to speak as equals rather than masters.

The marginalia describes a ceremony: if the leaders of all three castes stand together in the Archive Hall and read the compact aloud, the Völërath acknowledging their dependence on the Szkovërin's labor and the Draëthen's precision, the compact will be "fulfilled"—the voice will have "returned" in the sense that the Völërath will finally be speaking truthfully.

No one has read the marginalia in four thousand years. The Völërath assumed the notes were technical annotations. The Szkovërin who might have known the tradition are long dead. The Draëthen, who model systems with perfect fidelity, never had the data.

The Highborn's arrival disrupts this forgotten arrangement. If the Highborn wakes and speaks with Compulsion, they could command the entire village—including the Völërath, who lost their immunity along with their power. But the Highborn is bound, confused, and potentially willing to bargain. And Sivet, the trader, knows more than she's telling about why the Highborn was being transported and where they were supposed to go.

When Keth reads the Founding Ledger and the marginalia, she will face a choice: use the Highborn to force obedience (betraying everything her caste claims to value), reveal the truth and let the other castes judge whether the Völërath have earned their position (risking everything), or destroy the evidence and maintain the hollow authority her caste has clung to for ten millennia (becoming exactly what she fears she already is: insignificant).

## Trajectories

### If Keth Reveals the Truth

Keth brings the Founding Ledger to a council meeting and reads the compact aloud, including the marginalia. The Szkovërin and Draëthen are forced to confront the possibility that the Völërath's authority could become legitimate—if they choose to grant it. This path leads to a ceremony in the Archive Hall, with Mava and Brennek standing alongside Keth, acknowledging their interdependence. The Highborn, now awake, watches in confusion as a ritual renders their power irrelevant. The 4 of Wands (Reversed) becomes upright: blocked celebration becomes true community.

### If Keth Uses the Highborn

Keth bargains with the Highborn: freedom in exchange for using Compulsion to establish Völërath dominance. The Highborn agrees, desperate to escape. But Compulsion is a blunt instrument, and the Draëthen—who do not give quarter—respond with violence. The scenario ends in blood, with Brennholm's fragile peace shattered. The 4 of Wands remains reversed: the celebration is blocked forever.

### If Keth Destroys the Evidence

Keth burns the Founding Ledger rather than face its implications. The Highborn is returned to Sivet, who completes her delivery. Brennholm returns to its hollow stability, and Keth returns to her ledgers, recording slights she will never avenge. The 4 of Wands reversed becomes her permanent state: authority without substance, achievement without meaning.

### If Keth Does Nothing

Keth hesitates too long. The Highborn wakes, speaks with Compulsion, and commands the guards to release them. Chaos ensues as the Draëthen calculate, the Szkovërin resist, and the Völërath discover that they are as susceptible to Compulsion as any other caste. Brennholm falls into a three-way conflict that will take generations to resolve. The 4 of Wands reversed becomes inverted: not blocked celebration, but active destruction of everything that might have been celebrated.
