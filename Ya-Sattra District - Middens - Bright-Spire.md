# Bright-Spire

> **Type:** Tenement / Chain-Men stronghold
> **Population:** ~2,700 residents, 83 Chain-Men enforcers
> **Function:** Residential extraction zone, aquaculture
> **Notable feature:** Tapered architecture creating natural chokepoints

Bright-Spire's architecture embodies its social reality. The structure tapers as it rises, each floor smaller than the one below, compressing 2,700 residents into increasingly constrained spaces while 83 Chain-Men control movement through strategic bottlenecks.

## Access and Isolation

The building rises from the harbor itself—foundations submerged, accessible primarily by boat or by bridges connecting to Hollow-Egress. This makes it defensible: the Chain-Men need only hold bridges and stair-landings. But it also isolates the building from commercial life.

The connecting bridge at the eleventh floor allows Chain-Men to access Gallows drinking halls without descending to street level, where their victims might organize.

## Building Structure

### Sub-Basements: Bannick Aquaculture

Six levels of partially flooded space maintain different chemical compositions for specialized protein production. Bioluminescent algae provide dim light; eels adapted to industrial toxins; mussels concentrate metals in their shells rather than flesh.

The Bannicks work in near-darkness, their skin showing characteristic chemical lesions from chronic exposure to water that technically supports life but slowly destroys those who live in it. **Output:** ~100 kilograms of protein weekly, most of which Chain-Men tax or contaminate as punishment.

### Ground Through Second Floors: Maximum Density

The widest part of the spire houses families redefining density. Eight people occupy spaces designed for two. Salvaged materials create vertical subdivisions within already tiny rooms.

The architecture here still allows lateral movement—corridors wide enough for two people to pass, communal spaces capable of holding gatherings—making these floors the most politically significant. Organized resistance remains theoretically possible where people can meet without passing through controlled chokepoints.

### Third Floor: Sela's Kitchen and Chapel

Communal functions persist despite Chain-Men surveillance. The kitchen serves increasingly thin porridge to growing queues. The chapel altar—a salvaged industrial valve painted with revolutionary iconography disguised as religious imagery—provides focal point for the prayer circle's barely concealed militancy.

**The Prayer Circle:** 200 armed residents, weapons hidden as religious objects:
- Sapphire-glass knives sold as prayer beads
- Crossbows tensioned with leaf springs
- Cargo hooks serving "obvious legitimate purposes" until they don't

### Fourth Through Ninth Floors: The Compression Gradient

Each ascending floor loses approximately fifteen percent of the area below it:
- **Fifth floor:** Corridors require single-file movement
- **Seventh floor:** Adults must duck through doorways
- **Ninth floor:** Even small residents navigate by turning sideways

This architectural funnel transforms the stairwell into a series of defensible positions where one Chain-Man can control all vertical movement.

**Psychological effects:** Residents develop compensatory behaviors—walking single-file in open spaces, automatically turning sideways at doorways, calculating passage width before attempting movement.

### Tenth Floor: Calculated Provocation

Deacon Sela Loak lives directly below the Chain-Men barracks. The floor vibrates with their movement above—constant reminder of proximity to violence.

Her prayer circle maintains visible presence here: weapons obvious enough to establish deterrent, concealed enough to avoid triggering massacre.

**The fundamental calculation:** 2,700 against 83 favors eventual revolution. The architecture favors defense. The question isn't whether violence will erupt but whether it will flow upward through narrowing stairs or downward from positions of elevation.

### Eleventh and Twelfth Floors: Chain-Men Territory

Modified to suit Chain-Men preferences—floor structures broken through to create vertical connections trusting pre-Interdict core to hold despite vandalism.

Their common area reeks of home-distilled alcohol: industrial ethanol poisoned with methanol, fusel oils, occasionally hallucinogenic compounds from Bannick pools. They don't care. Warborn cancer will kill them by forty regardless of what they drink.

### The Peak: Benku Crane

Where the building narrows to eight meters across, the Benku family maintains the crane generating Bright-Spire's only legitimate income. Maximum elevation with minimum freedom—they can see the entire harbor but cannot leave without passing through Chain-Men territory.

The crane's reach extends fifty meters horizontally, allowing direct loading from ships to Ridge-bound wagons, bypassing dock fees and ground-level taxation. This geographic advantage would make the Benkus wealthy if they could keep their earnings.

Instead: the peculiar torture of handling fortune while living in poverty.
