## Core Principle: Rent Extraction

The Imperial economy generates wealth through extraction rather than production. Aureate families don't make goods; they authenticate. The es-Tsay patent doesn't produce lectors; it makes lectors function. The distinction is fundamental: capitalist profit comes from creating value; Aureate rent comes from taxing value others create.

Guild monopolies operate similarly. The Clothiers' Guild doesn't compete for customers through price or quality; their charter grants exclusive rights regardless of performance. A merchant cannot undercut guild prices by producing more efficiently; the charter forbids competition.

This creates an economy of inherited position rather than market competition. The guilds were chartered millennia ago by authority that no longer functions to revoke them. The patents self-enforce through quantum encryption. No entrepreneur can disrupt these arrangements through innovation.

**Historical parallel:** Late medieval guild economy, but more calcified. Medieval guilds could be dissolved by royal authority or undermined by rural production; Imperial guilds face no such pressures.

## Major Institutions

### Many's Countinghouse (Empire-Wide)

**What it does:** Banking, contract witnessing, deposit services, arbitration. Many is a colonial polychaete organism—thousands of specialized worm-segments functioning as a single consciousness—that has become the Empire's de facto central banking system. Many exists simultaneously in every major human city, not as franchises but as literally the same organism.

**Cash flow in:**
- Transaction fees: 0.5-2% of witnessed contracts, scaling with complexity
- Deposit fees: 0.1% monthly on stored funds
- Arbitration fees: 5-50 obols per proceeding depending on stakes
- Interest on loans: typically 1.5% monthly
- Real estate holdings accumulated over millennia

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 2-4 million obols in direct fees, plus investment returns on reserves exceeding 100 million obols.

**Cash flow out:**
- Ta-Kefyeh enforcement contracts: ~200,000 obols/year
- Facility maintenance: ~150,000 obols/year
- Vendetta funds for contract violators: unknown
- Nothing on executive compensation or biological needs—Many requires only organic matter to grow additional zooids

**Decision-making:** Many cannot lie, cannot understand politics, cannot model individual consciousness. Decisions emerge from biological processes that embody contracts as neural tissue. When Many creates an agreement, it literally grows brain matter configured to that agreement, making violation physically impossible.

**Distinctive feature:** The famous egalitarian queue. Ta-Kefyeh guards physically remove anyone attempting to skip ahead regardless of status. This creates the only truly egalitarian space in ya-Sattra.

### The Salvage Guild (Empire-Wide)

**What it does:** Monopoly charter for processing, certifying, and selling materials recovered from pre-Interdict ruins throughout Imperial territory. Nothing salvaged anywhere in the Empire can be legally sold without Guild certification.

**Scale of operations:**
- Headquarters: The great dome in the Iron Yards (ya-Sattra)
- 147 master memberships at headquarters
- Major branches in ya-Don, the Threshing Coast, every significant port
- Thousands of contractors operating under master licenses

**Cash flow in:**
- Auction and transaction fees (18-30%): 12-20 million obols/year
- Certification fees: 3-6 million obols/year
- Training programs: 1-2 million obols/year
- Storage fees: 1-2 million obols/year
- Credit system interest: 500,000-1 million obols/year

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 20-35 million obols, making the Guild one of the wealthiest non-Aureate institutions in the Empire.

**Cash flow out:**
- Bureau relationships Empire-wide: ~500,000-800,000 obols/year
- Legal defense: ~200,000 obols/year (perpetual lawsuit with Rod Aureates)
- Staff: ~3-4 million obols/year
- Infrastructure: ~1-2 million obols/year

**Decision-making:** The Grandmaster is selected through weighted voting where masters' votes count proportionally to revenue contribution. The 147 master memberships, fixed by charter, create an aristocracy—real decisions happen through informal negotiation among working masters.

**Distinctive feature:** The Guild maintains two sets of ledgers. The "White Books" satisfy Bureau auditors; the "Grey Books" record actual transactions including patent violations. Everyone knows. Both parties benefit from legal ambiguity.

### The Companions Guild (Empire-Wide)

**What it does:** Absolute monopoly over the care and management of semi-sentient beings (Mutterers, kalbats, and the Sickly) throughout Imperial territory. They operate the Empire's hospice system in partnership with the Bureau of the Creche and the Penitent Church.

**Scale of operations:**
- Headquarters: The Cathedral of the Plea (ya-Sattra)
- Major facilities: Monastery of the Silent Song (kalbat care)
- Chapter houses in every major Imperial city
- Total personnel: Perhaps 2,000-3,000 Companions Empire-wide

**Cash flow in:**
- Bureau of the Rod contracts: 4-6 million obols/year
- Bureau of the Creche contracts: 2-3 million obols/year
- Private Mutterer choir rentals: 1-2 million obols/year
- Serum sales: 1-2 million obols/year
- Training and hospice fees: 1.5-3 million obols/year

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 10-15 million obols.

**Cash flow out:**
- Companion salaries: 3-5 million obols/year
- Hospice operations: 2-3 million obols/year
- Care for Mutterers, Sickly, kalbats: 2-3 million obols/year
- Cathedral and Archive maintenance: ~300,000 obols/year

**Decision-making:** The Guild hierarchy runs from Initiate through Keeper, Companion, and Sri Companion to the Master of the Guild. Political factions exist among senior Companions balancing different client relationships.

**Distinctive feature:** The Mutterer monopoly is absolute. No one else can manage Mutterer choirs. The Bureau of the Rod literally cannot maintain Imperial infrastructure without the Guild.

### The Clothiers' Guild (Representative Local Guild)

**What it does:** Monopoly charter for textile production and sale within defined territory in the Medina Quarter. Representative of hundreds of similar local guild operations.

**Cash flow in:**
- Sales: 600-900 obols monthly for a medium guild shop
- Membership dues: 5-15 obols/month per worker
- Subcontracting fees from unlicensed producers

**Annual revenue:** A typical guild master with twelve employees might gross 8,000-12,000 obols annually.

**Cash flow out:**
- Materials: 30-40% of revenue
- Labor: journeymen 250-350 obols/month, apprentices 80-100 obols/month
- Guild dues: 3-5% of revenue
- Shop rent: 60-100 obols/month
- Bureau certification: 50-100 obols annually

**Decision-making:** The guild master makes daily operational decisions. The guild council sets prices, quality standards, and territorial boundaries.

**Distinctive feature:** The charter assumes automated production. When it specifies "output tolerances of 0.3%," this made sense for machines; human weavers cannot achieve such precision. The guild has developed elaborate inspection rituals that nominally verify compliance while actually approving everything that isn't obviously defective.

### Bureau of the Coin Revenue Station

**What it does:** Tax collection, currency authentication, financial regulation for a defined territory. One of dozens of stations throughout ya-Sattra.

**Cash flow in:**
- Transaction taxes: 0.5-1% of commercial transactions exceeding 10 obols
- Currency authentication fees: 0.1 obol per transaction
- Licensing fees: 50-200 obols annually per business
- Fine collection: 5,000-10,000 obols monthly

**Annual throughput:** A busy Medina station might process 50,000-80,000 obols monthly in taxes and fees.

**Cash flow out:**
- Staff: 70,000-150,000 obols/year
- Facility: 100-150 obols/month
- Remittance: 85-90% flows to central Bureau accounts

**Decision-making:** Station chiefs have limited discretion—fee schedules are set by Bureau central authority. The chief's real authority lies in enforcement priority: which violations to pursue, which to overlook, which businesses to audit.

**Distinctive feature:** The station maintains authentication scales calibrated to Bureau standards—the only legal measure of currency weight in its territory.
