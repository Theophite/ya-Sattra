# Near-Baseline

> **Status:** Empire's majority population
> **Defining feature:** What they lack (visible modifications)
> **Hidden modification:** Universal susceptibility to Compulsion
> **Classification threshold:** 94%+ near-baseline genetics

Most Imperial subjects appear almost natural. Near-baselines present the full range of human variation that existed before engineering began—height, build, coloring, and features distributed across populations without the distinctive markers identifying other castes.

## The Hidden Modification

**Compulsion susceptibility:** The neurological receptors that respond to Highborn vocal frequencies exist in every near-baseline brain. This modification is stable and heritable, passed from parent to child across ten thousand years without degradation.

**Experience of Compulsion:** Body acting without permission—mouth opening, words emerging, conscious mind watching from somewhere behind the eyes. Lasts perhaps ten seconds. Victims still dream about it sometimes.

## Type Specimen: Tam Orveth

**Age:** 29 | **Occupation:** Joiner's apprentice, Medina Quarter

**Physical description:**
- Five foot eight, brown skin, dark hair, calloused hands
- Nothing about his appearance marks him as engineered
- One among millions of ordinary people constituting the Empire's majority

**Daily life:**
- Wakes early, walks to shop, shapes wood into furniture components
- Eats lunch from street vendors
- Returns to shaping wood, walks home
- Eats dinner with widowed mother and younger sister, sleeps

**Concerns:** Whether the master will elevate him to journeyman, whether his sister's suitor is suitable, whether rent increases will force relocation.

**Compulsion experience:** Exactly once, during legal proceeding where a Highborn magistrate commanded him to speak truthfully.

**Knowledge of caste system:** Abstract, learned in Bureau schools as child. The Highborn exist, they rule, they generally leave people like him alone. His opinions about Compulsion approximately same as opinions about earthquakes: it happens, it's frightening, you hope it doesn't happen to you.

## Trace Markers

Many near-baselines show trace markers from intermarriage across generations:
- Tam's grandmother was half-stevedore, contributing slightly broader shoulders
- The Creche would classify him as 94% near-baseline if anyone checked
- No one has checked; no one is likely to

**Classification:** One undifferentiated unit among the millions who keep the Empire functioning.

## Position in Empire

Near-baselines constitute the working population that makes civilization function:
- Craftspeople, merchants, clerks
- Service workers, laborers (non-specialized)
- Families, communities, ordinary lives

The system functions through mutual distance more than active enforcement. Near-baselines rarely encounter Highborn directly; when they do, the experience is memorable and unwelcome.
