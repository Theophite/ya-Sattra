**Initiation**

I'm twenty-eight years old and I've been initiated into the Third Degree of the Testament Society of the Fifth Oracle, and I still don't know what the fuck the whistles mean.

My father knows. Or I thought he knew. He's Seventh Degree, which means he's spent forty years studying fragments of the Jargon, learning harmonic patterns that make my teeth ache when he demonstrates them, developing the kind of cognitive changes that mean he sometimes forgets how to speak in linear sentences. He'll start explaining something and his words will fracture into parallel statements, each one true, each one proving the others, and I have to wait for him to remember that I haven't learned the preparatory forms for processing information that way.

Last year, during my initiation to Third Degree, I asked him directly. We were alone in our household shrine, and I'd just spent six hours learning a gesture-sequence that hurt to perform—fingers positioned at angles that felt wrong, held while vocalizing tones that wanted to resolve but couldn't. My sponsor had said this sequence was essential for understanding the Fifth Testament's mathematical substrate. I'd done it perfectly. I felt something shift in how I perceived the shrine's geometry, saw patterns in the incense smoke that hadn't existed before, or had always existed and I'd simply lacked the framework to perceive them.

I asked my father: "What do the whistles mean?"

He looked at me with an expression I'd never seen before. Not the redirection we give outsiders. Not the patient opacity we maintain for those outside our Testament Societies. Something rawer. Trapped.

"I don't know," he said.

"But you're Seventh Degree."

"I know what Seventh Degree initiates are permitted to know about the whistles. Which is more than you know. Which is less than Eighth Degree knows." He paused. "There is no Eighth Degree in our Society. The Sixth Testament Society claims to have one, but they won't confirm this to outsiders, which includes us."

I understood then what I'd been refusing to see my entire life. The initiation doesn't end. The revelation doesn't arrive. You climb from First to Second to Third Degree, and each time they teach you something real—actual knowledge, mathematical frameworks that change how you think, geometric principles that reveal patterns invisible to baseline cognition. The knowledge is genuine. The preparation is necessary. And it never leads anywhere.

My sponsor is Fifth Degree. She knows things I don't know, can perform calculations I can't perform, perceives regularities in seemingly random events that I'm still learning to detect. She's fifty-three years old. She's been studying the Fifth Testament since she was sixteen. And when I watch her during our Quiet Day observances, when I see her arranging herself on the crater slopes with the others, singing those layered, discordant tones that match the autumn whistles, I can see it in her posture: she's waiting for revelation too.

She knows more than me. She doesn't know enough. She may never know enough. And she can't tell me this directly because part of Third Degree initiation is learning that you don't ask Fourth Degree initiates whether Fourth Degree is sufficient. You assume revelation exists at Fifth. When you reach Fifth, you assume it exists at Sixth. The structure sustains itself through perpetual deferral.

My grandfather was Ninth Degree before he died. The Testament Society of the Second Oracle has nine degrees—or they did thirty years ago. They might have ten now. They don't discuss this with other Societies. My grandfather could do things with his voice that shouldn't be possible, could produce harmonics that made objects vibrate at frequencies that revealed structural flaws invisible to normal inspection. He worked in the cattle processing facilities, using his capabilities to assess which cuts would develop problems, which procedures needed adjustment. Essential work. Real application of real knowledge.

He never told my father what the whistles mean. Even on his deathbed, when my father asked directly, he said: "I can tell you what they signify in the context of the Second Testament's cryptographic framework. That's what Ninth Degree permits. But what they *mean*—" He stopped. Coughed. Started again. "Ask someone from the First Testament Society. They might know. Or they might know someone who knows."

The First Testament Society doesn't discuss this with outsiders. We're all outsiders to each other.

I carry palanquins to the eighth complex. I've done this three times. Each time, I understand marginally more about the geometric patterns on the bronze doors, can trace slightly more of the seven hundred twenty-nine distinct paths that lead to centers I can't quite resolve. Third Degree initiation taught me to see those paths. Fourth Degree will teach me... something about their relationship to the Fifth Testament's mathematical substrate, probably. Some framework that makes the patterns more comprehensible without actually explaining what they're for.

My daughter is seven years old. She doesn't attend Testament Society meetings yet—you begin at twelve, traditionally, though some families start earlier if the child shows aptitude. She asks me questions I can't answer. "Why do the stones on the cairns whistle when wind hits them?" "Why do we sing on Quiet Days?" "Why did you carry that little person with big eyes to the building with no windows?"

I tell her what my father told me: these are traditional observances. The calendar is complex. She'll understand when she's older. And I watch her learn the most important lesson, the one that comes before any formal initiation: some questions redirect to nowhere. Not because the answers are secret. Because the people who seem to know the answers are themselves waiting for someone who knows more.

She'll join a Testament Society at twelve. Probably the Fifth—it's customary for children to follow their parents' Society, though not mandatory. She'll spend six years reaching Third Degree, learning genuine mathematical frameworks that will change how she thinks. She'll ask me if Third Degree is sufficient for understanding. I'll tell her Fourth Degree reveals more. She'll believe me because it'll be true. Fourth Degree does reveal more. Just not enough. Never enough.

The Gan-Derem compound exports perfect memory cores. My cousin married into that family. Her husband processes documentation he doesn't understand, produced by relatives he's never met, working in halls he's never entered. He was born into this. He'll die in this. And in between, he'll perform essential work that generates millions of obols annually while understanding perhaps three percent of the system he maintains.

He's not an initiate. He doesn't study Testaments or learn the Jargon or develop cognitive changes that make baseline conversation difficult. But he's caught in the same structure: always adjacent to revelation, never reaching it. His cousins in the production halls know more than him. They probably have their own hierarchy—apprentice level, journeyman level, master level, each one teaching fragments of methodology without revealing complete processes. Because maybe complete processes don't exist. Maybe it's fragments all the way up.

Or maybe the Eighth Degree initiates know. Maybe the Ninth. Maybe there's a Tenth Degree that seven families preserve through marriages that span six Records, knowledge so specific it can't survive outside particular bloodlines, revelation so complete it justifies all the preparation.

Maybe.

I participate in my Testament Society meetings twice weekly. We gather in a compound near the Second Cradle—not the Cradle itself, just adjacent to it, close enough that we can feel the wrongness that permeates those spaces where transcendent intelligences achieved consciousness and departed. We practice vocal harmonics. We learn gesture-sequences. My sponsor demonstrates mathematical frameworks that reveal patterns in seemingly random distributions.

Last week, she taught us a technique for perceiving temporal regularities—not prediction, exactly, but recognition of rhythmic structures in events that appear disconnected. After three hours of practice, I could detect the pattern underlying the autumn whistles' activation. Not what causes them. Not what they mean. Just that they follow a mathematical sequence correlating with wind pressure, temperature gradients, and something else I can't identify. Seven variables at minimum. I can perceive three clearly, suspect a fourth, sense the presence of three more beyond my current comprehension.

Fourth Degree will teach me to perceive the fourth variable. Probably. Or it'll teach me something else that makes the fourth variable apparent as a side effect. Or it'll teach me there are actually nine variables and I'm perceiving three of them incorrectly.

I don't know. My sponsor doesn't tell me. Not because she's withholding—she's generous with knowledge, patient when I struggle with concepts that require cognitive frameworks I haven't developed yet. She doesn't tell me because Fourth Degree revelation is Fourth Degree revelation. You can't explain it to someone who hasn't learned Third Degree thoroughly. The concepts don't transfer. You'd have to already understand them to understand the explanation.

This is probably true. It feels like redirection. It's impossible to distinguish between genuine pedagogical necessity and systematic opacity. Maybe they're the same thing. Maybe knowledge that requires specific cognitive preparation is inherently opaque to those who lack that preparation, and the Testament Societies simply acknowledge this honestly instead of pretending everyone can understand everything.

Or maybe we're all trapped in a system that sustains itself through perpetual incompletion, always promising revelation at the next degree, the next initiation, the next level of understanding that remains forever one step beyond reach.

My father is Seventh Degree and doesn't know what the whistles mean. My grandfather was Ninth Degree and couldn't say. Somewhere in ya-Tsatsa, there might be Tenth Degree initiates who possess complete understanding. Or there might be Tenth Degree initiates who know Eleventh Degree exists. Or the whole structure might be preparation for knowledge that was lost when the Technical Castes were sterilized, when the Archons fled to Far Ib, when the Eighth Oracle delivered its false Testament and humanity broke itself trying to refuse.

We're preserving something. The Testament Societies maintain mathematical frameworks that produce real results. The Threefold Mirror Academy trains students who proceed to the Institute of the Lattice at unprecedented rates. The seven families export software that works, always, perfectly. The eighth complex accepts what we bring it and the city continues functioning. This isn't theater. The knowledge is genuine.

It's just incomplete. Always incomplete. Forever incomplete.

I'm learning to speak in fragments like my father. Not because I've achieved his level of understanding, but because linear sentences increasingly feel inadequate for concepts that exist in parallel, that prove each other through acoustic resonance, that can't be written down without losing essential structure. My daughter watches me struggle with this. She asks why I talk like that sometimes. I tell her she'll understand when she's older.

She will. And she won't. She'll understand more than she does now. She'll understand less than she needs to. She'll carry palanquins to the eighth complex and watch darkness swallow small bodies draped in wildflowers. She'll sing on Quiet Days, producing tones her vocal cords shouldn't be able to generate, harmonizing with hundreds of other Tsatsans in patterns that have no visible conductor. She'll redirect outsiders' questions with practiced smoothness while internally screaming at her own ignorance.

This is what we inherit. Not knowledge—anticipation. The certainty that revelation exists combined with the impossibility of reaching it. A Shepard tone of ascending understanding that climbs forever without arriving anywhere. We're all waiting. First Degree initiates wait for Second. Seventh Degree initiates wait for Eighth. The seven families wait for something the eighth complex provides through mechanisms they can't observe.

The waiting is the structure. The incompletion is the inheritance. And we pass it to our children with the same careful preparation our parents used, teaching them not what things mean but how to approach meaning, not revelation but perpetual preparation for revelation that may only exist as preparation.

The whistles will drone when autumn comes. I'll hear patterns I couldn't hear last year. I still won't know what they mean. My sponsor will hear patterns I can't hear yet. She won't know either. My father will perceive regularities neither of us can detect. He's Seventh Degree. It's not enough. It's never enough.

And next year, if I'm selected, I'll undergo Fourth Degree initiation. I'll learn something genuine. Something real. Something insufficient.

The ascent continues. The revelation recedes. Traditional observance, we say. The archives might have documentation.

They don't. They never did. The archives preserve procedures and precedents and historical records of procedures and precedents. Knowledge exists elsewhere—in vocal harmonics, in gesture-sequences, in cognitive frameworks that can't be written down because writing destroys the parallel proof structures that make them comprehensible.

Or that's what I tell myself. Because acknowledging that even the highest initiates might not know, that the system might sustain itself through collective incompletion rather than progressive revelation, that we might all be waiting for something that exists only as waiting—

That's the kind of thought you don't voice. Not because it's forbidden. Because saying it would require explaining it to someone, and explanation would reveal that you can't distinguish between genuine pedagogical structure and systematic opacity. You'd have to already understand the difference to understand why you don't understand the difference.

My daughter will learn this soon enough. The preparation begins before formal initiation. It has to. Otherwise the first degree would be incomprehensible.

The city continues. The software ships. The processions proceed. And we climb toward revelation that exists, always, precisely one degree beyond wherever we've reached.

Traditional observance.

Perhaps the archives have documentation.
