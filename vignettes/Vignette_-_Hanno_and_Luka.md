**Hanno and Luka**

The chapel at Valve-Saint carried the smell of industrial lubricant and old incense. Luka had cleared it for this meeting \- just the four of them, with Valvehands posted outside. The Hanged Man badge on the floor caught the flickering candlelight, its brass surface worn smooth by generations of handling.

Hanno stared at it without moving, that terrible stillness settling over him like armor.

"Pick it up," Luka said. His 2.4 meter frame remained perfectly relaxed, but there was something in his positioning that suggested he could reach the door faster than anyone else if violence erupted.

"No."

"You already work for me." Luka's lipless mouth barely moved, the words emerging from that slash of a face with absolute certainty. "You followed my orders. You disciplined my cousin for unauthorized killing. The Astal vendor was under Yen Tam protection \- Garren violated treaty. I sent you to make an example."

The lie was so bold it felt like being slapped. Jak's fingers twitched in those nervous patterns, but he said nothing. Mesca watched from near the altar, already calculating how this would play in the intelligence markets.

"That's notâ€”" Hanno began.

"That's what happened." Luka cut him off with the voice of someone who'd been rewriting history since birth. "The Chain-Men saw you beat Garren. They saw their commander disciplined. By tomorrow, they'll know it was on my authority. My enforcer, carrying out my will."

He moved then, that fluid Springheel motion, until he stood directly in front of the badge without quite blocking Hanno's view of it.

"You think Garren will let this go? Half-blind, one-armed, but still breathing. Still his father's son, eleven generations of Warborn rage. He'll come for you. Maybe alone, maybe with Jarek's Iron Yards Boys. Unless..."

The pause stretched. Luka's face, inhuman as it was, revealed nothing.

"Unless you're one of mine. A Hanged Man. Protected by Knot law." His head tilted slightly, studying Hanno with those eyes that sat too deep in his skull. "I'm doing you a favor, fostering. Your Venn family can't protect you from what's coming. Your Guild master won't shield you from Garren's revenge. But I can."

"I don't needâ€”"

"You need exactly what I'm offering." Luka's voice carried the particular tone of someone explaining something to a child. "Legitimacy. Protection. Purpose. You've been collecting debts for the Guild, breaking fingers for scrap metal. I'm offering you real work. The kind that matters."

Mesca shifted slightly. "The documentation would be straightforward. Appointment papers, backdated. The beating becomes official Knot business."

Luka didn't look at her, keeping his focus on Hanno. "Garren exceeded his authority. He was disciplined. By my new enforcer, who's been working for me for months. That's the story. That's the truth."

The trap was perfect. By claiming credit for the beating, Luka transformed personal revenge into Knot business. Hanno could accept the badge and protection, or refuse and face Garren's retaliation alone. Either way, Luka won \- either gaining a powerful enforcer or watching a potential threat eliminate itself.

"The Vennsâ€”" Hanno started.

"Remain as they are. Under Knot protection like every family that pays their water fees." Luka's tone suggested this was generosity rather than existing arrangement. "Your fostering father keeps his Guild appointment. The lift continues operating."

He stepped back from the badge, giving Hanno clear access to it.

"Pick it up. Or don't. But decide now. Garren's Chain-Men are waiting to hear whether you're my enforcer or just another orphan who got lucky once."

The threat was implicit but clear. Without Knot protection, Hanno was just the fostered son who'd attacked a commander. With it, he was an official enforcer carrying out sanctioned discipline. The badge on the floor represented the only real choice \- accept Luka's version of events or face the consequences of the truth.

Hanno's jaw set in that way that meant he was working through something difficult. His hand moved slowly, as if fighting its own weight, until his fingers closed around the brass badge.

"Smart," Luka said, and there might have been satisfaction in his voice, though his inhuman features made it hard to tell. "Mesca, spread the word. Hanno Venn has been enforcing for the Knot for three months. Today's discipline was sanctioned. Anyone who says otherwise is calling me a liar."

He turned back to Hanno, who was still holding the badge like it might burn him.

"Welcome to the Hanged Men. You work for me now. Try to remember that's better than the alternative."

He left without waiting for a response, moving with that Springheel fluidity that made him seem to pour out of the room. Mesca followed, already composing the rumors that would solidify this new history.

Jak and Hanno remained in the chapel, the badge heavy in Hanno's hand, the weight of unwanted inheritance settling over them both like the industrial dust that never quite cleared from the Middens' air.
