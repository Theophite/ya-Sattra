## **The Judgment of Vaida es-Sennor**

The Adjudicator's voice carried the flat precision of someone reading numbers from a ledger. "In the matter of the es-Sennor Maritime Notarization Patent, Case 7,341 of the Inheritance Docket, this court finds for the petitioner."

Vaida did not move. Eighteen years of procedural warfare had taught her that reactions invited appeals. She had memorized every crack in the ceiling during the preliminary hearings. She had counted the brass studs on the Adjudicator's desk during the evidentiary phase. She had been provided for. She had four hundred thirty-seven thousand obols and a patent that would generate steady income for the rest of her life. She had won.

She sat on the bench in the Third Whorl's grey light and tried to remember what victory was supposed to feel like. The Plea spoke of burdens becoming weightless, of gates opening, of labor finding its reward. She had labored. The gates had opened. The burden remained.

Vaida es-Sennor, victorious petitioner, inheritor of the maritime notarization patent, sat alone in the filtered light of the Third Whorl and understood at last what she had purchased with eighteen years of her life.

She had purchased the right to keep something her husband had not wanted her to have.

The letter had been very clear about that.
