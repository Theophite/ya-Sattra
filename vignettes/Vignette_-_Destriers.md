**Destriers**

The destriers were perfectly still when Kael approached the paddock. Thirty of them, standing in the morning light, not grazing yet. Just standing. Watching.

He'd been tending riding beasts for the Imperial relay for six years before he took this posting. Horses moved. They shifted weight, flicked ears, swished tails at flies. They were *alive* in their stillness.

Destriers didn't move. They stood like statues with their heads turned toward him, those huge black eyes tracking. The clicking started \- a rhythmic sound from thirty throats at once, like a clock mechanism. *Tick-tick-tick-tick.*

Kael had been at the waystation for three months and he still wasn't used to it.

The Akama warband had ridden in last night after pushing hard from the border. The destriers needed water, grain, and a rubdown. Normally the riders handled their own mounts \- would only let their own mounts be handled \- but two of the Akama were injured and the rest were exhausted. So Kael got volunteered.

He opened the paddock gate. The destriers didn't react. Just watched. *Tick-tick-tick.*

Up close they were worse. From a distance you could almost mistake them for deer \- gracile, long-necked, oddly proportioned but recognizable. Up close the wrongness accumulated. The hairless skin, glossy black like wet leather but textured like a fingernail. Where the carapace thinned over the joints it showed iridescence \- oil-slick purples and greens. The legs folded at angles that didn't make anatomical sense, the front limbs reaching too far forward even at rest.

The ramified horns branched and rebranched like coral, smooth and black. Beautiful, if you didn't think too hard about what you were looking at.

Kael approached the nearest one \- a big male that had carried Tsokh himself. It didn't move as he got close. Didn't shift or step back. Just watched him with those placid black eyes.

He touched its neck. Still hot from last night's run, the skin radiating warmth like a fever. Damp. After a hard run they steamed like boiled meat, moisture evaporating off them in visible clouds. This one had cooled some but still felt wrong \- too hot, too smooth, the texture of the carapace plates like touching horn.

He started scrubbing with his brush. The destrier stood perfectly still. Not tense, not relaxed. Just *still*. Its head tracked him as he moved around its flank. Always watching. The clicking continued.

Its lips were mobile and delicate like a goat's, dark and agile. Kael had seen them strip leaves from branches with those lips, gentle and precise. He'd also seen what was behind them \- rows of thin translucent teeth like glass needles, and further back, ridged grinding plates. Omnivorous. The Akama fed them grain and grass and that was fine. But if you pushed them too hard, if their metabolisms demanded it, they needed meat.

Last month a destrier had gotten loose and killed three chickens. Not for food \- it had already eaten. Just because it was there and the chickens were there and the destrier was capable. The Akama rider had been apologetic but unsurprised. "Don't let them get hungry," he'd said. "They're unpredictable when they're hungry."

Kael moved to the legs, scrubbing down the impossible joints. The front legs could reach so far forward \- he'd seen them run, if you could call it running. More like leaping, bound to bound to bound, barely touching the ground between. Like someone running in a dream, weightless and wrong. They could hit seventy miles per hour on flat ground. Nothing else moved like that.

The destrier shifted its head to watch him work on its hindquarters. He could feel it watching. The clicking changed rhythm slightly \- *tick-tick...tick-tick* \- and Kael froze.

But the destrier just stood there. Watching.

He finished the rubdown and moved to the water trough. The destrier followed with its eyes but not its body. Only when Kael stepped away did it move, and the movement was immediate and complete \- from perfect stillness to walking in one instant, no transition. It dipped its head to drink, those agile lips working the water, and Kael saw the rows of teeth gleam translucent in the morning light.

Twenty-nine more.

The Akama said they were the best mounts in the world. Fast, enduring, loyal to their rider. They followed commands extraordinarily well \- better than horses, better than anything. A destrier bonded to you and it would carry you until it died. They were perfect tools.

They were also wrong. Everything about them was wrong. The glossy carapace, the impossible legs, the carnivore teeth in an herbivore's mouth, the way they watched you with those huge placid eyes, the way they stood absolutely still and then moved all at once like someone had cut a wire.

Kael didn't know what they were engineered for. Speed, obviously. But why this way? Why the carapace, why the teeth, why the uncanny stillness? The Creche had made them before the Interdict and then stopped. Now they were wild \- not domesticated, just used. Living in herds out on the scablands, breeding, eating grass and sometimes meat, running at speeds nothing else could match.

The Akama were the only ones who really understood them. Could ride them without fear, could push them to their limits, could handle them when they got unpredictable. Everyone else just... managed. Fed them, sheltered them, stayed wary.

Kael approached the second destrier. It watched him come. *Tick-tick-tick-tick.* Perfectly still except for the head tracking his movement. He put his hand on its neck \- hot, smooth, wrong \- and started scrubbing.

Behind him, the first destrier had finished drinking. It stood by the trough, head turned toward him. Watching. They were all watching.

He had twenty-eight more to tend and then he could go back inside where things moved like they were supposed to move and he could stop feeling those huge black eyes tracking him and stop hearing that clicking and stop touching skin that felt like fever and horn.

The destrier under his hands stood perfectly still. Its carapace caught the light, iridescent purple where it thinned. Its lips worked slightly, exposing just the tips of those translucent teeth.

Kael kept scrubbing. The clicking continued. The watching continued.

Somewhere out on the scablands, wild destrier herds were running, barely touching the ground, moving like dreams. And Kael was very glad they were out there and not here.

He finished the second destrier. Twenty-seven more to go.

*Tick-tick-tick-tick.*
