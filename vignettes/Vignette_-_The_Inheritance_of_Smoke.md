**The Inheritance of Smoke**

Outside, a shift whistle screamed. Four thousand workers changing posts, all members of the Furnace Union, all wearing Penitent symbols welded to their helmets. The es-Kostrel family controlled the coolant systems for a third of ya-Don's industrial base, but control was a flexible concept when your workforce genuflected to a different authority.

Tell me the policy for when the Thermal Works' primary heat exchanger fails and we need Bureau approval for repairs that we designed, that we've been maintaining for six generations, that we understand better than any bureaucrat who's never touched a wrench."

Revan had heard this story. He'd heard all his father's stories about Bureau interference, usually after negotiations with the unions went badly and Davrem needed someone else to blame.

Revan wanted to argue, but he'd seen it happen. Jonat es-Peller, another Rod heir who'd joined the Bureau a generation ago. He now sent formal inspection notices to his own family's chemical plants, relationships so strained he wasn't invited to clan gatherings.

"Tessit wants the succession," Revan said finally. "She's always wanted it."

"Tessit wants to build things. She'd be happiest in a workshop with a welding torch and nobody bothering her for progress reports. But she'll take succession because she's practical, and someone has to." Davrem's voice softened slightly. "She's also smart enough to know she'll need support. Bureau connections. "So you want me to join the Bureau to help her?"

We create reality. Your test scores mean you could excel at either. But only one of them will let you come home without filing paperwork first."

Revan looked out at the industrial sprawl his family had controlled for two centuries. Workers were already gathering for the evening Penitent service in the courtyard chapel, their devotion as much a part of operations as the machinery. "I could serve three years," Revan offered. "Establish connections, learn the bureaucratic structures, then returnâ€”"

The shift whistle screamed again. Emergency stop. Somewhere in the Works, something had broken. Tessit would already be running toward it, toolkit in hand. Bureau inspectors would arrive tomorrow, demanding reports. The unions would want assurances about worker safety. The dance would continue.

"I have a week to respond to the Bureau's offer," Revan said.

"You have a lifetime to regret the wrong choice," his father replied, already turning to his desk, pulling up diagnostic reports. The conversation was over. The Thermal Works needed attention. Reality, as always, took precedence over policy.

Revan left his father's office, walking through halls lined with portraits of es-Kostrel patriarchs and matriarchs who'd all faced the same choice. Most had stayed. A few had left for the Bureau. Only the ones who stayed had their names on the equipment outside.

He found Tessit in the courtyard, arguing with a union priest about overtime rates for emergency repairs. She had their grandmother's gift for making technical necessity sound like moral imperative. She'd be good at this, maybe better than him.

But she'd need Bureau connections. Someone who could navigate the inspections, translate between industrial reality and bureaucratic requirement.

Three years, Revan thought. Enough time to build connections, not enough to lose touch with industrial reality. His father would hate it. Tessit would use it.

The es-Kostrel Thermal Works would continue either way, breathing smoke into ya-Don's sky, converting heat into power into wealth into the influence that kept a Rod Aureate family relevant in a city that belonged as much to the unions and the Church as it did to the Empire.

The choice would define the next century of family operations.

No pressure at all.
