# **Tea at Ridge-Line Scriptoria**

The room's walls were lined with filing cabinets containing decades of notarized documents, creating an atmosphere that was part office, part archive, part comfortable receiving room for those who understood that information required proper curation.

He'd been coming here for these afternoon meetings since taking over his inspection route three years ago, and the ritual had settled into something comfortable. "Of course. We're neighbors, after all."

Nativa Galen entered from the adjoining room, carrying a leather portfolio under one arm. She was thinner than her husband, her hair gone mostly gray, and she moved with the focused attention of someone who spent her days managing cryptographic keys and reading other people's correspondence. She poured herself tea without interrupting the conversation, then sat in the third chair with her portfolio balanced on her knees.

"Inspector," she said by way of greeting.

"Contractor Galen," Qirren replied.

Everyone in the Rotten Quarter who needed to send secure communications eventually came to Nativa. She provided the cryptographic signatures, managed the keys, ensured the messages stayed private from everyone except the Lensâ€”and herself. It was a necessary fiction that she didn't read everything she encrypted, but everyone knew she did. That was the price of secure communication in a district where trust was a commodity more precious than salvaged archaeotech.

"I heard there was some excitement at Dry-Dock 3 last week," Leran said, his tone carefully casual. "Something about a hull section that couldn't pass decontamination?"

Qirren considered his tea for a moment, then nodded. "A salvage crew from Hollow-Egress brought up what they claimed was structural plating from a pre-Interdict orbital platform. It was glowing faintly, and when we got the dosimeters on it, the readings were enough that we had to call in a full quarantine."

"Was it actually from orbit?" Nativa asked, looking up from her portfolio.

"Unknown. The metallurgy was consistent with orbital construction, but so is a lot of archaeotech. The Salvage Guild wanted to auction it as premium grade, but Master Assayer Marst agreed with our assessment once she saw the numbers."

"Agreed publicly or privately?" Leran's tone remained light, but Qirren noted the question's precision.

"She filed the appropriate objection within the hour. The Lens released the hull to controlled storage, and the salvage crew received compensation for their trouble at reduced rate."

"How reduced?"

"Forty percent of estimated value if it had been clean."

Leran made a small note on a pad that had appeared in his hand. Not the details of the incidentâ€”those would already be in the reportsâ€”but something about the disposition or perhaps the response time. Qirren didn't mind. This kind of information helped people make informed decisions about what salvage was worth pursuing.

"The Hollow-Egress crews were not pleased," Nativa observed. "Their coordinating messages became quite colorful after the compensation announcement."

She said this with the mild tone of someone commenting on the weather, but Qirren understood what she was actually saying. The salvage crew had been using her encryption services to coordinate their operation, and she'd read their private complaints about the Lens's valuation. That was the bargainâ€”security from external observation in exchange for her knowing everything.

"I imagine they were disappointed," Qirren said neutrally.

"They understood the situation eventually. No one wants to be handling orbital waste without proper containment." Nativa paused, considering her tea. "Though there's been interesting traffic about deep salvage operations. More coordination between crews than usual, more careful location-sharing. People are being cautious about where they're diving."

"There's been a shift," Qirren confirmed. "The accessible salvage in the Rift's shallower zones is mostly exhausted, so they're going deeper. Deeper means older facilities, which means more exposure risk."

"The Grey Sheet mentioned something about revised safety protocols," Leran said, which was his way of noting that the Lens had flagged this as a topic of interest. "Master Assayer Marst issued new guidelines two weeks ago," Qirren said. "All salvage from below fifty fathoms now requires preliminary screening before it comes up to the docks. The Guild's not happy about the additional costs, but they're less unhappy than they would be about a major contamination incident."

"Will the guidelines be enforced?"

"The Guild will enforce them on documented crews. Undocumented crews will do what they always do."

Leran made another note. This would likely appear in next week's snippet salesâ€”carefully worded excerpts from the Grey Sheet combined with additional context from Qirren's field observations. The Lens wrote the Grey Sheet knowing contractors would sell pieces of it to interested parties. It was how the Bureaus communicated with district powers without official statements, how intelligence flowed through back channels that everyone pretended didn't exist.

"Some of the crew coordinators think the new guidelines are just the Guild extracting fees," Nativa said. "Their encrypted discussions suggest they're planning to work around the requirements."

This was her delicate way of saying she'd read planning discussions between salvage crews coordinating illegal operations. Unless they'd paid for discretionâ€”and clearly they hadn'tâ€”she was free to sell that intelligence to whoever found it useful. The Lens would get a report because deep salvage was a Grey Sheet topic, but others might find it valuable too.

"They'd be making a mistake," Qirren said. "I've seen the quarantine numbers. This isn't bureaucratic expansion, it's practical necessity."

"I'll make sure that perspective is available to interested parties," Leran said, which meant the next Grey Sheet snippets would include warnings about the genuine dangers of deep salvage alongside whatever else the Lens had flagged as important.

They drank their tea in companionable silence for a moment. Outside, the sounds of the Arcades filtered upâ€”the distant hammering of workshops, the occasional shout of someone navigating the angled corridors, the perpetual background noise of industry and habitation packed into spaces never designed for either.

"The Pipefitters are negotiating new rates with the Guild," Nativa said. "Their internal communications suggest they're coordinating something beyond the usual contract renewal."

"Foreman Glen presented a proposal for additional hazard compensation," Qirren confirmed. "The Guild countered. They'll probably settle somewhere in the middle."

"Before or after the next auction?"

"Before, if Glen has his way. The Pipefitters can make the Guild's life difficult if they choose to."

"How difficult?"

Qirren smiled slightly. "Let's say Master Assayer Marst is motivated to reach an accommodation. The Lens prefers that district labor disputes resolve themselves without requiring Imperial intervention."

"The Grey Sheet mentioned labor stability as a priority," Leran observed, which meant the Lens wanted contractors paying attention to anything that might disrupt the district's industrial operations. "Anything we should be watching for?"

"Glen's smart. He'll push until he gets a reasonable settlement, then he'll make sure everyone understands the agreement serves mutual interests. If I had to guess, the announcement comes sometime in the next week."

Leran made a note. That information would be worth something to merchants timing their salvage purchases, to smaller operations trying to estimate their own labor costs, to anyone whose business depended on understanding the district's power dynamics.

"Your mother mentioned the Val Waterworks might be expanding service to some of the Ridgeback Shelters," Leran said, changing subjects. "Is that moving forward?"

"She's negotiating with three tenement associations. The infrastructure is thereâ€”old standpipes that were sealed during the collapse but are still viable. The question is whether the residents can sustain the service fees."

"The Galens would be happy to assist with documentation if the expansion proceeds. Notarization, service contracts, that sort of thing."

"I'll mention it to her." Qirren paused. "Though she might appreciate discretion during the negotiations. The tenement associations are sensitive about outside involvement."

"Of course. Though if they're coordinating their negotiating positions through encrypted channels, I suspect they're being practical rather than sensitive."

Which was Nativa's way of saying she'd already read the tenement associations' internal discussions and knew exactly what leverage points they were planning to use. Whether the Vals got that information depended on whether they found it worth purchasing, but Qirren appreciated the notice that it was available.

"One more thing," Nativa said, opening her portfolio and extracting a single sheet. "This week's Grey Sheet included a mention of increased administrative attention to the Rotten Quarter. This was more sensitive. The Grey Sheet was technically classified intelligence that Nativa received as part of her contractor relationship with the Lens. Qirren read the excerpt carefully. The language was bureaucratic, but the implications were clear enough. Someone in the First Whorl or the Bureaus was considering changes to how the Rotten Quarter was managed.

"Have you seen anything in your inspection work that might explain this?" Leran asked.

"Nothing specific. The district operates the way it always hasâ€”legally ambiguous but functionally stable. If someone's planning changes, it's not based on conditions in the field."

"Politics, then," Nativa said.

"Most likely."

She took the excerpt back and returned it to her portfolio. "I thought the Vals might benefit from knowing this was being discussed. If such changes materialize, early preparation would be valuable."

"The Vals appreciate the Galens' consideration," Qirren said formally.

"Of course. We're neighbors."

Leran stood, signaling the end of the meeting. "We appreciate your time, Inspector. As always, the Galens value our relationship with the Vals."

"The feeling is mutual," Qirren said, rising. "I'll see myself out."

He descended the narrow stairs back to the scriptoria's main floor, where Tirin Galen was setting type for what looked like a batch of White Sheets. The licensed reprints were the family's most visible businessâ€”boring, public news that anyone could read, technically classified but functionally available to anyone who wanted it. The real money was in the Grey Sheet snippets that Leran sold to those who understood the value of advance intelligence, and in whatever else Nativa learned from reading everyone's encrypted correspondence.

Through the front window, Horizon Row stretched in both directions, its cobblestones clean and its shops maintaining their careful propriety. Behind him, the Galens were already discussing what they'd learned and how to use it. Leran would be composing next week's snippet offerings, carefully excerpting Grey Sheet material and combining it with Qirren's field observations. Nativa would be considering which encrypted communications were worth selling and which she'd been paid to keep quiet about. Sera would hear about the Pipefitters' negotiations through family channels, and the Hanged Men would adjust their own strategies accordingly.

This was how the Ridge worked. Information flowed through channels that shouldn't officially exist, was processed by families who understood its value, and was distributed to those who needed it in forms they could use. The Imperial bureaucracy's classification system had decayed into tiered news services where access was about price and connection rather than security clearance. The Lens knew this happened and tolerated it because preventing it would require admitting they couldn't secure their own intelligence distribution.

Qirren turned south, heading back toward the Iron Yards where his inspection route continued. Somewhere in Dry-Dock 3, another salvage crew was probably pulling up another questionable hull section, and he would need to make another judgment about what was dangerous enough to quarantine and what could be allowed to pass through into the district's hungry markets.

The tea had been good, though. He'd have to remember to bring some of his mother's honeycake next time. The Galens appreciated proper reciprocity.
