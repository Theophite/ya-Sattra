**The Plasma Garden**

Colonel-Hereditary Tam es-Norrel stood at the edge of his inheritance, watching the shimmer of heat distortion where reality grew soft. Seventeen plasma lances stood in their cradles, each one twenty meters of crystallized mathematics and hostile physics, arranged in the same ritual arc his great-grandmother had maintained, and her grandfather before her. Beyond them, the scrublands stretched toward the Thousand Kingdoms, where cookfires marked the latest warband to test his borders.

"They're moving again," his adjutant reported. Unlike es-Norrel, who wore his dress uniform despite the isolation, Lieutenant Cray dressed practicallyâ€”leather and ceramic, sweat-stained from checking the perimeter. The distinction mattered. Es-Norrel was Aureate. Cray was just competent.

"Let them come closer." Es-Norrel touched the command baton at his hip, its authentication circuits recognizing his gene-pattern with a soft chime. Only he could wake the lances. Only he could aim them. Only he could discover if they still functioned after six decades of silence.

The last time his mother had fired one, it had killed her. Not immediatelyâ€”the lance had performed perfectly, carving a molten canyon through an entire Akama cavalry charge. But the radiological afterburn had found something wrong in the weapon's containment. She'd lived another three months, long enough to pass him the command codes while her bones dissolved from the inside.

"Sir, they're entering the outer markers."

The warband had maybe sixty riders, typical Thousand Kingdoms probe. They'd test his conventional defenses, count his garrison's twenty soldiers, estimate his weakness. They didn't know about the lances because nobody remembered what they could do anymore. The weapons had been too effectiveâ€”any force large enough to warrant their use had learned to simply go around the es-Norrel holdings.

Es-Norrel climbed the observation platform his family had built into the lance array. From here, he could see the targeting solutions carved into stoneâ€”pre-calculated firing patterns for every approach, updated by six generations of es-Norrels who'd never had cause to use them. The mathematics were beautiful, even if he only understood half the equations. The Seven-Cornered Examination had given him enough education to operate the weapons, not comprehend them.

"Plasma Garden, this is Watchtower," the radio crackled. "Raiders have crossed the third marker. Garrison requests permission to engage."

He could send his twenty soldiers against sixty raiders. He'd lose three, maybe four. The raiders would retreat, declare victory, return in greater numbers next season. The slow erosion would continue.

Or he could wake the lances.

The command baton warmed in his hand, authentication circuits cycling through deeper verification. The lances began to hum, a sound felt in bone rather than heard. Crystalline structures that had seemed dead began to glow from within, plasma seeds germinating in magnetic bottles. The heat distortion spread, turning the air above the weapons into a shimmer of potential violence.

"Sir," Cray said quietly, "the containment readingsâ€”"

"Are within acceptable parameters." They weren't. Three lances showed fluctuations that might be nothing or might be what killed his mother. But acceptable was a flexible concept when you were the only person who could define it.

The raiders had stopped, their horses refusing to approach closer. They could feel something wrong in the air, some predator instinct warning them away from the garden where physics grew hostile. Their leader raised a rifle, an old coilgun probably salvaged from some Imperial depot, and fired a token shot that fell a hundred meters short.

Es-Norrel began the targeting sequence. The lances tracked in perfect unison, following his baton's movements like flowers following the sun. The mathematics carved in stone aligned with the weapons' internal calculations, centuries of preparation crystallizing into a single moment of potential.

He could glass the entire warband. Turn them into radioactive ash that would poison the ground for decades. Remind the Thousand Kingdoms why the es-Norrel holdings had stood inviolate for three centuries.

And then what? Wait another sixty years for the next excuse? Die like his mother, cooked from within by weapons he owned but couldn't repair? Continue this vigil over technology that killed its wielders as often as its targets?

The raiders were already retreating, horses wheeling away from the wrongness in the air. They'd return, eventually. They always did. But not today.

Es-Norrel lowered the baton. The lances powered down with a descending whine, plasma seeds dissolving back into potential. The heat distortion faded. Reality solidified.

"Sir?" Cray asked.

"Mark it in the log," es-Norrel said. "Successful deterrence. No ammunition expended."

He climbed down from the platform, joints aching from tension he hadn't noticed. Tomorrow he'd run the maintenance protocols again, check the containment readings, update the targeting mathematics. He'd send another letter to the Satara requesting reinforcements that would never come, petition for repairs that no one could perform.

The plasma garden would continue its vigil, beautiful and poisonous, too dangerous to use and too valuable to abandon. Just like the es-Norrel family itselfâ€”hereditary guardians of a frontier the Empire had already written off, wielding weapons that killed their owners, defending borders that existed mostly in their own minds.

Tam es-Norrel walked back to his estate, leaving sixteen soldiers to watch the horizon and four to carry out the ones who'd stood too close to the lances during activation. Their noses were already bleeding. By morning, they'd need replacement.

He'd request more from the garrison at Yarangar. They'd send him whoever they wanted to be rid of.

The cycle would continue.
