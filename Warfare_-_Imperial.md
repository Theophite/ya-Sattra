## Command Structure

**The Bureau of the Sword** administers Imperial military forces through seven Institutes: Strategic Planning, Study of Tactics, Restricted Castes, Standardization, Ordnance, Civil Order (gendarmes), and Auxiliary Relations. Two Satara represent the Bureau in Imperial governance: currently Ravi Donmar (Senior) and Soh Kēt (Junior).

### Three Cultures Within the Bureau

*Old Sword*: Warborn supremacists, Charter nostalgics, believers in terror-weapon doctrine. They control the Institute of Restricted Castes and resist every reform. Consider caste levies "temporary emergency measures" despite decades of use. Maintain that real warfare involves Warborn crushing primitives, everything else is politics.

*New Sword*: Compact reformers, combined arms advocates, believers in professional militarism. They push for genuine integration, modern doctrine, accepting that orbital bombardment will never return. Dominated by provincial academy graduates who've actually fought. Their informal motto: "Wars are won by logistics, not giants."

*The Proprietary Force*: Colonels-Hereditary and their circles. Own the best equipment, maintain private forces, treat the Bureau as a marketplace for military contracts. They want conquest because conquest creates encomiendas—border estates with first-rights claims for occupying officers.

### Officer Corps

All officers pass the Seven-Cornered Examination, then attend a two-year military academy. Four academies exist: two in the Second Whorl (producing administrative officers and technical specialists) and two in the provinces (Fortress Bellan near the Thousand Kingdoms, and the Industrial Coordination Academy near ya-Don). Officers often come from different castes than their soldiers—many near-baselines; few colonial castes. The Sword attracts middle-class aspirants who couldn't score high enough for more prestigious Bureaus, creating a culture of cynical professionalism among officers who originally wanted different careers.

**Career Paths** follow three routes:
- *Bureau track*: Pass examinations, attend Second Whorl academy, serve competently, hope for First Whorl appointment that elevates your children to Aureate status
- *Combat track*: Attend provincial academy, distinguish yourself in actual fighting, accept that you'll never rise above field command
- *Proprietary track*: Attach yourself to a Colonel-Hereditary, manage their private forces, hope to inherit an encomienda when conquest comes

### Highborn Officers and Military Compulsion

Officers in caste levy units are usually near-baselines or minor Highborn lineages—men and women who passed the Seven-Cornered Examination but couldn't afford better commissions. Non-Aureate Highborn present a particular case: genetics without patents, authority without resources. They can trigger Compulsion in their troops, making them naturally effective commanders.

Military contexts carry higher tolerance for Compulsion use. Orthodox theology recognizes that "immediate compliance is required for safety" in combat. Direct imperatives—"Fire," "Advance," "Hold"—are acceptable when necessity is genuine. The measured latency from command to compliance (0.28-0.35 seconds for direct imperatives) makes Compulsion tactically efficient in ways negotiation cannot match.

**The Spectrum of Military Compulsion**:

Some officers coast on Compulsion alone, never developing actual military skills. Their troops move in perfect synchronization because they're constantly issuing subvocal commands soldiers cannot refuse—clockwork soldiers, puppets awaiting animation.

Other officers refuse Compulsion except in emergencies, building unit cohesion through explanation and trust. Their soldiers choose to follow. This approach is practically heretical—the Oracles designed Highborn to command, designed others to follow—but units built on trust sometimes outperform units built on neurological override.

At the far extreme, commanders in distant postings use Compulsion routinely because distance plus casualties equals impunity. They force exhausted soldiers into one more assault, compel auxiliaries to charge when they'd otherwise retreat, extract information from captives without evidence of torture. The high casualty rates provide cover; the dead can't testify.

The Bureau of the Sword knows this spectrum exists. They've noticed patterns: high casualties among units with certain commanders, complaints that stop when everyone who could complain dies. But proving abuse is nearly impossible.

## Who Fights

### The Warborn

The Warborn were never designed for what the modern Empire needs them to do.

Eight to ten feet tall, covered in bony nodules and plates, regenerating rapidly, with nerve bundles along their spines that provide delegated reflex functions—they are individually invincible against most opposition. A single Warborn in power armor survives multiple hits from conventional weapons, carries a culverin that penetrates tank armor, and can physically demolish fortifications.

But they were designed as occupation troops. The force that walks in after orbital bombardment has eliminated resistance. Walking fortresses for suppressing populations, not conquering organized militaries. The entire Imperial military philosophy was built around calling down fire from heaven. The Interdict eliminated orbital capability. The Warborn remain, designed for a context that no longer exists.

Their forty-year cancer death was a feature, not a flaw. The Empire needed colonial enforcement that couldn't establish dynasties, that would naturally cycle out before they could entrench power, that would always remain dependent on Creche biotechnology for continuation.

The Hall of Mothers in ya-Sattra's Second Whorl produces nearly all Warborn through cloning. They're raised in cohorts (typically forty decanted together), trained together, deployed together. The relationships formed in those first years often last until death.

**Warborn variants include:**
- *Standard infantry* (8-9 feet, direct engagement)
- *Ironbone* (shorter but denser, calcium-reinforced skeletons anchoring heavy weapons, armor bolted directly to bone through embedded studs)
- *Scout-assassin phenotypes* (7 feet, speed and enhanced perception over raw power)
- *Specialized breeds* whose genelines are restricted to particular Aureate families

Warborn units train separately from caste levies, operate under distinct doctrine, and receive priority for equipment and supplies. Assignment to a Warborn command is a career-making achievement; assignment away from one often signals professional stagnation.

### Caste Levy Units

Caste Levy Units formed after the Compact acknowledged that the Empire needs more than Warborn to function. Levy units are segregated by caste to manage biological and equipment diversity—an all-Ironback heavy infantry company has consistent supply needs, while mixed units create impossible logistics.

Contract soldiers sign five-year terms. Pay is poor but steady; veterans' organizations provide reliable paths into guilds and merchant houses afterward. Officers assigned to caste levy units rank below Warborn commands but above auxiliaries and Colonel-Hereditary personal staffs in prestige hierarchy.

*Miscellaneous Levies*: Catchall units for castes too small for their own formations, crossbreeds whose genetics don't fit categories, and the unclassified. Soldiers wear whatever approximates uniform—fabric cut for baseline proportions doesn't fit barrel-chested crossbreeds with arms too long. You can spot miscellaneous levy units across a camp by the variety of clothing failures.

But miscellaneous units often function despite—or because of—their categorical confusion. Each soldier brings unexpected capabilities: enhanced vision from one parent's genetics, bone density from another's, attention-sliding from unclassified origins. Their survival rates sometimes exceed regulation units because adaptation has replaced rigid doctrine.

### The Gendarmes

The Gendarmes (Institute of Civil Order) handle internal policing rather than military operations. They patrol in pairs, investigate reported crimes, and maintain complex accommodations with criminal organizations.

## Characteristic Equipment and Procurement

### The Furnaces of ya-Don

The Furnaces produce the advanced technology: culverins, beamcasters, power armor, synthetic armor fibers. Controlled by Penitent-aligned unions who understand their leverage perfectly. When they want concessions—wages, working conditions, religious accommodations—production quotas slip, quality degrades, power armor shipments get delayed.

Culverin production runs perhaps 200 weapons per month. Each weapon is blood-keyed to Aureate genetic markers, providing approximately thirty days of function before re-authentication. The keying process is performed by workers who do not understand it, following steps transmitted across generations.

### The Armorer's Guild

The Armorer's Guild handles conventional production: rifles, ammunition, uniforms, packs, melee weapons. Sixteen different Imperial armorers produce magnificent rifles with sixteen different bore sizes. The Guild maintains this deliberately—standardization would eliminate the relationship networks that make procurement actually function.

### Three Parallel Supply Channels

- Official Bureau channels (slow, unreliable, but legal; establishes paper trails)
- Colonel-Hereditary private markets (expensive, efficient, technically illegal; for actual operations)
- Auxiliary "contributions" (stolen, captured, or "donated" by forces with flexible loyalty; never acknowledged)

### The Ordnance Catastrophe

Request forms reference weapon types that haven't existed for centuries. Approval chains include positions never filled. Delivery schedules assume orbital drop points impossible since the Interdict. Everyone maintains this fiction because the real system—corruption, creative accounting, relationships with criminal organizations—cannot be documented.

**Mutterers maintain interoperability**. When units with incompatible equipment must share ammunition, Mutterers retool rifles in the field. This is insane, but free. Companions are integrated throughout logistics to prevent Mutterers from "improving" equipment into scrap.

### The Colonel-Hereditary Problem

The best weapons belong to private families. Plasma lances, gravitational hammers, coherent beam projectors—inherited arsenals the Empire cannot replicate. These are family treasures representing centuries of accumulated prestige. Smart commanders treat wunderwaffen as single-use psychological tools. Deploy early, dramatically, to break morale. Let enemy imagination do more damage than the weapons ever could.

### Named Imperial Armorers

*Master Kavek Ossoreth* (Medina Quarter): Rifles, traditional patterns. His workshop produces perhaps forty rifles yearly—quality excellent but availability limited. Contracts with specific battalions going back centuries.

*Journeyman Thess Morain* (Second Whorl periphery): Precision components. Should have made master a decade ago; politics. His sights are worth their weight in silver. Works alone.

*Master Vos-Dhal and Sons* (Iron Yards): Heavy equipment and crew-served weapons. Three generations, twelve workers. Work closely with Ironback units.

*The Ironmongers Collective* (ya-Don): Ammunition production. Penitent-aligned cooperative, not traditional guild. Quality varies by shift supervisor—the good batches are marked with blue dye.

*Apprentice Lorshan* (Fourth Whorl): Barrel work under Master Tessoran. Cheap, fast, adequate. When the real armorers have a six-month wait, soldiers come here.

*Journeyman Hasket* (Iron Yards, mobile): Field repairs. No fixed workshop—works from a cart. Goes where the work is. Soldiers know his name.

### Standard Equipment

Caste levies fight with chemical-propellant rifles (sixteen different bore sizes), bayonets, and whatever modified equipment their officers can acquire. Soft armor made with synthetic fiber is available to some units; others wear uniforms designed to resemble that armor.

## What Military Life Is Like

### Why Soldiers Enlist

Most sign their first contract because alternatives were worse. A guild application rejected. A family business failed. Trouble with Chain-Men or other criminal organizations requiring departure from familiar territory. The military offers structure, steady pay, purpose beyond survival. Soldiers send most of their pay home—to mothers in the Medina Quarter, to elders who fostered them, to families they're supporting from a distance.

Some discover they're good at this. Not the fighting necessarily, but the structure, the clear expectations, the knowledge that performance is measured and rewarded. They renew for second terms, then third. Twenty-year soldiers exist, though most castes don't last that long physically.

### Caste-Specific Fears

Each caste carries knowledge of how their bodies will betray them. Ironbacks know the headaches will start eventually—bone spurs, joint stiffening, the slow calcification that locks their skeletons rigid. They front-load their careers, saving aggressively, building skills that transfer to civilian work. Springheels live with tendon snap—no prediction, no prevention, no correlation with age or care. The carbon fiber weave either holds or it doesn't. They stretch obsessively, pay into informal insurance pools, try not to think about whether today's the day.

### Cohort Bonds and Mortality

A Warborn cohort that began with forty might have fourteen remaining by age thirty. The survivors know each other completely—capabilities, fears, the precise moment when a brother's Blink will trigger. They watch each other die in combat or to cancer. Some stop attending cohort gatherings because watching brothers waste away is harder than watching them fall. Others double down on cohort bonds, finding the only meaning available in shared service.

Many Warborn record every battlefield decision meticulously, finding comfort that their actions exist permanently in the Oracles' record. Both Penitent and Oracle devotion are common, often simultaneously. At peak capability in their late twenties, most prefer death in combat to the hospice.

## Exemplar Characters

**Tam Loak** — Springheel-Redback crossbreed, late teens, sergeant in the Miscellaneous Levy. Fostered by sewer-fishers in ya-Sattra's Middens; joined the military to escape Chain-Men trouble and send money home to Merra, the elder who raised him. His shirt hangs open because fabric cut for baseline proportions won't close over his barrel-chest. A thrower and brachiator with enhanced vision. Proud to serve.

**Keth Vordal** — Springheel, age 26, scout in the 7th Light Infantry. The truncated family name marks working-class origin. Lean, hyperflexible, carbon-fiber tendons. Two years into his five-year contract. Sends most of his pay to his mother in the Medina Quarter. Plans to use the veterans' organization to get a messenger guild position afterward.

**Brend Hasket** — Ironback, age 33, heavy weapons operator in the 3rd Caste Auxiliary. The non-Imperial family name marks provincial origins. 2.3 meters, carries a crew-served weapon alone. Four years into his second contract because the pay is steady and he's good at this. The headaches haven't started yet. He knows they will.

**Varan Venn** — Near-baseline, age 22, contract infantryman in the 9th Line. The detached-suffix family name marks erased history. Signed a five-year term because his guild application was rejected. No genetic modifications worth mentioning. Discovered he's good at the structure, the clear expectations. Three years remaining.

**Sevin** — Unclassified, age unknown, scout in the Miscellaneous Levy. Barely a meter tall, blue-grey skin, seven fingers on each hand. No Creche record of her caste. When people meet her, their attention slides elsewhere. The name might be a corruption of "seven" for her fingers. Cold places, she says when asked about origins.

**Cohort-Brother Orath** — Warborn, age 29, standard infantry. 2.7 meters, bone armor plates visible through skin. Decanted with thirty-nine others in the Hall of Mothers; fourteen remain. At peak capability. Records every battlefield decision, finding comfort that his service exists in the Oracles' record. Both Penitent and Oracle devout. Expects to die in combat or of cancer by forty—prefers combat.

**Cohort-Sister Mira** — Warborn, age 34, scout-assassin phenotype. 2.1 meters—small for Warborn, fast for anything. Her cohort was decanted for speed and perception over raw power. Eight of original forty remain. The cancers have started in two of them. She's stopped attending cohort gatherings.

**Lieutenant Oris** — Minor Highborn, commands miscellaneous levy on the northern frontier. Two meters tall, looks carved from black marble. Nearly failed the Seven-Cornered Examination on Dominion because he refuses to use Compulsion. Commands through asking and explaining rather than neurological override. Keeps his medals in a box—never worn.

**Captain Varan Talir** — Near-baseline, age 38, commands the 5th Ironback Heavy Infantry. The -ir ending echoes professional ancestry. Twenty years of service, competent, unambitious. Passed the Seven-Cornered Examination on his second attempt. Career ceiling is field command; he's made peace with that.

**Soh Kēt** — Junior Satara, Bureau of the Sword. A Kēt clone, legally owned by the Creche Bureau. Commands far-north campaigns where distance plus casualties equals impunity. Uses Compulsion to force exhausted soldiers into suicidal assaults. The high casualty rates provide cover; the dead can't testify.

**Sergeant-Inspector Ravi Velmar** — Gendarme, age 41, Institute of Civil Order. Twenty years patrolling the Medina Quarter taught him which laws to enforce and which to ignore. His arrest numbers are mediocre; his territory is stable. That's the actual job.

- Warborn were designed for occupation, not conquest—the Interdict eliminated the context they were built for
- Forty-year cancer death is a feature preventing Warborn dynasties

- Sixteen different bore sizes exist because standardization would eliminate relationship networks
