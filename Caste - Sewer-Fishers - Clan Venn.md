# Clan Venn: The High-Rigging Daredevils

> **Location:** Distributed platforms in upper Middens structures; ropeworks in Iron Yards
> **Operation:** High-altitude rigging, cable work, toll-avoidance infrastructure
> **Products:** Cables from surgical sutures to dock lines
> **Specialty:** Building unauthorized crossings that appear improvised but are engineered

The Venns see the city in three dimensions while others remain psychologically ground-bound.

## Important Members

**Patriarch Eldin Venn** (Age 64): Senior rigger with forty years of high-altitude experience. Missing two fingers from his left hand from cable snaps, but maintains superior grip strength. Known for calculated risk assessment and even temper rather than recklessness. Manages relationships with Iron Yards authorities.

**Forewoman Ressa Venn** (Age 28): Young for her position but earned respect through technical skill. Performs installations others refuse—bridge cables during storms, emergency repairs on occupied structures. Her small frame and exceptional flexibility allow access to anchor points others cannot reach.

**Keras Venn** (Age 45): Master ropemaker overseeing the Middens Ropeworks. Proud of his work. Produces cables that exceed formal engineering specifications. Maintains the clan's technical library of pre-Interdict rigging manuals. Teaches the children.

**Tam Venn-Cho** (Age 31): Product of intermarriage with the Chos. Manages water-environment rigging—underwater cable runs, sewer crossings, flood-zone installations. His strongly webbed hands, bad for tying knots, provide unusual amphibious capabilities.

**Hanno Venn** (Age 20, Fen fosterling): Caught in a Venn safety net as an infant when Garren threw the newborn and his mother out of the top of Bright-Spire. Has been a Venn his entire life and treated as family. Unlike other Venns, he's a caste-registered stevedore and—despite difficulty learning to read and figure—has secured a Salvage Guild apprenticeship.

**"Little" Bren Venn** (Age 52): Despite his nickname, stands average height for a sewer-fisher. Designs toll-avoidance infrastructure, calculating stress loads for unofficial bridges and zip lines. Maintains mental maps of every unauthorized crossing in the district.

**Sara Venn** (Age 16): Shows exceptional spatial mathematics even by sewer-fisher standards. Being groomed to shadow the Forewoman. Her ability to visualize three-dimensional stress patterns surpasses even Eldin's.

**Goss Venn** (Age 22, Beamcrawler Fostering): Not genetically sewer-fisher but a springheel variant with arms reaching nearly to his ankles. Invaluable for stringing initial guide-lines across impossible gaps.

**Dek and Rol Venn** (Ages 19 and 17, Near-Baseline Fosterlings): Twin brothers from agricultural districts, taken in after parents died in a granary collapse. Handle ground-level operations and customer interactions requiring baseline appearance.

## Family Business and Relationships

The Venns operate from two primary locations: the Iron Yards ropeworks and a network of storage caches throughout the Middens' upper structures. The ropeworks produces everything from delicate surgical sutures to massive dock cables, each product incorporating mathematical patterns that optimize strength beyond what materials alone would suggest.

Their toll-avoidance infrastructure represents careful political calculation. Every unofficial crossing reduces Hanged Men revenue, but the Venns ensure losses remain below the threshold triggering violent response. They maintain approximately thirty unauthorized routes—enough to provide alternatives but not enough to constitute open rebellion. The routes demonstrate sophisticated engineering: self-adjusting tension systems accommodating weather changes, breakaway anchors preventing catastrophic failure, redundant safety lines hidden from casual observation.

The relationship with the Loaks involves critical reciprocity. Venn cables keep Lift-3 operational, while Loaks provide priority lifting for Venn materials. Both clans understand neither could function without the other.

Competition with "official" rigging companies remains minimal because Venns operate in spaces others won't enter. Insurance excludes sewer-fisher work, formal companies won't touch illegal modifications, and most engineers lack the mathematical intuition to recognize optimal anchor points.

## Living Situation and Idiosyncrasies

The Venns don't occupy a single structure but maintain distributed presence throughout the Middens' vertical spaces. Their primary residence consists of interconnected platforms built into support structures of three adjacent buildings, connected by bridges only they fully trust. These platforms, constructed from salvaged materials and Venn-woven cables, sway with wind but never collapse—the clan's mathematical intuitions account for resonance patterns that killed previous high-dwellers.

Living spaces reflect adaptation to height. Furniture anchors to walls. Possessions store in secured containers. Children learn to sleep through building sway that would nauseate ground-dwellers. The constant wind creates a sonic environment of whistles and moans that clan members interpret as weather information.

The clan maintains emergency caches throughout the district—coiled cables, anchoring equipment, emergency food supplies sealed against moisture. Only clan members know all locations, information passed verbally to prevent documentation.

Education focuses on three-dimensional thinking. Children build string figures brutally evaluated by elders. This is not cruelty: to work at height means lives depend on your work. They learn to visualize stress patterns, understand material fatigue, recognize the sound of cable approaching failure.

Safety practices mix technical knowledge with superstition. Every rigger carries a "luck carabiner"—a carabiner connected to a line, connected to nothing, which elders "joke" will catch on skyhooks if you fall. They maintain numerical taboos about certain installation patterns, avoiding configurations that feel mathematically unstable even if engineering suggests they're sound.

The family shrine includes unusual elements: etchings and descriptions of every event killing a family member due to misfortune, serving as technical education and memorial. Children study these failures, learning to recognize subtle material changes preceding catastrophic failure.
