# Avouvar Architecture

**Core intention:** "We remain. We have time. We were here before you arrived; we will be here after you leave."

Material culture speaks across centuries. Patience is the medium.

---

## Physical Environment

Asovoë occupies the southern shore of the Fraser River delta, where the river meets tidal waters.

**The glaciers:** Visible to the north on clear days—a wall of white marking the end of habitability. This is the furthest north outpost of human settlement; there is nothing beyond.

**Climate:** Cold and wet. Persistent fog rolling off the ice sheets, rain more days than not, temperatures hovering 35-50°F most of the year. The harbor ices over completely for months each winter, isolating the city.

**The aurora:** Charged particles interacting with the Interdict produce structured geometric light in greens, blues, magentas, violets that flash and flow through winter nights. Not diffuse like baseline auroras but structured, following invisible force lines.

## Materials

**Dark concrete:** Nearly indestructible, smooth, absorbing light.

**Shining black alloy:** Reflective, scattering aurora colors into fragments.

**The combination:** Dark masses punctuated by gleaming highlights, absorbing and scattering light simultaneously.

**Autofactory fabric:** Original purpose forgotten. Perfected through generations of experimentation.

## Wrong Proportions

Architecture built for every Imperial caste—none of them Avouvar:

- Highborn corridors fifteen feet wide and thirty feet high
- Doors twelve feet tall
- Stairs six inches high and three feet deep (for legs that moved differently)
- Warborn barracks for half-ton soldiers
- Technical Caste geometries that baseline cognition finds disorienting

Ten thousand years of adaptation and the architecture remains Highborn, Warborn, Technical Caste—built for beings who are gone.

---

## They Do Not Build. They Modify.

This is the crucial difference from every other culture.

**The autofactory maintains everything.** Streets cleaned for absent millions. Lights functioning. Infrastructure operating at capacity for populations that never returned. The Avouvar don't need to build because everything is already built and will continue to be maintained forever.

**Modification vocabulary:**
- **Platforms built on furniture:** Creating surfaces at accessible heights in Highborn spaces with forty-foot ceilings
- **Fabric screens:** Partitioning vast chambers into human-scaled spaces
- **Walkways:** Bridging stairs designed for longer legs, assembled from autofactory structural elements
- **Accumulated small modifications:** Each refined by the next occupant across centuries

---

## Hobbyist Craftsmanship at Civilizational Scale

An Avouvar craftsperson might spend sixty years perfecting a single technique. A screen-maker might produce three hundred screens across a four-hundred-year life, each incrementally better than the last.

**The autofactory's mysterious bounty:** The materials aren't natural. Wood is almost unknown. Natural fiber, natural dye—exotic luxuries, not baseline materials.

Instead, the autofactory produces constantly, maintaining output for millions:
- **Fabric:** Strong, takes dye beautifully, drapes in ways natural silk doesn't. Original purpose unknown.
- **Ceramic elements:** Geometric forms—hexagons, octagons, complex polyhedra. Meant as structural components?
- **Metal wire:** Hair-thin to finger-thick, in alloys that don't corrode. Braided into jewelry, furniture joints, bindings.
- **Pigments:** Concentrated colors for industrial processes. Diluted and experimented with across generations.

**Figuring out uses is the craft.** A family might spend two hundred years learning what one pigment does when applied to one fabric type in different concentrations.

## Aurora Dress

Against absolute darkness, clothing becomes transcendent. Dozens of long scarves in aurora shades—deep blues, electric greens, magentas shifting toward violet, colors unnamed in other languages.

**House affiliations** encoded in which colors touch, which cross over, which trail longest.

**Color floating on darkness.** Avouvar bodies absorb light completely; their clothing carries it.

## Time Scale

**"We remain."** Infrastructure for millions maintained by thousands says: "Others left or died. We're still here."

**"We have time."** Decoration in registers that require decades to perceive. "You can't see what we see. You don't have time to see it."

**"Patience is our medium."** With centuries to look, why make things bold? What looks empty to you is full to us.

**Hot zone austerity:** What point is visual novelty when you'll look at the same wall for four hundred years? The simplicity is sophistication we can't access.

**Cool zone variation:** Lower contamination means shorter lifespans—only one or two centuries. More decorative variation because viewers have less time.

---

## Generating New Details

*Example: A wall in a hot-zone residence.* To a visitor: blank, unremarkable. To the resident who has lived there for three centuries: a chronicle. The slight discoloration from the wet century. The texture change from refinishing after the trade war. The almost-invisible repair from when a visitor (now two hundred years dead) damaged the surface. The wall is dense with memory.

*Example: A master screen-maker's workshop.* She has been making screens for two hundred and forty years, using fabric the autofactory produces for something no one remembers. Her early work embarrasses her with its crudity. Her current technique involves layering material in ways that create patterns only visible after twenty years of looking.

*Example: A platform in a Highborn chamber.* Built eighty years ago. Sits on furniture—ancient chairs repurposed as structural elements—creating usable surface twelve feet below the original ceiling. Stairs are Avouvar-scaled, railings are braided metal wire in a pattern this family has refined across six generations. It still doesn't feel like it belongs there. It never will.
