# The Medina Guild System

> **Foundation:** Ancient charters predating Bureau authority
> **Scale:** Neighborhoods (large guilds) or streets (small guilds)
> **Function:** Legal protection from permit raj, quality control, monopoly enforcement
> **Original design:** Automated production operators—now protecting handcraft

## The Permit Raj

The Imperial bureaucracy has accumulated millennia of regulations governing commercial activity:
- Permits from Scale (premises)
- Certifications from Rod (equipment)
- Registrations with Coin (taxes)
- Safety compliance through channels no one can identify

**The requirements contradict each other.** A food vendor needs:
- Health certification (Creche sub-office)
- Commercial licensing (Scale)
- Tax registration (Coin)
- Equipment permits (Rod if using heating)

Each permit's fees, waiting periods, and documentary requirements assume the others don't exist. **Full legal compliance would take years and cost more than most businesses earn.**

This isn't deliberate oppression—it's accumulated bureaucratic sediment no one has authority to clear.

## How Guild Membership Cuts Through

A charter from before the Bureaus existed cannot be invalidated by Bureau regulations issued after.

When a Scale inspector demands to see a craftsman's commercial premises license, **guild credentials are sufficient response**:
- The guild charter grants all members the right to practice their craft
- The Bureau can take complaints to the guild masters, not to individual members
- The guild handles its own standards, quality control, internal discipline
- Bureau authority stops at the guild's boundaries

## The Original Design vs Current Reality

**Original design:** Guild members as independent operators of automated production facilities—small versions of ya-Don's Furnaces.

**Current reality:** The automation is gone. The charters remain, creating legal frameworks for automated production that now protect manual craftwork.

A charter granting rights to "operate automated thermal processing units within specifications established by Bureau of Rod certification" now protects a blacksmith:
- Heating metal over a coal forge
- Hammering it by hand
- Creating bespoke products that violate every specification
- Because the automated unit broke down eight centuries ago

**This fossilization creates the Empire's economic stagnation.**

## Guild Neighborhoods (Large Guilds)

### The Clothiers' Quarter
- ~15 blocks in Medina's central section
- Charter grants exclusive right to "production, finishing, and sale of textile garments"
- Boundaries predate current street names, require periodic arbitration
- Guild hall: four-story structure built against ancient arcology remnant
- ~300 workshops, warehouses, cramped apartments for journeymen

### The Salvage Guild's Medina Territory
- Extends from Archive Gate into commercial districts
- Charter language: "all materials recovered from pre-Interdict sources requiring assessment, certification, or modification"
- Less geographically coherent—scattered offices, warehouses, certified dealers
- Certification turns gray market goods into white market goods

### The Grocers' District
- Blocks surrounding the Grain Exchange
- Charter covers "provisions necessary for sustenance"
- Centuries of jurisdictional disputes with Butchers ("flesh") and Bakers ("processed grain products")
- Daily rhythm: pre-dawn arrivals, morning wholesale, afternoon retail

## Guild Streets (Small Guilds)

### Booksellers' Row
- ~300 meters, beginning at Archive Gate
- Boundaries marked by bronze plaques with deep-antiquity patina
- ~80 members, 23 licensed shops
- Charter assumes automated printing facilities that no longer exist

**Many's countinghouse** occupies the most prominent position:
- Anyone entering Medina from Rotten Quarter passes Many's doors first
- Queue extends into street on busy days
- Ta-Kefyeh enforcers maintain egalitarian order—no queue-jumping regardless of status

### Coppersmith Lane
- ~200 meters
- Original grant: "thermal processing of copper and copper alloys within automated facilities"
- Current reality: hand tools, coal forges, apprenticeship techniques
- ~40 workshops, constant hammering, smell of hot metal

### The Alley of Lenses
- ~60 meters of narrow passage
- Opticians' Guild: 32 members, 18 shops
- Charter assumes "automated optical fabrication systems"
- Reality: painstaking handwork, years of apprenticeship
- Journeymen sit at benches for hours, slowly shaping glass

## Outside Guild Territory

**Unlicensed vendors:** Cart vendors moving through streets, paying informal fees, never stopping long enough to trigger enforcement.

**Precariously licensed:** Operations with paperwork (permits, registrations) but lacking ancient charter protection. A 20-year-old Bureau license can be revoked; many effectively pay protection to established guilds.

**Gray space:** The gaps between guild territories where regulations exist but enforcement doesn't arrive. Scribes copying personal correspondence (not official contracts), vendors selling items that fall between guild jurisdictions.

The system is inefficient, inconsistent, and thoroughly adequate for the commerce it enables.
