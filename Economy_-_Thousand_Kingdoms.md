## Core Principle: Gambling Redistribution

Wealth distributes through rigged games where everyone knows the rigging. A successful warband leader "loses" to subordinates who need resources. A widow with seven children develops miraculous luck. Distribution follows need, disguised as fortune.

This violates market logic completely. In capitalism, need requires offering something in exchange. In the Thousand Kingdoms, need produces favorable dice. The mechanism is transparent to participants but maintains the fiction that fortune rather than charity determined outcomes. Direct aid would be "theft of sacred experience"; gambling losses are fortune's judgment.

The salmon economy is seasonal and non-accumulable. For six weeks everyone feasts; the rest of the year survives on preserved fish. Canal positions are held by force and inheritance, not purchased in markets.

**Historical parallel:** Pacific Northwest potlatch economies where wealth demonstrated through giving, combined with Central Asian pastoral economics of raid and patronage.

## Major Institutions

### Khan Ataru Blackfish's Salmon Enterprise (Representative Warband)

**What it does:** Commercial empire built on salmon runs—controlling weirs, smokehouses, processing infrastructure throughout Ogon's western districts. Approximately 800 warriors plus commercial infrastructure; 70 vassals controlling weir positions.

**Cash flow in:**
- Salmon processing: ~500,000 fish during six-week run = 200,000-300,000 obols equivalent annually
- Vassal tribute: 70 vassals pay fixed obligations regardless of fortune
- Mercenary contracts: auxiliaries for Imperial tasks

**Annual revenue equivalent:** 400,000-600,000 obols in good years; much less in bad.

**Cash flow out:**
- Warrior support: ~100,000 obols equivalent annually
- Infrastructure maintenance
- Gambling redistribution: Ataru "loses" at dice to subordinates who need resources
- Public goods: libraries, infrastructure

**Decision-making:** Ataru commands. His vassals owe fixed tribute; he owes them protection and opportunity. Decisions are personal—no council, no vote, no bureaucracy. Authority derives from demonstrated capability: good years produce loyalty; bad years produce desertion.

**Distinctive feature:** The tribute is fixed regardless of fortune. If your weir position produces poorly and your raid target is protected, you still owe Ataru his fish. This forces vassals to raid, trade, or find other means—Ataru cannot excuse obligations without undermining the system.

### Kma-Dhol Engineering Firm (Representative Technical Operation)

**What it does:** Assesses autofactory ruins, advises Akama clients on safe salvage, provides technical expertise. Perhaps 50 specialists in one extended family operation.

**Cash flow in:**
- Assessment contracts: 2,000-4,000 obols annually
- Advisory fees: 50-100 obols per complex project
- Imperial factor contracts: 5,000-10,000 obols annually
- Artifact authentication

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 15,000-25,000 obols.

**Cash flow out:**
- Family compound (communal property)
- Food (imported from Imperial sources): 3,000-5,000 obols annually
- Equipment and reference materials: 2,000-3,000 obols annually
- Religious observance

**Decision-making:** Family elders make business decisions through consensus. Technical expertise concentrates in senior members trained through years of apprenticeship.

**Distinctive feature:** The Kma-Dhol can direct autofactory output more precisely than anyone admits. Some of what the Salvage Guild buys was produced to specification. If the Guild knew, they'd have to acknowledge it. So no one asks.

### The Junta of Ogon

**What it does:** Coordination between seven token-holders who control Ogon's military politics. Not a government—no laws, no bureaucracy, no monopoly on violence.

**Cash flow:** No direct revenue. Individual members fund participation from warband resources. Minimal administrative support for the Slab meeting space.

**Decision-making:** Seven irreplaceable tokens produced by the Ogon autofactory seventy years ago. Whoever holds a token sits on the Junta. Method of acquisition—purchase, inheritance, combat, theft—is irrelevant.

Monthly meetings at the Slab; votes by raised or lowered hands; dissent can be challenged through trial by combat.

**The Accession Ritual:** New members must survive a night of beatings from existing members. Not a test of strength—you cannot fight back. A test of endurance.

**Distinctive feature:** The Junta prevents any single Khan from dominating. Seven holders with different power bases produce stability without systematic governance.

## Household Profiles

### Household 16: Saya Blue-Fish, Malpais Village Family (Salmon Economy)

**Profile:** Akama female, 40s. Widowed, five children (ages 8-19). Village headwoman. Malpais settlement on the Grand Canal.

**Monthly Allocation:** Variable—controls village distribution

| Season | Resources |
|--------|-----------|
| Salmon run (6 weeks) | Abundant—everyone feasts |
| Processing/preservation | Work-intensive, food adequate |
| Winter | Preserved fish, stored grain, careful rationing |
| Spring | Leanest—waiting for next run |

**Budget Balance:** The concept doesn't apply. Saya doesn't earn income—she manages distribution. Good runs mean abundance shared; bad runs mean everyone tightens equally.

**Possessions:**
- House (village property, allocated to headwoman)
- Processing equipment
- Preserved food stores
- Tools, weapons
- Objects with stories—her late husband's knife, her mother's spindle

**Economic Anxieties:**
- The salmon. Everything depends on the run.
- The children. Her oldest son wants to join a warband; her daughter wants to marry a Kma-Dhol boy.
- The tribute. Khan Tren Green-Eye expects fish regardless of the run.
- Winter. Always winter.

### Household 17: Gharn Three-Rivers, Warband Rider (Warband Economy)

**Profile:** Akama male, 25. Single. Rider in Khan Ataru Blackfish's warband. Lives in barracks when in Ogon, on the trail otherwise.

**Monthly Allocation:** ~8 obols equivalent in goods/loot (highly variable)

| Category | Provision |
|----------|-----------|
| Housing | Barracks (provided) |
| Food | Warband meals (provided) |
| Equipment | Warband issue plus personal accumulation |
| Cash | Occasional—mostly from loot or gambling |

**Budget Balance:** Gharn doesn't think in budgets. The warband feeds him, houses him, arms him. What he earns through combat, gambling, and favor accumulates as objects and reputation, not savings.

**Possessions:**
- Horse (his most valuable possession—gift from Ataru after a successful raid)
- Weapons (axe inherited from father, knife won at dice)
- Armor (warband issue, personalized over years)
- Clothing appropriate to rider status
- Small collection of loot (coins, a silver ring, an Imperial compass)
- Nothing else

**Economic Anxieties:**
- The horse. If it dies, he's nothing.
- Ataru's favor. Success brings gifts; failure brings nothing.
- Age. Warband riding favors the young; what happens at 40?
- Fortune. He believes genuinely in standing in the rain—good times end, bad times end, fortune redistributes.

### Household 18: Vey Khet-Dorel, Kma-Dhol Engineer (Technical Family)

**Profile:** Kma-Dhol male, 35. Married to Kma-Dhol woman (arranged within community). Two children (6 and 3). Lives in upper-level compound in Ogon ziggurat.

**Monthly Income:** ~60 obols (family share of firm revenue)

| Category | Amount | % |
|----------|--------|---|
| Housing | 0 | 0% |
| Food (imported) | 25 | 42% |
| Clothing | 5 | 8% |
| Equipment | 8 | 13% |
| Religious observance | 5 | 8% |
| Children's education | 8 | 13% |
| Savings | 9 | 15% |

**Budget Balance:** Modestly positive. The family accumulates slowly—savings held in Many deposit at San Juan factoria.

**Possessions:**
- Compound quarters (family property)
- Technical instruments
- Reference materials
- Clothing (Kma-Dhol prefer simpler styles than Akama)
- Third Oracle devotional objects
- Children's learning materials

**Economic Anxieties:**
- Imperial connections. The Salvage Guild could stop buying; the assessments could stop coming.
- Akama clients. If a Khan decides the Kma-Dhol know too much...
- The community. Endogamy is mandatory; finding appropriate matches for children is difficult.
- The secret. They can direct autofactory output. If this became known...

## Consistency Requirements

- Thousand Kingdoms revenue is seasonal and variable—good salmon years vs. bad
- Gambling redistribution disguises charity as fortune's judgment
- Warband riders are fed, housed, equipped by warbands—obol conversion is misleading
- Kma-Dhol can direct autofactory output more precisely than anyone admits
- The Junta's seven tokens are irreplaceable; method of acquisition doesn't affect legitimacy
- Fixed tribute obligations force vassals to raid, trade, or find alternatives
