# The Districts of Ya-Don

## Geographic Overview

Ya-Don occupies the southern end of the Central Valley, where the flat agricultural plain rises toward the Sierra Nevada foothills. The city spreads across approximately fifteen square miles of semi-arid basin, shaped by the Kern River to the north, the irrigation networks branching from it, and the rail lines connecting to ya-Sattra and the coastal provinces.

The climate is brutal: summer temperatures exceed 110°F for weeks; winter brings occasional frost. Annual rainfall averages twelve inches, concentrated in winter months. The landscape beyond irrigated zones is tan and ochre, dotted with engineered blood-red trees along watercourses.

The Furnaces sit at the city's heart, slightly north of geographic center. The oldest residential districts cluster immediately south and west; newer development spreads in all directions, with recent expansion pushing into agricultural margins.

## The Furnaces

**Location:** City center, slightly north of geographic middle  
**Population:** No permanent residents; workforce of ~40,000 across shifts  
**Character:** Industrial complex, the city's reason for existence

Two square miles of interconnected production systems, bounded by walls and checkpoints. The Furnace Board governs; the guilds administer; the Bureau of the Rod maintains nominal oversight. Workers enter through guild-controlled checkpoints with credentials marking union affiliation and access levels.

### Notable Locations

**The Crucible** (Line 7-Tesseral)  
Western quadrant. Material preparation facility for culverin production. Conception chambers that glow in colors Orevet can see and baselines cannot.

**The Lattice Hall** (Line 7-Tesseral)  
Western quadrant. Mutterer choir facility where crystalline matrices for culverins are produced. Acoustic properties are precise; even minor architectural modifications disrupt production.

**Primary Deposition** (Line 3-Orichal)  
Southeastern quadrant, deep sections. Creates crystalline substrates for lector computational cores. Restricted access; Unwritten-Axiom Parish territory.

**Chamber 7-Keth-Ascending**  
Southeastern quadrant, deepest accessible point. Pre-Interdict chamber containing six mummified Medials adorned with jewelry made from reject electronic components. Sacred site for Unwritten-Axiom Parish. Temperature exceeds 60°C; visits limited to minutes.

**Line 12-Aurech Biological Cultivation**  
Northern section. Two hundred tanks containing bioluminescent organisms for Imperial illumination systems. Five tanks have died in recorded history; their capabilities are permanently lost.

**Cooling System Hub** (Line 15-Vorath)  
Adjacent to primary cooling infrastructure. Produces heat-exchange components under es-Vorath patent. The recursive relationship—cooling systems producing cooling systems—makes this facility both essential and vulnerable.

## The Threshold

**Location:** Immediately south of the Furnaces  
**Population:** ~80,000  
**Character:** Oldest residential district; transition zone between industrial and domestic life

The name reflects function: the crossing point where workers transition from shift to home. Streets orient toward the checkpoints that thousands pass through at each shift change. Mixed-caste housing reflects the Furnaces' mixed workforce.

### Notable Locations

**Cathedral of St. Akhmet**  
Western edge, anchoring a plaza where Assembly crowds gather. Ya-Don's largest religious structure—functional rather than beautiful, massive enough to hold thousands, acoustically engineered so a single voice reaches every corner. The Arch-Curate's residence occupies an adjacent building.

**The Burning Rest**  
Three blocks from the Cathedral. Tavern in a former furnace monitoring station; original control panels remain embedded in one wall. Proprietor: **Common-Task Veradha** (Ashrat, late forties). Union meetings happen in the back room; Curates drink here after Assembly. Art deco stools, patchwork Penitent-guild wall hangings, stone bar. Drinks calibrated for Ashrat metabolism.

**The Shift Bell**  
Intersection of Furnace Road and Threshold Street. Ancient bronze bell that once marked shift changes; now ceremonial. Workers touch it for luck entering or leaving the Furnaces.

**Korrad's Kitchen**  
Side street off Cathedral Plaza. Food stall run by **Senna Korrad** (Ashrat, 34) during off-shift hours. Known for festival preparations and experimental Ashrat cuisine. She's writing a cookbook.

**The Order of St. Akhmet Administrative Offices**  
Adjacent to Cathedral. Union headquarters; Master Tender council meets here. The Confession Booths—sealed meeting rooms—are where real negotiations happen.

**Threshold Medical Clinic**  
Near southern checkpoint. Caste-specific medical services: Ashrat respiratory care, Orevet optical treatment, Redback metabolic management. Staffed by practitioners who understand bodies that ya-Sattra's standardized medicine fails.

## Ember Row

**Location:** East of the Threshold, climbing toward Sierra foothills  
**Population:** ~60,000  
**Character:** Family housing; marginally cooler temperatures from elevation

Developed as overflow from the Threshold for workers wanting distance from constant Furnace presence. Higher proportions of near-baselines and Orevet whose bodies benefit from temperature differential.

### Notable Locations

**Parish Church of St. Calis**  
District center. More architecturally ambitious than the Cathedral—builders attempted beauty with mixed results that residents defend loyally. Curate elected by congregation.

**The Overlook**  
Terraced plaza on the Ridge's highest point. Views across the entire city: Furnaces to the north, agricultural districts spreading south and west, Sierra foothills rising east. Families gather here evenings to watch the Furnaces' glow.

**Ember Row Market**  
Daily market serving domestic needs. Food vendors for diverse castes; craftspeople producing household goods for different body types; specialty items unavailable in the Threshold's shift-focused commerce.

**Veris Optical Services**  
Branch operation of Bright Eyes Optical serving Ember Row's Orevet population. Smaller than the St. Oreli's Shade location but convenient for families who've settled east.

**The Coalbed Inn**  
Residential inn catering to workers whose shifts make commuting impractical. Weekly rates; shared cooking facilities; predominantly near-baseline clientele.

## The Depths of St. Yerren

**Location:** Underground, beneath portions of the Threshold, Ember Row, and Furnaces  
**Population:** ~15,000  
**Character:** Three-dimensional Karst district; tunnel systems that surface dwellers barely know exist

Surface entrances are unobtrusive—doorways in ordinary buildings, stairways descending from street level, ventilation shafts. The underground geography bears no relationship to streets above; chambers connect through geology rather than surface property lines.

### Notable Locations

**St. Yerren's Shrine**  
Deepest accessible point for general worship. Carved around the site where the legendary Karst worker died after maintaining systems in darkness for three days following a tunnel collapse. Pilgrimage site before descending to the Furnaces' deepest sections.

**The Speaking Stones**  
Junction of six major tunnel systems. Acoustic properties allow Karst tap-communication to carry for hundreds of meters. Community announcements are tapped here; the vibrations reach most of the Depths.

**Oma's Chamber**  
Tertiary corridor, mid-depth. Home of **Oma Tessik** (Karst, 41), pressure-seal technician. Unremarkable except as example of Depths residential space: low ceiling, minimal lighting, proportions suited for Karst physiology.

**The Deep Market**  
Weekly gathering in an excavated commercial space. Vendors selling high-nutrient foods, specialized equipment, goods suited for underground life. Surface merchants rarely attend; the disorientation that tunnel systems produce in non-Karst visitors makes commerce impractical.

**Depths Administrative Center**  
Near primary surface access points. Where liaison functions with surface institutions occur. Karst community governance happens here; surface administrators rarely attempt to visit directly.

## St. Oreli's Shade

**Location:** Western edge of city  
**Population:** ~25,000  
**Character:** Orevet quarter; architecture designed for light-sensitive vision

Afternoon shadows arrive earliest here; buildings exploit natural shade patterns. Deep overhangs block direct sunlight; small windows with heavy curtains; interiors painted in muted colors. Street lamps dimmer than elsewhere; shop signs in colors that baseline vision reads as nearly identical but Orevet vision distinguishes easily.

### Notable Locations

**Shrine of St. Oreli**  
District center. Memorial to the Orevet furnace-seer whose perception of developing failure saved the Furnaces from catastrophe. Pilgrimage site for Orevet workers; the dim interior is comfortable for their vision.

**Bright Eyes Optical**  
Prominent corner location. Proprietor: **Trembling-Light Voreth** (Orevet, 60s), master craftsman making eyewear for four decades. Heavy goggles calibrated for individual customers; equipment repair for professional monitoring instruments. Wife **Warm-Shadow Ketta** handles business operations.

**The Dim Hearth**  
Tavern catering to Orevet clientele. Lighting barely sufficient for baseline vision; Orevet patrons find it comfortable. Food emphasizes textures over visual presentation; drinks served in vessels Orevet can identify by touch.

**Vorran Household**  
Residential street, western section. Home of **Keth Vorran** (Orevet, 47) and partner **Veris Thenn**. Keth's vision is degrading; Veris has already transitioned to administrative work after losing her enhanced sight.

**The Shade Gallery**  
Exhibition space for Orevet visual art—heat-maps of the Furnaces painted in colors Orevet see and then translated for baseline-visible spectrum. Visitors from outside the district find the work abstractly pretty; Orevet recognize it as depictions of thermal reality.

## The Freightyard

**Location:** Northern edge, around rail terminus  
**Population:** ~45,000  
**Character:** Logistics district; Covenant of Saint Aureline territory

Rail terminus where lines from ya-Sattra arrive; cargo facilities for goods transfer; warehouses for material accumulation. District rhythms follow cargo schedules rather than Furnace shifts.

### Notable Locations

**Rail Terminus**  
Pre-Interdict structure, original function unknown. Platforms and switching systems modified over millennia for Imperial rail technology; basic infrastructure remains ancient.

**Covenant Administrative Hall**  
Union headquarters for the Covenant of Saint Aureline. Pragmatic logisticians and tough negotiators rather than devout clerics. The building is functional, undecorated, focused on the commerce it coordinates.

**Suran's Caloric House**  
Former warehouse near terminus. Proprietor: **Keth Suran** (near-baseline, third generation). Restaurant serving labor caste metabolic requirements. Menu organized by caloric density; twelve kitchen staff working continuous shifts. Prices posted in Imperial currency and guild scrip.

**The Weighmaster's Office**  
Covenant facility where cargo is assessed for tariffs. Every shipment through ya-Don passes this checkpoint; the Covenant's leverage derives from their control of this bottleneck.

**Redback Row**  
Residential street near primary loading facilities. Housing designed for Redback proportions: wider doorways, reinforced furniture, high ceilings. Predominantly cargo handlers and their families.

**The Transit Chapel**  
Covenant-maintained religious facility. Services timed to shift changes; attendance is technically required for union membership, genuinely observed by some workers and perfunctorily attended by others.

## The Amber Quarter

**Location:** Eastern edge of Freightyard  
**Population:** ~8,000  
**Character:** Serrulata trading district; connection to Republic of Ganat

Architecture reflects Ganati influence: rounded adobe forms, copper-inlaid decoration, color palette of rust, ochre, and teal. Population predominantly Serrulata with near-baseline staff and occasional members of other castes in cross-border commerce.

### Notable Locations

**The Amber House**  
Primary trading establishment. Proprietor: **Yev-Tessik Arandi** (Serrulata, 40s). Handles commerce between ya-Don and Ganat: Furnace components eastbound, maguey-silk and copper work westbound. Dim interior designed for Serrulata comfort; employs mixed staff including **Weightless-Burden Kass** (Ashrat) as guild liaison.

**The Amber Quarter Chapel**  
Ganati-style Penitent services. Liturgy follows Republican forms; theological emphases differ from Imperial cousin faith. Visitors from the Threshold find the differences disorienting.

**Taho Textiles**  
Maguey-silk merchant. Fabrics in weights and weaves Imperial looms cannot produce. Supplies Kallis Verret's shop and other near-baseline merchants who resell to Imperial customers.

**The Crossing House**  
Inn catering to traders moving between Imperial and Republican territories. Rooms designed for Serrulata comfort but accommodating baseline visitors. Information exchange happens in the common room; what's discussed there shapes trade decisions across both territories.

**Arandi Warehouses**  
Storage facilities for goods awaiting transport or sale. Officially documented contents; unofficially, some materials that neither party discusses in detail.

## The Coolant District

**Location:** Near primary heat-exchange facilities  
**Population:** ~2,000  
**Character:** Bureau of the Rod enclave; Institute of Flow territory

Architecture distinctly coastal: buildings designed for active climate control. The es-Vorath compound anchors the district. Population predominantly near-baseline with technical training, recruited from ya-Sattra.

### Notable Locations

**The es-Vorath Compound**  
Walled residence maintaining internal temperatures comfortable for Highborn bodies. Climate-controlled interiors, formal gardens. Home of **Sri Pater Cassien es-Vorath** (Highborn Tessarine, early fifties) and extended family.

**Institute of Flow Headquarters**  
Technical facility managing the cooling systems that prevent the Furnaces from destroying themselves. Near-baseline technicians completing the Seven-Cornered Examination work here; their expertise is the Bureau's only real leverage in ya-Don.

**The Formal Chapel**  
Imperial-style Penitent services approved by the Bureau of the Lens. Theology carefully calibrated to avoid ya-Don's heterodox enthusiasms. Sparsely attended; most Institute workers maintain coastal religious habits.

**Institute Housing Blocks**  
Residential facilities for technical staff. Coastal accents persist; children sent to ya-Sattra for education. Social separation from surrounding labor neighborhoods is nearly complete.

## Thornback Ridge

**Location:** Rising ground southeast of Threshold  
**Population:** ~35,000  
**Character:** Mixed residential; overflow from older districts

Named for a geological feature—a low rise of harder stone resisting erosion. Housing climbs the flanks in terraced rows. Mixed population without strong caste majorities.

### Notable Locations

**Parish of Mended-Spirits**  
District church. Name reflects Penitent concept of redemption through acknowledged failure. Mainstream theology by ya-Don standards (heterodox by ya-Sattra standards). Democratic governance; homiletic names mark roughly a quarter of congregation.

**Ridge Market**  
Twice-weekly market on the district's largest terrace. Mixed goods for mixed population; less specialized than Threshold or Freightyard commerce.

**The Prospect**  
Tavern at the Ridge's highest point. Known for views; workers gather after shifts to watch the Furnaces' glow. Mixed clientele; drinks for various metabolisms.

**Thornback Clinic**  
Medical facility serving the Ridge's diverse population. General practice rather than caste-specialized; refers complex cases to Threshold Medical.

## The Sifting Grounds

**Location:** Eastern margin, downwind from city proper  
**Population:** ~1,000 permanent; transient workforce varies  
**Character:** Waste processing; Cinder-Sifter territory

Industrial margin where hazardous materials are processed. Location chosen for practical reasons—distance from residential areas protects broader population from contamination; isolation serves unofficial purposes.

### Notable Locations

**Primary Processing Facility**  
Where hazardous Furnace waste is sorted, separated, recovered or disposed. Genuinely essential work performed by workers accepting hazard premiums. Also where materials that shouldn't exist are diverted to appropriate channels.

**Curate Malachi's Chapel**  
On-site religious facility. Services led by **Curate Malachi** (near-baseline, late fifties) when present. Theology is standard Penitent; the congregation understands that the Curate's other activities fund the hospices their faith demands.

**The Sifter Dormitories**  
Housing for workers whose shifts make commuting impractical. Functional rather than comfortable; most employees live elsewhere.

**Storage Shed 7**  
One of several facilities where questionable materials accumulate before processing through networks reaching as far as Ganat. Contents described in coded ledgers. A Bureau inspector examining the right shed at the right moment would find evidence of crimes spanning decades. No inspector has.

## The Aureate Enclave

**Location:** Low hill, southern edge  
**Population:** ~500  
**Character:** Highborn residential; patent-holding families

Elevation provides marginally cooler temperatures; distance provides marginally cleaner air. Architecture aspires to coastal elegance without quite achieving it.

### Notable Locations

**The es-Kiral Compound**  
Largest structure. Walled estate with climate-controlled interiors, formal gardens watered by private cisterns. Home of **Sri Pater Morain es-Kiral** (Highborn Amantine, fifth decade) and heir **Veradis es-Kiral** (31). The family holds the Furnace patent but cannot exercise the authority it theoretically grants.

**The es-Etaie Residence**  
Deliberately modest by Aureate standards. Home of **Sri Matrice Dawn-of-Ascension es-Etaie** (Highborn Oravan, seventh decade). The family's capitation arrangement—paying Penitent head taxes for all ~400,000 guildsmen—funds religious freedom throughout ya-Don.

**The Enclave Gardens**  
Shared green space between compounds. Watered by private cisterns; the only significant ornamental vegetation in ya-Don. Aureate families gather here for the social functions that substitute for genuine governance.

**The Formal Shrine**  
Small Oracle Cult facility. The Aureate families practice formal religion when they practice at all; the es-Etaie exception emphasizes how unusual their Penitent devotion is.

## St. Brennam's Garden

**Location:** Southern and western edges  
**Population:** ~20,000 across scattered settlements  
**Character:** Agricultural districts; food production for the city

Collective name for farming operations where irrigation transforms semi-arid land into productive fields. Boundary between city and agriculture blurs across miles of transitional zones.

### Notable Locations

**Brennam Agricultural Collective**  
Largest operation. Organized as Penitent parish rather than commercial enterprise. **Curate Foundation-Stones Gerram** leads both religious community and agricultural production. Perhaps 300 members across extended families; predominantly near-baseline with Serrulata characteristics from generations of intermarriage.

**The Processing Hall**  
Where harvest becomes food the city can use. Grain milling, vegetable preparation; copper-clad dome visible from main road, weathered to verdigris providing the only green in otherwise tan landscape.

**Parish of Common-Ground**  
Serves agricultural district's religious needs. Name reflects theological emphasis on shared labor and collective purpose. Curate is a farmer herself.

**Irrigation Control Station**  
Where water allocation for the agricultural districts is managed. Ancient infrastructure; the water rights predate current Imperial administrative structure by millennia.

## Warren-Under-Stone

**Location:** Beneath multiple surface districts  
**Population:** ~5,000  
**Character:** Ductworker quarter; infrastructure spaces

Exists in three dimensions—basement levels, sub-basements, infrastructure corridors. Geography vertical as much as horizontal; boundaries defined by access points rather than streets.

### Notable Locations

**Deep Throat Maintenance Office**  
Basement space sized for Ductworker proportions. Headquarters of the Thost family's infrastructure maintenance company. Proprietor: **Minni Thost** (Ductworker). Clients rarely visit; most business happens on-site in infrastructure spaces.

**The Gathering Hall**  
Excavated communal space where Ductworker community meets weekly. Conversations sound strange to outsiders: detailed descriptions of shaft configurations, debates about compression techniques, fond memories of particularly challenging crawls.

**Warren Market**  
Small-scale commercial space serving Ductworker needs. Food vendors specializing in compact, calorie-dense meals; craftspeople producing specialized tools and equipment for infrastructure maintenance.

**St. Tenna Memorial**  
Shrine to the Ductworker who discovered a catastrophic blockage in the Furnaces' primary ventilation system and died clearing it. Feast day observed with processions through infrastructure corridors.

## The Quick Lanes

**Location:** Marginal land northwest of city  
**Population:** ~3,000  
**Character:** Astal settlement; rapid-generation agricultural community

Where irrigation doesn't quite reach and other castes haven't bothered to establish claims. No formal structure; leadership changes too frequently for stable institutions; buildings constructed for replacement rather than permanence.

### Notable Locations

**Swift Generations Farm**  
Largest operation. No single proprietor in baseline sense; current leader approximately **Twitching-Nose** (Astal, age unclear). Fast-growing crops planted and harvested in cycles that would exhaust baseline farmers.

**The Quick Chapel**  
Simplified Penitent services. Liturgy reduced to essentials transmittable quickly; theology condensed into aphorisms. Celebrant rotates through community members who remember the necessary words.

**Astal Market**  
Irregular gatherings where Serrulata intermediaries buy agricultural surplus. **Yev-Tessik Arandi** of the Amber House has developed relationships with the community that persist despite constant personnel turnover.

## Pilgrim's Rest

**Location:** Southern approach to ya-Don  
**Population:** ~2,000 permanent; transient varies  
**Character:** Traveler services; gateway to hermitage routes

Where the road from the hermitages enters the city. Developed to serve pilgrims returning from wilderness contemplation.

### Notable Locations

**The Wayfarer's Inn**  
Largest lodging establishment. Caters to pilgrims in various states—some seeking rest before returning home, others preparing for journeys to hermitages, some in spiritual crisis seeking guidance before deciding next steps.

**Chapel of Transition**  
Serves pilgrims with liturgies focused on return—prayers for safe arrival, thanksgiving for journeys completed, preparation for reentry into working life. Contrast with industrial religion of the Threshold is marked.

**The Hermitage Roads**  
Trails extending south and east into wilderness where hermit-priests maintain isolated chapels. Not roads in formal sense—worn by generations of pilgrims, marked by cairns, occasionally maintained by volunteers.

**The Hermitage of St. Caris the Still**  
Four hours' walk from the city. Pre-Interdict structure maintained by **Father Silence-Before-Dawn** (near-baseline, 60s) for thirty-seven years. Domed structure of pale concrete with massive circular void where a window would be expected. Pilgrims seek contemplation rather than answers.

## The Verethani Compound

**Location:** Near Furnaces' southeastern access points  
**Population:** ~300  
**Character:** Endogamous Highborn community; Unwritten-Axiom Parish members

Cluster of buildings functioning as district in miniature. The five families—Vereth, Somnal, Tessik-Vor, Keth-Ascending, and Dwell-Silent—have lived here longer than anyone can document.

### Notable Locations

**The Compound Hall**  
Central gathering space for the five families. Architecture suggests origins contemporaneous with the Furnaces; modifications accumulated across millennia of continuous occupation.

**Family Residences**  
Five distinct household compounds, each maintained by one of the Verethani families. Unusual proportions; acoustic features that baseline construction lacks; materials whose composition resists casual analysis.

**The Deep Access**  
Passage connecting the compound directly to Furnaces' southeastern sections. The Verethani work in deep sections alongside Karst, Ductworkers, and Orevet; this route allows them to reach their work without passing through general Furnace checkpoints.

## District Relationships

The districts relate through function rather than formal administration:

- **The Threshold** depends on **the Freightyard** for supplies
- **The Freightyard** depends on **the Furnaces** for cargo
- **The Furnaces** depend on **St. Brennam's Garden** for food
- **The Garden** depends on the Covenant's logistics to reach markets

Underground districts—**the Depths of St. Yerren** and **Warren-Under-Stone**—relate to surface geography in ways surface dwellers rarely comprehend. A Karst worker living beneath Ember Row might work in tunnels beneath the Furnaces, worship at St. Yerren's shrine beneath the Threshold, and maintain social connections with Karst whose chambers lie beneath entirely different surface addresses.

The **es-Etaie capitation arrangement** binds districts together beyond formal structures. Every Penitent worker benefits from the fiscal arrangement making religious practice free. An attack on es-Etaie would threaten every union member's religious freedom, which means every district has reason to defend the family's position regardless of other disagreements.
