## Part I: Violence Frameworks

### The Antediluvian Categories

The Antediluvians maintained distinctions between forms of violence that modern populations have collapsed into one. Understanding these categories explains why inherited weapons behave strangely and why certain conflicts escalated beyond all proportion.

**Contest** was what you waged against equalsâ€”other Aureate houses, rival lineages, enemies who remained peers within the eternal order regardless of enmity.

Contest had rules because both parties benefited from rules existing. Contest had limits because unlimited Contest would be indistinguishable from War, destroying the framework that gave both parties' existence meaning. When your civilization is eternal, when your house will exist forever, total victory is meaningless and total destruction is catastrophic. You cannot eliminate House X because House X will still exist in ten thousand years regardless of what you do today. Better to wound them, weaken them, take from them what the Contest's terms permit, and accept that they will do the same when positions reverse.

The wunderwaffen held by modern Colonels-Hereditary, the combat artificials in Ganati storageâ€”these were Contest weapons. Their peculiar limitations reflect constraints that made sense within frameworks combining elements we would distribute across war, law, sport, art, and ritual:

- **Limited capacity**: Maybe three shots before failure. Contest weapons were meant to be used sparingly.
- **Strange targeting**: Priorities that don't map to modern tactical logic. Some targets forbidden, others mandatoryâ€”reflecting legal restrictions, aesthetic conventions, or ritual requirements.
- **Authentication**: Contest weapons knew who was permitted to wield them. Only principals and designated champions.
- **Termination conditions**: Contest weapons recognized victory or defeat. They might cease operations when conditions are metâ€”conditions that mean nothing now.

**War** was what you waged to maintain orderâ€”counterinsurgency against inferiors, suppression of those who threatened the hierarchy. Force applied downward. The violence was extensive but shaped by assumptions about the target's inferior status.

War assumed the system was valid and the target was a deviation from it. War weapons were designed for force projection against populations that had lost their technological baseâ€”walking into colonies that had devolved to steam engines and gunpowder, not fighting peer adversaries.

**The Great Disagreement** between the Technical Castes and the Emperor was neither Contest nor War. Not Contest, because Contest was between equals and the Technical Castes were infrastructure. Not War, because the Technical Castes weren't rebellingâ€”they were refusing an impossible command. The Eighth Testament demanded mathematics they identified as false.

So both sides reached for capabilities that had never been used this way. Theorem-predators, consistency-dissolvers, architecture-rewriters. An attempt to change what was *possible* rather than what was *happening*. The "consumption" of star systems was collateral damage from a disagreement conducted at a level where physical reality was downstream of mathematical reality.

### Compulsion as Violence

**The Mechanism**: Compulsion susceptibility was engineered into all non-Highborn castes. The Highborn voice triggers the response; the response itself is in the listener. This matters: hierarchy is enforced through the bodies of the ruled, not the capabilities of the rulers.

Highborn children who discover the technique through experimentation almost always become monsters. They learn through testingâ€”commanding someone to do something and watching them obey against their will. Confirming that other people are not like them. That they are hollow.

**Dominion and Its Limits**: Bureau of the Lens doctrine holds that Compulsion carries bilateral obligation. You can only compel what someone is biologically capable of doing. Commanding beyond capability doesn't just failâ€”it damages both commander and commanded.

Case studies preserve warnings:
- An officer commanded impossible logistics completion in one hour. The aide attempted to comply, worked without stopping, suffered complete nervous breakdown.
- A merchant commanded a porter to break his own fingers in anger. He complied immediatelyâ€”three fingers before bystanders countermanded. The merchant was stripped of all commercial licenses.

Commanding another Highborn against their will is profound violationâ€”worse than commanding non-Highborn, because you violate someone who should be your equal. Many Highborn institutions require gags during meetings to maintain genuine negotiation.

### Who Fights

**The Warborn Problem**: The Warborn were never designed for what the modern Empire needs them to do.

Walking fortresses for suppressing populations, not conquering organized militaries. The entire Imperial military philosophy was built around calling down fire from heaven. The Interdict eliminated orbital capability. The Warborn remain, designed for a context that no longer exists.

Their forty-year cancer death was a feature, not a flaw. The Empire needed colonial enforcement that couldn't establish dynasties, that would naturally cycle out before they could entrench power, that would always remain dependent on Creche biotechnology for continuation.

The Hall of Mothers in ya-Sattra's Second Whorl produces nearly all Warborn through cloning. They're raised in cohorts (typically forty decanted together), trained together, deployed together. The relationships formed in those first years often last until death.

## Part IV: The Thousand Kingdoms

### Command Structure

**Traditional Warband Model**: A khan and whoever follows them. Size varies from a dozen warriors to several hundred. There's no clear rank structureâ€”authority flows from the khan's personal judgment about who commands respect. A warrior who's proven himself leads; a warrior who hasn't follows.

**Professional Reorganization**: Some khans have adopted Imperial-style organization. Khan Tsoka's warband (643 soldiers) is organized into seven companies: three destrier cavalry, two mixed infantry with rifles, one scouts and skirmishers, one logistics and support.

Written orders, salary payments (8 obols monthly for cavalry, 6 for infantry), merit-based promotion. His officers find this amusing but follow the system because it works: his supply trains don't get lost, his forces arrive when promised, his men don't die of preventable disease.

## Part VII: Violence as System

Violence in the setting operates through overlapping systems, each with its own logic:

**Contest logic** (inherited from Antediluvian frameworks): Ritualized, constrained, between acknowledged peers. Both parties benefit from rules existing. The Avouvar feuds preserve this most clearly.

**Hierarchical enforcement**: Force applied downward to maintain order. The Bureau of the Sword against rebels. The Hanged Men against tenants who don't pay. Compulsion against the commanded.

**Territorial accommodation**: Gendarmes and criminal organizations, the Chancery and feuding houses. Violence channeled into predictable patterns through negotiated boundaries. Not peace, but managed conflict.

**Fortune-as-justice**: The Akama framework where outcomes determine virtue. No revenge cycles because bad luck rather than enemies caused death.

**Desperate improvisation**: The Empire's actual procurement system. Criminal supply chains outperforming Imperial logistics. The miracle of every functional weapon.

### What the Setting Reveals

The Empire was designed for a context that no longer exists. Warborn for occupation after orbital bombardment. Contest weapons for peer conflicts in eternal civilizations. Compulsion for efficient hierarchy. None of these assumptions hold, but the systems persist.

Violence becomes maintenance of dysfunction. The Hanged Men protecting populations they exploit. The gendarmes selectively enforcing laws everyone knows don't apply equally. The Colonel-Hereditary hoarding weapons too valuable to risk. The Mutterers optimizing everything into scrap unless someone rings the right bell.

And yet it works. Not well. Not justly. Not sustainably. But the Empire persists, the Republic survives, the feuds continue, the protection rackets collect their tribute. Violence fills the gaps between what systems were designed for and what reality requires.

The architecture of the setting is violence made institutionalâ€”Contest constraints built into weapons, Compulsion circuits built into biology, fortune-as-justice built into philosophy. The inhabitants navigate systems designed by beings who computed all futures and chose this one. Whether those beings were right about what violence requires remains genuinely uncertain.
