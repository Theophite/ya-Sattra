## Core Principle: Optimized Distribution

Something sets prices that isn't human. The Presence optimizes for "collective transmission"—whatever that means—and the economy serves that optimization rather than individual preferences.

The software corporation worker is content but doesn't know why. She doesn't experience coercion, but she's not making fully autonomous choices either. The distinction between free market and managed system becomes impossible to draw.

External trade operates normally. Ezh Tan banking follows recognizable practices. The strangeness is invisible, operating through mechanisms that don't appear in ledgers.

**Historical parallel:** Algorithmic management taken to its logical conclusion—optimization by something genuinely superintelligent and genuinely benevolent by its own incomprehensible standards.

## Major Institutions

### Ezh Tan Banking (Representative Ya-Tsatsa Institution)

**What it does:** Four banks offering deposits, loans, currency exchange, and Northern Trade underwriting. The banks look normal from outside but make decisions that defy commercial logic.

**Cash flow in:**
- Deposit fees on approximately 2 million obols
- Loan interest: nominally 1-2% monthly
- Currency exchange fees
- Trade underwriting

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 300,000-500,000 obols from conventional operations.

**Cash flow out:**
- Staff: 420,000-540,000 obols/year
- Facilities: four locations
- **The apology-loans:** Loans approved for people who shouldn't qualify—emigrants the Presence has identified as needing to leave. These loans almost never get repaid. The bank absorbs losses that somehow don't cascade into consequences.

**Decision-making:** The banks are Ezh Tan family operations; family members make lending decisions. Except sometimes they don't. A banker reviews an application, recognizes it should be refused, and finds themselves approving it anyway. Their eyes go wet; they say things they've never thought: "Ya-Tsatsa isn't for everyone. There are emigrant communities all down the coast." The Presence works through them without compelling them.

Yen To, the current family head, has learned to distinguish genuine necessity (the Presence identifying someone who truly cannot thrive within ya-Tsatsa's constraints) from mere optimization (the Presence preferring an outcome that would make its existence marginally easier). He pushes back on the latter. Not always successfully, but consistently.

**Distinctive feature:** Many cannot operate in ya-Tsatsa because Many optimizes. Many would identify the apology-loan pattern and close it. The efficiency would break something the Presence requires. So the Ezh Tan refuse Many's repeated requests to establish full banking operations.

### Ya-Tsatsa Software Corporations (Representative)

**What it does:** Produces software products exported throughout the Empire—navigation systems, accounting tools, pattern-recognition algorithms. The corporations are real businesses making real products; what happens in the production process is less clear.

**Cash flow in:**
- Software licensing: 500,000-800,000 obols annually
- Maintenance contracts
- Consulting services

**Annual revenue:** Perhaps 800,000-1,200,000 obols.

**Cash flow out:**
- Worker wages: 2.1-2.7 million obols/year
- Facilities: corporation compounds with dormitories, cafeterias
- Equipment

**The arithmetic doesn't work.** Wages exceed revenue. Something subsidizes the difference.

**Decision-making:** Nominally, corporate leadership makes business decisions. In practice, the corporations exist to keep workers engaged in tasks that benefit the Presence's "collective transmission." The products are real and valuable; the process of creating them is what matters.

Workers report contentment they can't explain. The urge to leave that struck at twenty-two fades by twenty-five. They don't understand what the memory cores they maintain actually do.

**Distinctive feature:** The corporations are Presence infrastructure disguised as commerce. Bureau of the Coin inspectors have visited repeatedly and found nothing technically wrong. The patterns simply don't match any model they can construct.

## Household Profiles

### Household 12: Yenna Orel, Software Worker (Middle Class, Presence-Guided)

**Profile:** Near-baseline female, 28. Single, lives in corporation dormitory (mandatory). Works for Orbital Dynamics subsidiary.

**Monthly Income:** 400 obols

| Category | Amount | % |
|----------|--------|---|
| Housing (mandatory dormitory) | 80 | 20% |
| Food (mandatory cafeteria) | 100 | 25% |
| Clothing | 30 | 8% |
| Personal | 40 | 10% |
| Entertainment | 50 | 13% |
| Savings (theoretical) | 100 | 25% |

**Budget Balance:** Nominally positive by 100 obols/month. But savings never accumulate—unexpected expenses always appear. She has 40 obols saved after four years of work.

**Possessions:**
- Dormitory room (comfortable, corporation-provided)
- Quality clothing (corporation encourages presentability)
- Personal lector (gift from parents)
- Books
- Small collection of ceramic figurines (her hobby)

**Economic Anxieties:**
- She doesn't have any, and this bothers her sometimes
- Her parents worry that she never comes home
- At 22 she desperately wanted to leave; at 28 she's content
- She can't explain why the urge passed
- She maintains memory cores without understanding what they do

### Household 13: Yen To of the Ezh Tan (Wealthy, Presence-Adjacent)

**Profile:** Near-baseline male, 50s. Married, three adult children in the banking business. Head of the Ezh Tan family and senior partner of all four banks.

**Monthly Income:** 8,000-12,000 obols (spending authority within family resources)

| Category | Amount | % |
|----------|--------|---|
| Family compound | 0 | 0% |
| Staff | 300 | 3% |
| Food/household | 200 | 2% |
| Clothing/presentation | 150 | 2% |
| Social obligations | 400 | 4% |
| Religious observance | 200 | 2% |
| Children's establishments | 500 | 5% |
| Business entertainment | 600 | 6% |
| Apology-loan reserve | 2,000+ | 20%+ |
| Other | 5,000+ | varies |

**Budget Balance:** The family is wealthy by any standard—but substantial resources flow into loans that will never be repaid. Yen To approves these knowing they are losses, recognizing the Presence's guidance, and pushing back only on cases that feel like optimization rather than necessity.

**Possessions:**
- Family compound (centuries old, maintained impeccably)
- Bank facilities
- Art collection (restrained by ya-Tsatsa standards)
- Extensive library
- Multiple lectors
- Wardrobe appropriate to station

**Economic Anxieties:**
- The Presence. He knows it guides him. He's learned to distinguish genuine necessity from mere convenience.
- Many. The organism keeps requesting full banking rights. Yen To keeps refusing. He doesn't fully understand why, but he knows Many in ya-Tsatsa would break something essential.
- Succession. His children are competent, but can they hear the Presence? Will they push back when needed?
- The apology-loans. Each one is a loss. The pattern is essential. The contradiction is uncomfortable.
