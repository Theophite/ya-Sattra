# Notable Buildings in the Medina

These structures demonstrate the relationship between pre-Interdict construction and current occupation—architecture from before recorded history, adapted across geological timescales.

## The Processional Tower

> **Height:** 11 stories (originally taller—clean truncation at top)
> **Footprint:** Triangular, ~60 meters per side
> **Original function:** Ceremonial space for events whose nature is lost
> **Current:** Guild headquarters + ~400 residents on suspended platforms

**The material:** Grey pre-Interdict substrate showing no joints, no seams. Patina changes with light—almost blue at dawn, grey at noon, amber at sunset.

**The atrium:** Central space rising full height, originally empty. Now colonized by seven centuries of construction:
- Platforms suspended on cables anchored to galleries
- Bridges crossing the void at irregular heights
- A vertical neighborhood occupying processional emptiness

**The wear:**
- Main entrance threshold worn through entirely—8cm groove from feet across eras
- Gallery floors worn into channels following routes millions walked
- Handrails sculpted by touch into organic curves following human grip

**Current occupants:**
- **Processional Guild:** Ancient charter for "ceremonial conveyance." Now event coordination, diplomatic facilitation, institutional memory. Archives contain documentation from before the current calendar.
- **~400 residents:** Apartments ranging from cramped single rooms to multi-story compounds across multiple platforms. Children grow up crossing rope bridges over sixty-meter drops.

## The Lens-Grinders' Courtyard

> **Structure:** 7 pre-Interdict buildings on single foundation (~200m square)
> **Function:** Opticians' Guild headquarters, precision workshops, ~200 residents
> **Notable:** Calibration Hall with light that reveals invisible flaws

**The Calibration Hall:**
- Walls contain channels carrying illumination from unidentified sources
- Light differs subtly from natural illumination
- **Reveals imperfections invisible under ordinary conditions**
- No windows; interior stays lit through mechanisms operating continuously since before the Interdict
- Some people are affected badly (headaches, nausea, lasting changes to color perception)

**The Pattern Archive:**
- Walls covered in surfaces responding to touch
- Display patterns that might be text, images, or something else
- Patterns are meaningless to current occupants—no one can read them
- Patterns seem to respond to events (brightening during arguments, dimming during quiet work) but correlation doesn't survive systematic testing

**The Residential Blocks (three buildings, connected by bridges):**
- Climate control still functions—maintaining temperature regardless of external conditions
- But systems respond to occupancy patterns that no longer match current use
- Families learn building rhythms: which rooms warm in morning, which cool in afternoon

The es-Velan household has occupied the same apartment for eleven generations.

## The Stewards' Block

> **Size:** Most of a city block
> **Structure:** Single pre-Interdict construction with interior divisions functioning as separate buildings
> **Name origin:** Unknown—no "Stewards" organization in accessible records, yet name persists across thousands of years

**The exterior:** Familiar grey substrate, featureless except for accumulated signage, awnings, staircases.

**The interior walls:** Different—almost cream-colored, responding to touch with barely perceptible warmth. As if the building is alive and the walls are skin.

**Original proportions are wrong:**
- Ceilings too high, rooms too large for efficient residential use
- Original doorways stand **four meters tall**
- Current occupants added frames reducing them to human scale

**Navigation requires local knowledge:**
- "Third door past the warm wall"
- "Up the stairs that curve left"
- "Through the corridor where the ceiling drops"
- New residents get lost for weeks

**The upper levels (largely unmodified):**
- Vast rooms, equipment that might be furniture or machinery or art
- One family has maintained an apartment here for forty generations, claiming upper levels are healthier

## The Foundation Market

> **Structure:** Depression ~200m across, ~10m deep where surface subsided into infrastructure
> **Function:** Commerce built in exposed pre-Interdict foundations
> **Character:** Archaeological excavation as active commercial district

**The exposure revealed:**
- Substrate walls
- Equipment housings
- Conduit channels
- Structures whose purposes remain opaque

**The adaptation:**
- Commerce happens on platforms built across foundation structures
- In alcoves carved from substrate
- In tunnels extending into underlying infrastructure
- Equipment housings become anchors for awnings, walls for stalls

**The conduit channels became pathways:**
- Where pipes/cables once ran, merchants cleared space for foot traffic
- Covered passages through foundation infrastructure
- Stay cool in summer, warm in winter (residual climate control)
- Premium retail space

**The tunnels extend beyond the market:**
- Expeditions have followed conduits hundreds of meters
- Reaching collapsed sections, sealed doors, equipment that still operates
- Bureau of the Rod monitors boundaries

**What happens in active infrastructure is unclear:**
- The market receives power from somewhere—no one pays for it
- Occasionally lights dim throughout simultaneously (something drawing power)
- Occasionally sounds emerge from deeper tunnels—not mechanical but biological, or combining both

**The arrangement continues** because whatever persists in the foundations hasn't objected to commercial occupation of its upper levels. The market has operated for over a thousand years without catastrophic incident.
