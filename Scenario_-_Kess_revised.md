# Scenario: Kess

> **Type:** Ground-level survival
> **Location:** Iron Yards / Middens border
> **Focus:** The texture of marginal existence
> **Tone:** Melancholy without despair, exhaustion rather than fear

---

## What This Is

Not a plot. A life.

Kess is sixteen, half-Springheel, half-Tsatsan immigrant. She sleeps in a rope shed when she's welcome and rough when she's not. She runs courier jobs through the vertical spaces of the Rotten Quarter. She is trying to survive long enough to become something else, though she cannot name what that would be.

This scenario explores what survival looks like from inside. The small negotiations. The daily rhythm. The economics of subsistence when subsistence is the entire project.

---

## Kess

All arms and legs, the way some girls are at sixteen before their bodies figure out what they're becoming. Five-two, five-three, and most of that is limb. Pretty in an unfinished way—bones that might settle into striking, features that haven't caught up. Good teeth, which people notice because bad teeth are common in the Middens. Dark hair she cut herself, ragged.

Her father vanished before she was born. She has his legs, his calves that curve wrong, his tendons visible beneath skin, his six-foot vertical leap. Pure-line Springheels can clear twelve feet standing. Kess is half that inheritance, half something else. Enough to take routes baseline couriers cannot. Enough to feel her calves absorb the landing the way they were built to.

Her mother came from ya-Tsatsa, or somewhere adjacent. Ended up alone in Vetch-Rise. Didn't come home when Kess was eleven.

Kess has her mother's name. She doesn't know what it means.

### What She Remembers

Her mother sang sometimes. Tsatsan songs, words Kess didn't understand even then. The melody stays. She catches herself humming it on high routes, the city spread out below, before she realizes what she's doing.

Her mother taught her to count money. To recognize the signs that meant trouble. To move through crowds without drawing attention. Survival skills disguised as games. Kess didn't understand at the time that her mother was preparing her for the possibility of being alone.

The room on the second floor of Vetch-Rise had a window that faced east. She remembers the light in the mornings.

She doesn't go to that floor anymore.

### What She Thinks About

Routes. The map in her head is three-dimensional. She thinks in vertical distance as naturally as horizontal. When she's bored, she plans paths she'll never run, optimizes routes through imaginary obstacles.

Food. Where to get it, how much it costs, whether today is an eating day. The awareness of hunger that never quite goes away.

The people she's attached to. Whether Silk is okay. Whether Pek is performing again. Whether the Venns need anything from her.

Not the future. The future is an abstraction she cannot afford.

### What She Wants (That She Won't Say)

A door.

The way Pek talks about ya-Tsatsa—a place he could go, a life that exists in potential even if he never takes it. Kess has no doors. Not Venn enough for the sewer-fishers. Not baseline enough for the Yen Tams. Not documented enough for guild work.

She is exactly where she is, with no exits visible.

She doesn't articulate this. It would hurt too much to name.

### The Knife

Cheap iron blade, wrapped handle. She found it in a salvage pile when she was thirteen.

The first time: a man in Vetch-Rise who followed her into a stairwell. She showed the knife. He left. Simple.

The second time: night, Knife-Edge territory, someone who didn't leave when she showed it. She remembers the movement of her arm, the resistance, the sound. She doesn't remember running, but she must have run. She never went back to check.

She doesn't think of herself as someone who killed a person. She thinks of herself as someone who did what was necessary.

The distinction matters to her. She couldn't explain why.

## The Day

### Morning

She wakes in the rope shed if she's there, or wherever she slept if she's not. The shed is better—dry, secure, smells of hemp and machine oil. The Venns don't charge her. They don't guarantee access either. If someone needs the space for actual rope work, she's out.

Breakfast is optional. If she has coin, she buys something from the Transient Market vendors in Vetch-Rise. A rice ball, a skewer of something, tea if it's cold. If she doesn't have coin, she waits.

She checks for work at three places:

**Ethira Morir's workshop** deep in the Arcades, near the Sluice—the corridor where rotated architecture opens onto the tidal canal separating the Ridge from Penthouse Block. A cramped space in a converted elevator maintenance room, floor laid across what was a wall. Ethira is quiet, professional-class name, pays reliably. Two to three obols per run depending on distance. The work is irregular—sometimes three jobs in a day, sometimes nothing for a week. Kess carries packages she doesn't open to addresses she doesn't question. Ethira doesn't explain. Kess doesn't ask.

**The Yen Tam contact point** at the Seven Branches. Less legitimate. Pays better—four to five obols for difficult routes. The work is carrying things she doesn't look at to places she doesn't ask about. They use her because she's fast and because her mother's blood makes her marginally trustworthy.

**Word of mouth** through Venn contacts. Someone needs something moved vertically. Someone needs a message carried through routes that require climbing. The Venns take a cut, but they vouch for her, which matters.

### Midday

She runs. The Springheel inheritance shows here—six-foot jumps, vertical shortcuts, routes through the three-dimensional maze of the Rotten Quarter that baseline couriers cannot take. She knows the catwalks and cable-crossings, the Venn-built shortcuts that save twenty minutes of walking if you're willing to climb.

The work is physical. By midday she's covered miles, much of it vertical. She eats when she can. Street food, whatever's cheapest, calories over taste.

Between runs she might stop at the Yellow Glass to see Silk. The bathhouse is warm and the light is golden and Silk will talk to her like she matters. These visits are short. Kess doesn't have money for bathing, and loitering draws attention.

### Evening

If there's coin, she eats properly. A bowl of something hot from one of the Torchline Row stalls. Sits somewhere with a view of foot traffic. Watches people. Processes the day.

If Pek is around, they might talk. He finds her, usually—she doesn't go looking for him at the Seven Branches. They sit somewhere the Yen Tams won't see them being too friendly. He complains about his work. She listens. Sometimes he talks about ya-Tsatsa, the holy city, like it's a door he could walk through.

She doesn't tell him she has no doors.

### Night

Back to the rope shed if it's available. If not, she knows places. A corner of Vetch-Rise where the patrols don't check. A maintenance alcove in Hollow-Egress where the Loaks tolerate her presence. Worse options if those aren't available.

She doesn't sleep well. She never has.

## The Economics

### Income

**Courier rates:**
- Routine delivery (Medina, short distance): 2 obols
- Difficult route (vertical work, bad neighborhoods): 4-5 obols
- Rift work (smuggling, no questions): 6 obols
- Tip for standing straight and asking properly: 0.5-1.5 obols extra

**Weekly income:**
- Good week: 30-40 obols (six to eight runs, some tips)
- Average week: 20-25 obols (four to five runs)
- Bad week: 10-15 obols (work dried up, or injured)

**Monthly:** 80-120 obols at average. Below 60 is destitution.

### Expenses

**Food (street prices):**
- Basic meal (noodles, skewers, rice ball): 1/8-1/4 obol
- Large portion or better quality: 1/4 obol
- Seated meal at a tavern: 1/2-1 obol
- Beer: 1/16 obol
- Coca tea: 1/8 obol
- Bowl deposit at Mir Kee's: 1/4 obol (returned when the bowl comes back)

**Daily food cost:** 1.5-2 obols if eating properly (three street meals). 45-60 obols per month.

**Housing:**
- Rope shed (Venn tolerance): 0 obols
- Bunkhouse (Shale Rest or similar): 5 obols per week (20-22 per month)
- Cheap private room: 35-50 obols per month
- Decent housing: 80-120 obols per month

**Clothing:**
- Unlicensed shirt: 1-1.5 obols (wears out in months)
- Guild shirt: 3-4 obols (lasts years)
- Complete outfit, unlicensed: 4-6 obols
- Complete outfit, guild: 12-18 obols
- Courier's satchel (essential equipment): 12 obols to replace

**Other:**
- Small knife: 2-3 obols
- Soap, candles, small necessities: 10-15 obols per month if you're not careful

### Parish Capitation

The official rate is 160 obols per year. Forty obols quarterly, documented, stamped, filed with the Bureau of the Coin. At Valve-Saint, the actual cost is perhaps twelve—subsidized through arrangements Curate Aram maintains with wealthier congregations, recorded as carefully as any debt.

Aram would sponsor her. He's done it before—found ways to document the undocumentable, to make the Church's parallel records catch people the Bureau refuses to see. He would ask her name, her mother's name, what parish her mother attended in ya-Tsatsa if any. He would find a way to make it work.

Kess does not know this.

She knows that systems exist to process people like her into categories she doesn't fit. She knows that asking for help reveals vulnerability. She knows that forty obols is half a month's income and 160 is impossible and either number might as well be a wall.

She does not know that some walls have doors. She is sixteen and her mother disappeared when she was eleven and no one has ever explained to her how institutions might bend. The Venns are kind but they are not churched. Pek is Yen Tam, and the Yen Tams handle their own. Silk would help, but Silk doesn't understand systems any better than Kess does.

So she remains uncounted. Not because the count refuses her, but because she cannot imagine asking to be counted.

### The Math

At average income (80-100 obols per month):
- Food: 45-60 obols (50-60%)
- Housing: 0 (rope shed)
- Clothing and miscellany: 15-20 obols
- **Margin: 0-25 obols**

At good income (110-120 obols per month):
- Same expenses, margin grows to 30-50 obols
- This is when Kess replaces worn shoes, eats meat, saves a little

At bad income (50-70 obols per month):
- Food drops to 30-40 obols (skipping meals)
- Clothing deferred
- **Margin: negative**—she's eating into nothing, sleeping rough, falling behind

The difference between surviving and not surviving is about 20 obols a month. One bad week. One injury. One job that doesn't come through.

### What Moving Up Would Cost

**Guild registration:** Impossible without caste documentation she doesn't have

**Legitimate employment:** Requires references she cannot provide

**Apprenticeship fee:** 50-200 obols depending on trade—even if someone would take her

**A room of her own:** 35-50 obols per month minimum, which would eat her entire margin

She's not trying to solve these problems. She's trying to survive long enough that something changes.

## The People

### The Venns

The sewer-fisher clan lets her use their space. They are kind about it. This is not formal fostering—they cannot take on another mouth to feed—but it's not coldness either. They like her. They want her to be okay. They cannot make her one of them without resources they don't have.

**Keras Venn** (45, ropemaker): Teaches her knots when work is slow. Gives her jobs when he has them, pays fair. Worries about her in ways he doesn't say directly.

**Ressa Venn** (28, forewoman): Respects Kess's climbing ability. Has used her for difficult installations. Would advocate for formal fostering if the clan could afford it.

**Sara Venn** (16): Closest thing Kess has to a peer among the Venns. Pure-line sewer-fisher with exceptional spatial mathematics. They talk sometimes. Sara shares food when she has extra.

The rope shed is the best they can do. Kess knows this. She is grateful for it.

### Ethira Morir

Quiet. Forty, maybe forty-five. Professional-class name—the -ir suffix marks guild training, though she doesn't talk about what she trained for. Brown hair going gray at the temples, kept practical. Hands that move with precision. A face that has learned not to show what it's thinking.

She works from a cramped workshop deep in the Arcades, near the Sluice—the corridor at the far end where rotated architecture opens onto the tidal canal. The space was once an elevator maintenance room; now it's horizontal, the floor laid across what was a wall, orb-fixtures casting light from the wrong surfaces. Salvage pieces line the walls. Technical instruments Kess doesn't recognize. The faint hum of something running that probably shouldn't be.

Ethira does assessment. When strange things come up from Lift-3—pre-Interdict remnants with functions, Technical Caste artifacts the clans don't understand—she evaluates them. Determines what's real, what's functional, what's valuable. Her Companion training lets her see what others can't. If she says a piece is good, it's good. Sixty percent of Asaph es-Tsay's collection is forgeries. None of the pieces that came through Ethira.

She knows the right people to broker the actual sales. Mesca Galen at Ridge-Line Scriptoria, who runs three intelligence networks and can detect lies. Asaph es-Tsay in his Vermillion Tower, who failed his examination on Doctrinal Conformity and spends his allowance on artifacts he half-understands. The Aureate collector market. The information traders. The quiet economy of things that shouldn't exist.

What Kess doesn't know:

Ethira was a Companion. Trained in the Guild—the harmonic frequencies, the cascade interruption, the protocols for managing Mutterers. She did a two-year rotation at the Silent Song, the kalbat monastery in the Second Whorl. Absolute silence. Padded floors. Seven living records in cyclic dormancy, their slack-jawed faces like the carvings on ya-Tsatsa's cliffs.

Something happened there. She doesn't talk about it. She left the Guild under circumstances that would interest the Bureau of the Rod.

In a converted storage chamber deeper in the Arcades, she runs a choir. Unregistered Mutterers—"wild," the Guild would call them. Six of them, living in the rotated spaces, maintaining equipment the workshop owners cannot maintain themselves. She knows the chimes that keep them from cascading. She knows the frequencies that let distributed consciousness cohere.

She is breaking laws that would send her to the Chain-Men. She is gentle, and she is ruthless about secrets, and she pays Kess two to three obols per run without ever explaining what she's paying for.

To Kess, she is a quiet woman in a cramped workshop who pays on time.

### Pek Yen Tam

Sixteen. Near-baseline, Tsatsan. Bad mustache he shouldn't be trying to grow—patchy, darker on one side than the other. He won't shave it because shaving it would mean admitting it's not working.

He says he likes violence. He says he likes the life. He talks about the work like it fits him.

It doesn't. He's too soft. His hands shake after jobs that require hurting people, and he hides them in his pockets, and Kess notices.

He treats Kess like a person. This costs him with his own people. The Yen Tams are near-baseline chauvinists; befriending a half-Springheel marks him as soft. He does it anyway.

**His family:** Parents run a small tea stall in the Iron Yards market. Legitimate. They don't know the details of what he does for the Yen Tams, or they pretend not to. His younger sister is twelve and thinks he's important. He can't look at her after certain jobs.

**His position:** Low-level. Running numbers, carrying messages, occasionally collecting debts from people who can't fight back. The Yen Tams are evaluating him. He knows he's being tested. He knows what passing the tests would require.

**What he actually likes:** Card games—the mathematics of probability, not the gambling. Tsatsan food his mother makes. Walking through the market in early morning before the crowds. Talking to Kess because she doesn't require him to perform.

**What he pretends to like:** Fighting. Intimidation. The respect that comes from being feared. He's watched the older Yen Tam boys and learned to imitate their posture, their expressions. The imitation is good enough to fool people who aren't paying attention.

It doesn't fool Kess.

**Ya-Tsatsa:** He's never been there. His grandparents came to ya-Sattra before his parents were born. But he's heard the stories—the white-gray streets, the fog that never lifts, the Presence everyone feels and no one names. He imagines it as a place where he could be someone else. Where the performance wouldn't be necessary because the thing he's performing wouldn't be valued.

He talks about it with Kess sometimes. "Maybe I'll just go," he says.

He never will. The door is real for him in a way it isn't for her, and he still won't walk through it. This is something Kess understands about him that he doesn't understand about himself.

**With Kess:** They sit on a maintenance platform above Torchline Row, legs dangling. He complains about the work. She listens. He doesn't ask about her work because asking would mean acknowledging she works for the same people. They talk about nothing—the food stalls, the weather, a fight they saw. The nothing is the point. It's the only time either of them gets to be sixteen instead of a survivor.

### Silk

Nineteen. Bathhouse attendant at the Yellow Glass. Half-Gilded Petal, half something else—her father was near-baseline, she thinks. She never knew him.

The Petal shows in her face: too symmetric, gold-tinted skin, features that draw the eye. Not as pronounced as pure-line—she can pass for baseline if people aren't looking closely—but visible. Beautiful in a way that makes certain people look twice, and not always safely.

She keeps making bad decisions because the architecture that would make her careful is attenuated. The Gilded Petals were built from Sickly genes—stock engineered without the capacity for negative emotion. Silk has half that inheritance. She trusts people she shouldn't. She walks toward danger she cannot feel.

She knows what she is, kind of. The part of her that would care is exactly the part that's missing.

**Her work:** Sweeping, scrubbing, restocking linens, heating water, occasionally attending to clients who need something fetched. She's good at the work—attentive, pleasant, never complains. The Yen Tams who oversee the bathhouse like her.

This is not as safe as she thinks it is.

She lives in a corner of the storage room, curtained off with canvas. It's warm and dry and she's grateful for it. The gratitude is genuine. The inability to see the strings attached is the problem.

**Her history:** Mother was full Gilded Petal, worked in a wealthy household in the Medina until she died when Silk was eleven. Silk doesn't remember her clearly—soft hands, a sweet smell, a voice that made everything seem fine. After that: the Bureau of the Creche, briefly. A processing center for orphans of illegal castes. Then the bathhouse, arranged by someone she never met. She doesn't know who pays for her documentation or why. She doesn't think to ask.

**What she's like:** Warm. Genuinely kind. She remembers details about people—their names, their preferences, the things they mentioned in passing. She asks Kess how her day was and listens to the answer. She shares food when she has extra, offers her bath time to Kess even though it would cost her. The kindness is real. The inability to protect herself from people who would exploit it is also real.

**The bad decisions:** Lending money she can't afford to lose. Trusting a client who said he'd help her find better work. Letting someone store packages in the bathhouse without asking what was in them. Each decision made perfect sense at the time. The pattern only becomes visible from outside.

**With Kess:** They meet in the golden light of the Yellow Glass, steam rising from the baths, the sound of water and voices. Silk talks about small things—a client who told a funny story, a recipe she wants to try, a dream she had. Kess listens. Sometimes Kess warns her about something—a person to avoid, a situation that seems wrong—and Silk nods and agrees and then forgets, or doesn't quite feel the weight of the warning.

Kess keeps warning her anyway.

Silk thinks Kess worries too much. Kess thinks Silk can't worry enough.

They're both right.

**What Kess understands:** Silk isn't stupid. The Gilded Petal inheritance didn't damage her intelligence. It damaged her threat detection. She can analyze a situation correctly if you explain it to her, but she cannot feel the alarm that would make her analyze it in the first place. The danger is invisible to her until it's too late, and then she's confused about how she got there.

Kess cannot protect her from everything. Kess cannot protect her from most things. But Kess watches, and warns, and hopes the warning sticks.

Sometimes it does. Mostly it doesn't.

### The Three of Them

Pek and Silk know each other through Kess, but they're not close. Pek is uncomfortable around Silk—she's too trusting, too open, and he knows what his people do to people like her. Being near her reminds him of things he'd rather not think about. Silk likes Pek, of course. She likes everyone. She doesn't notice his discomfort.

Kess is the hinge. She moves between them, carries concern in both directions. She doesn't tell Pek about Silk's vulnerabilities because he might be required to exploit them someday. She doesn't tell Silk about Pek's struggles because Silk would try to help and make everything worse.

The three of them together are rare—maybe once a month, a stolen hour on Torchline Row, sharing street food. Those hours are the closest thing any of them has to normal. They talk about nothing. They laugh at things that aren't that funny. For a little while, the machinery of their lives stops grinding.

Then Pek has to go back to the Seven Branches. Silk has to go back to the bathhouse. Kess has to go back to running routes. The machinery starts again.

What Kess knows: This won't last. Pek's performance will become real or it will break him. Silk's blindness will walk her into something she can't walk out of. The margins they all survive in are shrinking. Something will happen.

She doesn't know what. She doesn't know when. She just knows the shape of it, the way you know a storm is coming before you see the clouds.

## The Pressures

These are not plot. They are background weight.

**The Yen Tams** use Kess and will continue to use her. She's valuable as a courier, disposable as a person. The work pays well enough to be worth the association, dangerous enough to carry risk.

**The Venns** care about her, but caring is not capacity. They're a small clan with limited resources. If times get harder—if salvage work dries up, if someone gets injured, if the clan's margins tighten—the rope shed might be needed for something else. Not because they want her gone. Because survival math doesn't account for kindness.

**Pek** is performing a self he doesn't believe in. His hands shake after bad jobs. His sister thinks he's important. The Yen Tams are watching him, testing him, waiting to see if he'll become what they need. Eventually something will force him to choose between the performance and the truth. Kess doesn't know what that will look like, but she knows it's coming.

**Silk** cannot feel the danger she's in. Working at a Yen Tam business, half-Gilded Petal, no protector, documentation arranged by someone she's never met—she's vulnerable in ways she doesn't register. She lends money she can't afford, trusts people who don't deserve it, accepts arrangements without asking who benefits. Kess warns her when she can. The warnings don't always stick. Eventually something will happen. Kess will see it coming before Silk does. Whether seeing it will be enough is another question.

**The body** Kess doesn't think about. The one that didn't get up. Someone might come looking. Someone might connect that night to her. Probably not. But the possibility exists, and Kess carries it.

These pressures don't have deadlines. They're the weather of her life.

## The Places

### The Rope Shed

Ground level, Iron Yards side of Venn territory. A converted maintenance alcove built into the quarried bedrock, where the Middens' flooded foundations give way to solid stone.

**The space:** Ten feet by twelve, maybe. Coils of cable stacked on wooden racks along the walls—dock lines thick as Kess's wrist, surgical sutures fine as hair, everything in between. Anchor equipment. Splice tools. The smell of hemp and machine oil and the faint salt damp that gets into everything this close to the water.

There's a door that locks from inside. This matters more than anything else about the place.

**Kess's corner:** A bedroll wedged between two cable racks, hidden from casual view. A canvas bag with everything she owns—a change of clothes, a wrapped bundle of coins, a comb with missing teeth, a wooden toggle her mother gave her. The knife stays on her person.

**The sounds:** The Venn ropeworks hum through the walls. Footsteps on the walkway outside. Water, always water—the slap of it against the Middens foundations, the drip of condensation, the gurgle in pipes she can't see.

**What it feels like:** Safety. The closest thing to home she has. When the door is locked and she's wrapped in her bedroll and the ropeworks have gone quiet for the night, she can almost believe nothing can reach her here.

Sometimes she lies awake and maps routes in her head. Sometimes she hums her mother's song, so quiet no one could hear. Sometimes she just listens to the water and waits for sleep.

### Vetch-Rise

The gateway tenement. Twenty-five hundred people in a building that connects the Middens to the rest of the city—the only pedestrian route in or out.

**The structure:** Pre-Interdict foundations on the eastern edge, where flooded Middens meets quarried Iron Yards bedrock. The building spans the gap like a bridge, listing slightly toward the water. Eight floors of corridors that smell of cooking and mold and too many bodies. Yen Tam markers at ground level—everyone entering or leaving on foot passes their checkpoints.

**Ground floor:** Chaos. The Transient Market materializes in the corridors wherever Chain-Men aren't currently patrolling. Vendors with their entire inventories in hand-carts, ready to move in seconds. Rice balls wrapped in leaves. Skewers of meat that might be rat or might be fish. Tea from urns that have been boiling since morning. Kess knows which stalls are reliable and which will make her sick.

**Second floor, eastern side:** Where her mother lived. Kess doesn't go there. The room is occupied by someone else now—has been for five years. She's seen their laundry drying on the walkway. She takes the western stairs.

**The checkpoint:** Yen Tam boys at the ground floor entrance, watching who comes and goes. They know Kess by sight—she's a courier, she's half-Tsatsan, she belongs to the margins they control. They let her pass without trouble. This is not friendliness. This is inventory.

**What Kess uses it for:** Food, when she has coin. Transit, when she needs to reach the Iron Yards from the Middens interior. Avoiding, when she can. The building holds too much of her history.

### The Yellow Glass Bathhouse

Torchline Row, elevated above the Iron Yards basin. The building is old—pre-Interdict glass panels along the upper walls filter everything to amber-gold.

**The approach:** Stone stairs climbing the quarried ridge from the Yards floor. The air gets slightly better as you climb—the smoke and metal dust settle in the basin below. By the time you reach Torchline Row, you can almost breathe without tasting industry. The bathhouse sits midway along the Row, its yellow windows glowing even in daylight.

**Inside:** Steam. The immediate, enveloping warmth of it. Coal smoke from the boilers mixing with the smell of carbolic disinfectant and wet wool and human bodies. The light is golden, filtering through those ancient glass panels, making everything look softer than it is.

Communal baths in the main room—workers washing off shift grime, laborers too poor for private housing, radiation-exposed needing decontamination. The water is hot. The water is always hot. Tomorrow's shift comes regardless.

Sleeping platforms along the walls, curtained with canvas. Forty obols per night—more than Kess earns in some days. A place for workers between shifts, for people with nowhere else to go.

**Silk's corner:** Storage room in the back, behind the boilers. Warm from the heat bleeding through the walls. A canvas curtain for privacy. Cleaning supplies stacked around a sleeping pallet. It's not much, but it's hers.

**What Kess does here:** Sits with Silk in the amber light. Talks about nothing. Watches the steam rise and the workers come and go. Lets herself be warm for an hour. Doesn't pay for bathing—can't afford it—but Silk sometimes sneaks her a basin of hot water and a rag when no one's watching.

The Yen Tams own this place. Kess never forgets that, even when the light is golden.

### The Seven Branches

Yen Tam gambling house on Torchline Row. Converted forge building, soot-stained ceiling from decades of fire.

**The exterior:** Nothing marks it as what it is. A door in a stone wall, heavy timber, no sign. Everyone who needs to know, knows. Kess passes it on courier routes and doesn't look too long.

**Inside:** Oil lamps creating pools of amber light, corners staying dark. Timber tables where serious players study seven-sided dice and Testament-suit cards. This isn't rowdy gambling—the games are religious observance, Tsatsan faith made into mathematics. The house is quiet. The stakes are real.

**The shrine:** Seven brass plates arranged in an open octagon, inscribed with Jargon notation. The eighth point is a mirror, reflecting the room back at itself. Incense burning. The Presence of the Oracles invoked even here, even in a gambling house in the Iron Yards.

**The atmosphere:** Dangerous. Everyone here knows the Yen Tams are watching. Those who cheat are not seen again. Those who cannot pay work off debts in Yen Tam operations—forever, if the debt is large enough. The mathematics are designed to trap.

**Where Pek works:** The back rooms, usually. Running numbers between the floor and the accountants. Carrying messages. Learning the business that will either make him or break him.

**What Kess does here:** Picks up courier jobs from the contact desk near the entrance. Takes packages to places she doesn't ask about, delivers messages to people she doesn't question. She doesn't linger. The shrine watches everything, and she knows she doesn't belong.

### Knife-Edge

The leaning tenement. Fifteen degrees off vertical, visible from anywhere in the Middens.

**The structure:** Pre-Interdict foundations shifted but held—structural failure arrested, never corrected. The building leans like a drunk against the bedrock, its eastern edge where the flooded Middens gives way to the Iron Yards' quarried stone. Forge smoke drifts from upper floors where salvage networks process metal.

Sixteen hundred people live at an angle. Furniture shimmed. Floors sloped. Residents develop the tilted gait, walking crooked even on level ground.

**The atmosphere:** Less water here than deep Middens—you're at the transition zone, almost to legitimate industry. More heat and smoke instead. The clang of metalwork from a dozen small forges.

**What happened here:** A night Kess doesn't think about. An alley off the seventh floor, tilted like everything else. Someone who didn't leave when she showed the knife.

She doesn't go to Knife-Edge anymore. Not for any job. Not for any reason.

### The Vertical Routes

Not a place. A way of moving.

**The network:** Catwalks strung between tenement roofs. Cable-crossings the Venns maintain for their own use. Maintenance ladders bolted to pre-Interdict infrastructure. Gaps that require jumping. Handholds that require trusting.

The Rotten Quarter is three-dimensional. Most people move through it on the ground level, threading corridors and crossing bridges. Kess moves through the space above—the routes that only Springheels and sewer-fishers can navigate.

**What it feels like:** Freedom. The only time she feels fully herself. The city opens up when you can move through it vertically—you see the patterns from above, the flow of people and goods, the way the districts fit together. The Middens' flooded foundations, the Iron Yards' quarried basin, the Ridge rising above it all.

Six-foot jumps. Her calves absorb the landing, her tendons taking the shock they were built to take. She calculates trajectories without thinking about it, the spatial processing that came with her father's blood.

**The Venn-built shortcuts:** Thirty unauthorized crossings, according to clan gossip. Rope bridges and cable runs that shave twenty minutes off walking routes, if you're willing to climb. The Venns don't charge her for using them—she's done work for the clan, and Keras vouches for her. This is worth more than money.

**The danger:** The tendon failure that pure-line Springheels fear. The lines that fray without warning. The platform that wasn't anchored properly. One mistake at height is the only mistake you make.

Kess doesn't think about the danger while she's running. The thinking happens before, when she's planning routes. Once she's moving, there's only the movement—the next handhold, the next jump, the next landing. Her body knows what to do. Her mind gets out of the way.

### The Maintenance Platform

Above Torchline Row. A structural remnant from some pre-Interdict system no one understands anymore—a metal grid bolted to the quarried ridgeline, accessible only by climbing.

**The place:** Maybe twenty feet by ten, rusted but solid. Rails along the edge that Kess doesn't trust. A view of the Iron Yards spreading below, the forge-fires and salvage operations, the workers moving like insects between the pits.

**What it's for:** Nothing, now. Whatever system it once served is long dead. The Venns know about it but don't use it—too exposed, no practical value.

**What Kess uses it for:** Meeting Pek. The one place they can sit together without anyone seeing. High enough that the Yen Tams don't patrol here. Quiet enough that they can talk.

They sit with their legs dangling over the edge. They share street food when there's money for it. They talk about nothing—the stalls, the weather, a fight they saw.

The nothing is the point.

Sometimes Pek talks about ya-Tsatsa. The white-gray streets. The fog.

Sometimes they just sit in silence and watch the fires below.
