## Command Structure

**The Institute of Auxiliary Relations** manages relationships with Akama vassal bands—a diplomatic role as much as military administration.

**Colonels-Hereditary** hold territory at the Empire's northern edge, claiming authority over populations they cannot actually control. These estates survive through managing flows of goods, information, and military capacity between Imperial core territories and Thousand Kingdoms groups.

*Colonel-Hereditary es-Valent* controls trade routes generating forty thousand obols annually through "safe passage fees." Her "tribute presentations" are actually diplomatic summits where policy is negotiated under feudal ceremony.

*Colonel-Hereditary es-Tammuz* commands perhaps eighty soldiers garrisoning a fortified estate. His relationship with local Akama bands operates through elaborate fictions—band leaders visit quarterly for "tribute presentations" that are negotiations among rough equals.

*Colonel-Hereditary es-Verath* commands only forty soldiers. She survives by providing services: neutral ground for negotiations, arbitration that both Imperial law and Akama custom recognize. Her power rests on being too useful to attack, too weak to threaten.

### Military Brokerage

When the Bureau of the Sword requires northern auxiliaries—for border suppression, punitive expeditions, or grinding campaigns—the request flows through intermediaries who can actually mobilize Akama forces. The Akama despise Highborn as a matter of cultural identity; they won't answer calls from Imperial Aureates. They will answer calls from respected intermediaries who can negotiate terms: payment in coin and plunder, guarantees about treatment, promises that Akama dead will be honored.

## Who Fights

### Garrison Troops

Eighty soldiers holding an estate that claims authority over thousands. Near-baselines with cavalry modifications—improved balance, hip structure optimized for riding. Competent horsemanship, adequate marksmanship.

### Vassal Band Categories

*Legitimist Bands* maintain Imperial allegiance as core identity. They preserve ceremonies, maintain archives, treat vassalage as sacred obligation passed down through generations. The Khan Sevak band (643 soldiers) exemplifies this category—operating with Imperial discipline while maintaining Akama cultural practices.

*Pragmatic Vassals* acknowledge Imperial authority when beneficial and ignore it when inconvenient. The Torek Confederation (300 warriors) maintains nominal vassalage to es-Valent because commercial advantages exceed tribute costs. Their vassalage is transactional contract, not political subordination.

*Opportunistic Groups* drift between Imperial vassalage and Thousand Kingdoms independence based on immediate tactical considerations. The Khen Descent appears as Imperial vassals during harvest season, then operates as independent warband during winter.

*Nominal Vassals* use Imperial cover for activities the Empire officially cannot sanction. The Vess Raiders (120 warriors) maintain nominal vassalage so es-Tammuz can claim they're his auxiliary forces when Imperial officials inquire about raiding.

## Characteristic Equipment and Procurement

Garrison troops receive equipment through Colonel-Hereditary private markets rather than official Bureau channels. Quality depends on the family's wealth and relationships. es-Valent's garrison has modern rifles and good horses; es-Verath's has older equipment maintained carefully.

Auxiliary equipment comes from the bands themselves—whatever they've captured, traded for, or inherited. The Institute of Auxiliary Relations provides ammunition and occasionally heavy weapons as part of tribute negotiations, but auxiliaries are expected to equip themselves.

## What Military Life Is Like

### Garrison Life

The eighty soldiers holding an estate learn the polite fictions quickly. "Tribute presentations" are actually diplomatic negotiations. "Border patrols" are shows of force. "Bandit suppression" is protecting caravans who paid the right fees. Competent horsemanship and adequate marksmanship matter less than looking impressive during ceremonies. The pay is better than regular service, the duties lighter, the politics exhausting.

Young Highborn officers assigned to frontier estates—usually minor family branches, no patents of their own, placed here because family provides for family—learn uncomfortable truths. Their Compulsion doesn't work on Akama. The psychological substrate is wrong; the harmonics don't trigger the right responses. Their authority rests on being useful, not powerful. The sixty-year-old sergeant who commands the actual garrison knows more about keeping everyone alive than anything the academy taught.

### Legitimist Identity

In bands like Khan Sevak's, Imperial allegiance is sacred obligation. Soldiers keep copies of service contracts in oiled leather—written orders, fixed pay, proper documentation. The Empire may be far away, but the forms matter. Fathers served Sevak; sons will serve Sevak's heir. The bond is religious in a way that pragmatic vassals find incomprehensible.
