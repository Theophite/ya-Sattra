## Command Structure

The Avouvar organize military forces by house rather than centralized command. Each house maintains fighters equipped from autofactory production and inherited arsenals.

### House Forces

Vesztraëlin and Droëvan each field several hundred fighters equipped with autofactory-produced alloys, conventional firearms, and fixed railgun emplacements. House forces answer to their house leadership—matriarchs, patriarchs, or elder councils depending on the house; coordination between houses happens through Chancery precedent rather than unified command.

### The Feud Structure

The millennia-long Vesztraëlin-Droëvan conflict operates under Chancery constraints—certain acts prohibited (killing children, destroying infrastructure), certain targets protected, violence channeled into forms both houses recognize. The architecture of dispute keeps conflict contained while ensuring it continues.

### The Chancery

The Chancery is not a court of law. It offers precedent—incredibly long, incredibly precise chains of precedent stretching back millennia. When houses submit disputes, they ask whether any prior case establishes resolution. The process is voluntary, but the alternative is feud without constraint.

### The Chancellor's Veto

The Chancellor cannot impose peace, but can define boundaries of acceptable violence. A house violating a veto faces unified opposition from every other house in the city.

## Who Fights

### House Fighters

Several hundred per major house, equipped with autofactory-produced alloys and conventional firearms.

### Fixed Emplacements

The garrison district's railguns—too heavy for individual Avouvar to carry—become fixed positions crewed by three-person teams. During active phases of the feud, these emplacements trade fire across disputed corridors, punching holes through structures the autofactory repairs within weeks.

### Unusable Inheritance

Power armor stands in racks, sized for Warborn frames. The armor would require extensive modification to fit Avouvar bodies—modification no one knows how to perform. Occasionally a house attempts activation; occasionally someone dies. The armor waits for soldiers who will never return.

## Characteristic Equipment and Procurement

### Autofactory Terminals

Autofactory terminals operate according to schedules the Medials direct. No one else can access these schedules or predict when a terminal will activate. A terminal might run for three years producing armor alloys, then fall silent for a decade.

The garrison district contains the military-relevant terminals:
- Vesztraëlin terminals: armor alloys (the hardest materials available anywhere), pressure-rated tubes, volatile compounds
- Droëvan terminals: capacitors, electromagnets, power systems essential to the railguns
- Intermittent terminals: variant armor compositions in smaller quantities

### Manufacturing Limitations

The houses cannot manufacture. They can shape what the autofactory produces—forge alloys into weapons, assemble capacitors into power systems—but they cannot create the base materials. This makes them entirely dependent on systems that might fail without warning.

### Trade Dependency

The Avouvar import almost all their food from the south. A sufficiently ruthless power could blockade them into starvation. But no one else can produce what the Smelters produce—components the Furnaces cannot match, alloys Imperial metalworking cannot replicate.

### Medical Leverage

Lavoëran provides most medical care. Houses that fall behind on tribute find clinic services degrading. No one acknowledges this explicitly. The correlation between payment and outcomes simply exists.

### Named Avouvar Operations

*Vesztraëlin Terminals* (Garrison District): Three active terminals. Primary: armor alloys—metals that stop penetrators, absorb impacts, resist plasma heat. Secondary: pressure-rated tubes for pneumatic and hydraulic systems. Third: volatile compounds—oxidizers, propellants, specialized chemicals requiring careful handling.

*Droëvan Terminals* (Garrison District): Two active, one intermittent. Primary: capacitors and electromagnets essential to every railgun in Asovoë. Secondary: power systems—compact generators, energy storage, conversion equipment. Intermittent: variant armor compositions.

*Vesztraëlin Armory Prime*: Where terminal output becomes weapons. Shaping alloys into armor plates, assembling components into functional equipment. Cannot create base materials—only transform what the autofactory provides.

*Droëvan Emplacement Works*: Railgun installation and maintenance. The sixty-kilogram weapons require fixed mounts and three-person crews. Every precinct's defensive emplacements depend on Droëvan components.

*Czernaël Trade Armory* (neutral ground): Assembly operation serving the external market. Where Avouvar goods meet outside customers—primarily Akama seeking armor alloys through San Juan factorias.

*San Juan Factorias* (San Juan Islands): Trade interface with the outside world. Armor alloys sized for non-Avouvar customers, weapon components adapted for larger hands, authentication certificates. Significant markup.

*Vroëszka Smelter Seconds* (Smelters Precinct): Salvage operation. Terminal output that doesn't meet spec, damaged components, variable quality. Cheaper than primary production—adequate for many purposes.

*Lavoëran Archives* (Cloning Precinct): Documentation of every significant weapon in Asovoë. Not a manufacturer—a registry. When disputes arise about weapon provenance or house claims to particular armaments, Lavoëran records determine truth.

## Exemplar Characters

**Arsenal-Keeper Droëvan** — Three hundred forty years old, void-black skin absorbing light, controls Czernaël Trade Armory at the San Juan factorias. Sells to anyone who can pay, maintains strict neutrality. Knows the Interdict is a prison. Focuses on the immediate: trade relationships maintained across centuries.
