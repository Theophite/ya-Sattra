# Professional Kilits and Certification

## The Licensing Principle

The Republic does not license individuals. It certifies kilits as authorized to credential practitioners in regulated professions. To practice law, medicine, engineering, or other controlled trades, one must belong to a kilit that holds state certification for that profession. The kilit determines who meets its standards; the state determines which kilits may make that determination.

This structure emerged from revolutionary compromise. Dawn wanted to break pre-revolutionary guild monopolies that restricted professional access to established families. Dusk wanted to preserve quality standards and professional self-governance. The solution preserved guild authority over credentialing while stripping monopoly status. Multiple kilits may receive certification for the same profession, creating competition among certifying bodies that theoretically broadens access.

In practice, the old professional families still dominate most fields. New kilits can apply for certification, but the review process favors established institutions with proven track records. Revolutionary ideology created formal openings; practical barriers preserved most existing advantages.

## Legal Practice

### Certifying Kilits

Three kilits currently hold certification to credential attorneys: the Taho Bar Fraternal, the Confraternity of Sekhal, and the Advocates of the East.

**The Taho Bar Fraternal** is oldest, wealthiest, and most prestigious. Founded under the Sultanate as the legal arm of the kilit-zem-kilitam, it survived revolution by accepting Republican oversight while retaining internal governance. The Bar maintains its own examination system, requiring demonstrated fluency in Imperial legal language plus mastery of commercial and property law. Its members dominate Taho's commercial courts, handle major contract disputes, represent merchant kilits in regulatory proceedings. The examination is notoriously difficult, and passage rates hover around 30% for first-time candidates.

The Bar's membership is overwhelmingly tephri and near-baseline—populations with generational legal traditions and the educational resources to produce competent candidates. Its roughly 450 members remain minority presence among the Republic's attorneys despite dominating commercial practice. Serrulata attorneys exist within the Bar but comprise perhaps a fifth of membership despite Serrulata comprising 65% of Republican population. The examination's Imperial language requirement functions as filter: fluency requires either childhood exposure or years of dedicated study, and childhood exposure correlates strongly with urban, commercially-connected families.

**The Confraternity of Sekhal** takes its name from the Serrulata name for the Third Shining One—the Oracle whose virtue is Adjudication, bilateral symmetry in law. The Confraternity serves Serrulata populations who want legal representation from practitioners who share their religious traditions and understand pastoralist culture.

The Confraternity's examination tests legal knowledge in Ganati rather than Imperial. Its curriculum emphasizes land rights, water disputes, kinship obligations, and the customary law that governs pastoral communities—matters the Bar considers beneath its commercial sophistication. Confraternity attorneys represent agricultural kilits in land-grant disputes, mediate water-sharing conflicts between pastoral bands, and handle the family law that governs Serrulata inheritance patterns.

The religious naming is deliberate. Sekhal intercedes with the Binder—the Foreigner who enforces the Mandate against interfering with what holds the world together. Law, in this theology, maintains the bindings that preserve social order. An attorney serves Sekhal by ensuring disputes resolve through proper judgment rather than violence or chaos. This theological framing appeals to traditional Serrulata who find the Bar's secular commercialism spiritually empty.

The Confraternity has roughly 700 members, the largest of the three legal kilits, concentrated in Ganat proper and the pastoral territories. Its attorneys are predominantly Serrulata, though near-baselines who learned Ganati legal traditions can qualify. The examination is less demanding than the Bar's in technical sophistication but tests knowledge the Bar doesn't value—customary precedent, pastoral custom, religious dimensions of legal obligation. Revolutionary legal changes have swelled the Confraternity's ranks as land-grant disputes multiply and novel questions of pastoral rights under republican law require adjudication.

**The Advocates of the East** formed to serve frontier settlements beyond either established kilit's reach. The eastern territories—from High Junction to the marcher lands—need legal practitioners, but neither the Bar's commercial sophistication nor the Confraternity's pastoral specialization fits frontier conditions.

Advocates certification emphasizes practical problem-solving. Can you draft a land-grant transfer that will survive Ministry review? Can you mediate a boundary dispute between neighbors who will have to live next to each other regardless of outcome? Can you defend a criminal case before a circuit judge who visits twice yearly? The examination tests these competencies rather than scholarly accomplishment.

The roughly 400 Advocates scatter across settlements where they often serve as the only legal practitioners within a hundred miles. They handle everything: property transfers, contract disputes, criminal defense, estate administration, whatever needs doing. The generalist practice differs fundamentally from Taho's specialization or the Confraternity's pastoral focus. Their numbers have doubled since the revolution began, tracking frontier expansion eastward.

### Discipline and Expulsion

Each kilit maintains its own disciplinary procedures. An attorney expelled from the Bar Fraternal loses the right to practice before commercial courts where Bar membership is effectively required. Expulsion from the Confraternity removes standing in disputes involving pastoral custom and religious dimensions of law. Advocates of the East expulsion cuts off the referral systems that sustain remote practice.

The kilits share information about disciplinary actions. An attorney expelled from one for serious misconduct—embezzlement, fraud, repeated incompetence—will find the others unwilling to accept their application. This isn't formal coordination; the legal community is small enough that reputation travels. But expulsion from one kilit doesn't automatically preclude practice through another. An attorney expelled from the Bar for insufficient revolutionary commitment might find the Confraternity willing to accept someone who understands Serrulata tradition; an attorney expelled from the Confraternity for theological heterodoxy might find the Bar indifferent to religious matters.

## Medical Practice

### The Companions Question

The Companions Guild does not operate freely in Republican territory. Guild members may enter Ganat only under specific contract—hired by kilits that need Mutterer management for particular projects, or by institutions requiring expertise unavailable domestically. A Taho merchant kilit building new warehouse facilities might contract a Companions choir for construction; the contract specifies duration, scope, and compensation, and the Companions depart when the work concludes.

This arrangement emerged from revolutionary principle. The Guild is an Imperial institution answering to masters in ya-Sattra. Allowing free Guild operation would acknowledge Imperial authority within Republican borders—anathema to revolutionary ideology regardless of practical utility. Contracted work is different: the Republic hires foreign expertise as sovereign equals hire each other's specialists. The legal fiction preserves independence while accessing capabilities the Republic cannot replicate.

The arrangement satisfies no one completely. Dawn considers any Guild presence ideological contamination; Dusk considers contract restrictions economically inefficient. But the compromise has held for eighteen years. Companions enter, perform contracted work, and leave. They do not establish permanent facilities, do not recruit Republican members, do not provide ongoing medical services to the general population.

### Republican Medical Kilits

Two kilits hold certification to credential medical practitioners.

**The Taho Physicians Confraternity** represents formally trained doctors—practitioners who completed structured education in anatomy, diagnosis, pharmacology, and surgery. The Confraternity maintains rigorous examination standards testing both theoretical knowledge and clinical competence. Its members practice throughout the Republic but concentrate in Taho and other urban centers where their sophisticated skills command premium compensation.

Confraternity physicians trained in various ways: some studied in Imperial institutions before emigrating, some apprenticed with established practitioners, some attended the Academy's limited medical curriculum supplemented by clinical rotations. What unifies them is demonstrated competence at the Confraternity's demanding standard. The roughly 450 members are doctors in the full sense—capable of complex diagnosis, surgical intervention, and pharmaceutical treatment.

The Confraternity is politically neutral, serving patients regardless of factional alignment. Its members include both Dawn sympathizers who see medicine as revolutionary service and Dusk pragmatists who see it as respectable profession. The institution's prestige derives from competence, not ideology.

**The Union of Republican Medics** emerged from the Yarangar War and encompasses a broader range of practitioners. Doctors, yes—field surgeons who learned their craft under combat conditions, roughly 650 physicians—but also nurses, midwives, apothecaries, bone-setters, and paraprofessionals whose skills don't fit Confraternity categories. The Union's 2,800 members make it one of the Republic's larger professional kilits.

The Union maintains its own certification chain. A candidate enters as apprentice, advances to journeyman after demonstrating basic competence, and achieves full practitioner status through examination and peer evaluation. Different certification tracks exist for different specializations: surgical certification requires different demonstration than midwifery certification, which differs from pharmaceutical certification.

This structure reflects revolutionary ideology about professional access. The Confraternity's demanding single standard excludes practitioners who possess genuine healing skills but lack formal education. The Union's tiered system creates pathways for diverse practitioners while maintaining quality through specialized certification. A Union-certified midwife has demonstrated competence at delivering babies; she need not demonstrate competence at surgery to earn her credential.

The Union serves populations the Confraternity doesn't reach. Its certified practitioners scatter across frontier settlements, pastoral territories, and working-class urban districts. They provide primary care, handle emergencies, and refer complex cases to Confraternity colleagues when sophisticated intervention is needed.

The two institutions maintain professional cooperation despite different constituencies. A Confraternity physician in Taho accepts referrals from Union practitioners in the eastern settlements. A Union midwife facing complications summons Confraternity surgical expertise. The division reflects specialization more than competition.

Political alignment differs sharply. The Confraternity is neutral; the Union is firmly Dawn. Its origins in revolutionary warfare, its constituency among frontier and working populations, its ideological commitment to accessible care—all align with Dawn politics. Union representatives in Senate proceedings reliably vote the Dawn position on health-related matters.

## Engineering

### The Academy Pipeline

The Taho Academy trains most Republican engineers through its three-year technical program. Students learn hydraulics, metallurgy, electrical systems, mechanical engineering—the practical knowledge that keeps infrastructure functioning. They maintain actual systems during training: Taho's waterworks, its generating plants, its electrical distribution, its port facilities. Graduation produces competent practitioners, not theoretical scholars.

But Academy completion is not professional certification. A graduate cannot independently practice engineering without kilit membership. The Academy provides education; kilits provide credentials.

### Engineering Kilits

**The Taho Engineers' Fraternal** is the primary certifying body. Founded by Academy graduates in the revolution's early years, it established examination standards, professional ethics codes, and the mutual-aid arrangements that sustain engineering practice. About 800 members hold Fraternal certification, working throughout the Republic on infrastructure projects, industrial installations, and technical consulting.

Fraternal examination tests both theoretical knowledge and practical competence. Candidates must demonstrate they can design systems that work, not merely explain principles abstractly. The failure rate is moderate—perhaps 40%—but successful candidates emerge with credentials that command respect and reasonable compensation.

**The Dam Workers' Technical Section** provides alternative certification for engineers working specifically on Dam infrastructure. This is narrower credential than Fraternal membership: Dam Section certification authorizes practice within Dam operations, not general engineering throughout the Republic. About 200 engineers hold Section certification, embedded within the Dam's operational structure rather than practicing independently.

The Section exists because Dam operations require specialized knowledge the Fraternal doesn't test. Hydroelectric systems, water management, the specific equipment configurations that make the Dam function—these require expertise beyond general engineering competence. Dam Section engineers often hold both credentials, Fraternal for general practice and Section for Dam-specific authority.

**The Frontier Technical Association** serves engineers in remote settlements, much as the Advocates of the East serves frontier attorneys. Its certification emphasizes practical problem-solving over formal credentials: can you repair this pump, can you maintain this generator, can you improvise solutions with limited resources? About 300 members practice under Association credentials, often the only technical expertise within vast territories.

### Imperial Equipment Complications

Engineering certification cannot escape the Republic's technological position. Much critical infrastructure runs on Imperial equipment that only specialists can maintain. Cultivators for the agricultural kilits. Generating equipment that preceded the revolution. Communication systems that connect settlements across vast distances. This equipment requires knowledge that ultimately traces to Imperial sources.

Some engineering kilits require demonstrated ability to work with Imperial systems as certification condition. This limits membership to people trained in Imperial institutions or apprenticed to Imperial-trained masters. Dawn finds this dependency ideologically objectionable but cannot propose alternatives that actually function. Equipment doesn't care about revolutionary principles; it requires maintenance by people who understand it.

The practical result is that engineering leadership remains partially dependent on Imperial knowledge transmission even as the Republic trains domestic engineers. Academy graduates can maintain systems others design; they cannot yet design systems entirely from Republican knowledge. The dependency diminishes as reverse-engineering progresses, but it remains significant constraint on professional independence.

## Other Professions

### Architecture

Two kilits hold architectural certification. The Taho Architects' Guild serves urban practice, designing commercial buildings, public facilities, merchant compounds. The Builders' Collective serves frontier construction, emphasizing practical structures over aesthetic sophistication. The division reflects different clienteles: urban architects serve wealthy patrons who want impressive buildings; frontier architects serve communities that need functional shelter.

Architectural certification requires demonstrated design competence plus practical construction knowledge. Architects must understand what can actually be built with available materials by available labor. The purely theoretical designer—someone who draws beautiful plans that cannot be executed—fails certification regardless of aesthetic merit.

### Accountancy

The Taho Accountants' Fraternal holds monopoly certification for professional bookkeeping and auditing. This is the one profession where no competing kilits have emerged: accounting requires trust, and trust requires institutional reputation accumulated over generations. The Fraternal existed under the Sultanate, served the Royal Exchequer, and continues serving the People's Exchequer. Its members audit kilit declarations, manage commercial accounts, verify the financial records on which the entire tax system depends.

Accountancy certification requires examination in bookkeeping, auditing procedures, and commercial mathematics. The Fraternal maintains rigorous standards because its institutional position depends on universal confidence in member competence. A scandal involving fraudulent auditing would threaten every member's livelihood; collective interest enforces individual discipline.

### Surveying

Land surveying is critical profession in a republic distributing land grants across vast territories. The Surveyor's Congress holds certification, training members in measurement techniques, boundary determination, and the mapping skills that define property rights. Roughly 800 surveyors practice under Congress credentials, concentrated in frontier regions where new grants require demarcation. The number has grown steadily as eastern settlement accelerates.

Surveying disputes generate significant litigation. When two kilits disagree about where property boundaries lie, surveyors testify as expert witnesses. The profession's technical credibility underlies property rights across the eastern settlements.

### Veterinary Practice

Animal husbandry is economically significant in a republic with substantial pastoral population. The Herders' Medical Association provides certification for veterinary practitioners—people who treat destriers, maintain breeding stock, and keep the animals that pastoral kilits depend upon. About 300 certified veterinarians practice throughout the Republic, mostly serving pastoral kilits under contract arrangements rather than independent practice.

## Certification Process

### Application

A kilit seeking certification authority submits application to the Ministry of Justice, which administers professional licensing. The application must demonstrate institutional capacity to examine candidates, maintain membership records, enforce disciplinary standards, and serve public interest. Review takes six months to two years depending on profession complexity and political considerations.

New kilits rarely receive certification in professions where established kilits already operate. The review process asks whether additional certification authority serves public interest—and established kilits argue convincingly that competition fragments professional standards, confuses clients, and undermines quality control. This reasoning protects incumbents from competition while wearing public-interest clothing.

### Examination

Each certified kilit maintains its own examination system. The state does not dictate examination content; kilits determine what competence means in their profession. This creates variation: Bar Fraternal examinations emphasize Imperial legal language while Confraternity of Sekhal examinations emphasize Ganati procedure and pastoral custom. Both produce certified attorneys, but the certifications represent different professional profiles.

Examination failure creates waiting period—typically one year—before re-examination. Multiple failures eventually trigger permanent exclusion from that kilit, though candidates may apply to alternative certifying kilits if any exist.

### Discipline

Certified kilits maintain disciplinary authority over members. Procedures vary by institution but typically involve complaints, investigation, hearing, and sanction ranging from private reprimand to public censure to suspension to expulsion. Appeals from kilit discipline go to commercial courts, where judges review whether procedures were followed rather than substituting their judgment for professional determination.

The state can revoke kilit certification for systematic failure to maintain professional standards. This has never happened. The threat exists as background constraint rather than active enforcement mechanism.

## Political Dynamics

### Professional Kilits as Swing Votes

Professional kilits vote their specific interests without consistent ideological alignment. The Bar Fraternal leans Dusk through commercial connections. The Confraternity of Sekhal votes with traditional Serrulata constituencies—sometimes Dawn on land issues, sometimes Dusk on questions of customary authority. The Advocates of the East align with frontier settlement interests. The Physicians Confraternity maintains studied neutrality; the Union of Republican Medics is firmly Dawn. Engineers split depending on which industries employ them.

This political fragmentation makes professional kilits valuable swing votes in close Senate matters. A major policy debate might turn on whether the Engineers' Fraternal sides with Dawn or Dusk. Both factions court professional support through regulatory favors, contract preferences, and policy adjustments that serve professional interests.

### Monopoly Protection

Existing kilits resist new certification applications because competition threatens their position. A new legal kilit would divide the client population; existing kilits would lose members, revenue, and political weight. The review process allows incumbents to raise objections, and objections from established kilits carry significant weight with Ministry reviewers.

This protection creates tension with revolutionary ideology. Dawn rhetoric celebrates competition and opposes inherited privilege; Dawn-aligned professional kilits protect their monopolies as vigorously as Dusk-aligned ones. The contradiction is visible but unresolved.

### The Language Question

Imperial fluency requirements for certain professions maintain barriers that revolutionary ideology should reject. An attorney must read Imperial to practice commercial law; an engineer must understand Imperial technical documentation to maintain critical equipment. These requirements exclude populations without access to Imperial education—which correlates with caste, geography, and class.

Reform proposals emerge periodically. Translate the legal code into Ganati. Produce technical documentation in Republican languages. Create examination pathways that don't require Imperial fluency. These proposals die in committee because the professionals who would implement them are the same professionals whose competitive advantage depends on existing requirements. The Bar Fraternal votes against legal translation; its members' expertise becomes less valuable if anyone can read the statutes.
