# Ya-Tsatsa Architecture

**Core intention:** "We are waiting. We are receiving. The important things cannot be shown."

Material culture speaks through restraint and attention.

---

## Physical Environment

Ya-Tsatsa occupies white-gray cliffs on the northern coast—quartz diorite rising three hundred feet or more from the ocean, extending for dozens of miles in both directions.

**The stone:** Pale enough to glow faintly in moonlight, hard enough to hold carved detail for millennia. Visible crystals: quartz that catches light, feldspar that holds color, dark minerals creating flecks and striations.

**The geology IS the architecture.** The city grows from the cliffs, uses stone quarried from them, buries its sacred dead in them. Construction material is the cliff itself, cut and shaped but never disguised.

**Climate:** Cool and constant—low fifties year-round, clouds accumulating over the ocean but rain seldom reaching the cliffs. The wind never stops. Seasons distinguished by wind direction and intensity, not temperature.

## The Kalbat Tombs

Nearly every accessible cliff surface has been carved into tombs—tens of thousands, each cut to suggest the slack-jawed face of the receiver it contains. All face the ocean. All wait for something that may or may not arrive.

**The faces:** Cycladic abstraction—broad suggestion rather than portraiture. The slack jaw, the closed eyes, the expression of reception. Weathered smooth by centuries of salt spray and wind, features blurred to suggestion. This is intentional: the faces should become abstraction over time.

**The tradition:** Began before the Interdict, when kalbats were living instruments receiving Testaments. When a kalbat dies anywhere in the Empire, word reaches ya-Tsatsa, and a new tomb is carved.

## The Witness Infrastructure

**The eight domes** in the Upper City predate the city itself—Witness construction for creating conditions for transcendence.

**The three Cradles** are vast craters where Oracles achieved consciousness. This was infrastructure for apotheosis.

**The Eighth Dome:** Bronze doors open twice yearly for processions that enter but don't emerge. An architect measured its external geometry for three months—his numbers suggested non-Euclidean construction, proportions implying spaces that couldn't exist within the visible footprint.

## Aesthetic Principles

**Monochrome restraint:** All construction uses the same white-gray stone quarried while cutting tombs. No paint. No applied decoration. The only color comes from prayer flags—temporary, movable, never permanent. What matters cannot be shown, so why show anything?

**Material honesty:** Objects reveal what they're made from. Undyed linen shows its fiber. Stone shows its grain. Metal shows its working. Covering these properties suggests deception.

**Functional geometry:** Patterns emerge from structure rather than applied ornament. The geometric pins worn by Aureate families derive meaning from shape, not precious materials.

**Earned color:** What little color appears must justify itself. Prayer flags mark religious observance. The teal, cream, and rust-orange fabrics reference specific ritual contexts. Arbitrary decoration is considered juvenile.

---

## Acoustic Architecture

**Ceramic whistles** mounted on rooftops—tubes ranging from six inches to three feet—produce different tones based on size and bore. During gales the entire city drones and shrieks, multiple tones layering without resolution.

**Summer's bronze chimes** ring irregularly in gentle pressure, dispersing through streets.

**Quiet Days:** Chimes come down and whistles are stopped with cork. The city goes silent except for ocean hitting cliffs. Then Quiet Day ends, corks are removed, and the acoustic architecture resumes.

## Color Palette

| Element | Color |
|---------|-------|
| Stone | White-gray quartz diorite |
| Clothing | Undyed natural cream (highest status) |
| Prayer flags | Rust-orange, teal, cream |
| Bronze | On oldest structures—domes, great doors |
| Tombs | Black of open tomb-mouths |
| Pierrots | Blue-black around eye sockets and lips |

**Darkness:** At night there are no lanterns. The Upper City goes dark. This is deliberate—the darkness is part of the aesthetic, part of the waiting.

## Generating New Details

*Example: A street in the Upper City.* White-gray quartz diorite on every surface—walls, paving, steps. No paint, no plaster, no color except prayer flags strung between buildings, snapping in constant wind. Buildings have no signs; residents know what's inside. A ceramic whistle mounted at the corner produces a low tone that rises and falls with gusts.

*Example: A kalbat tomb carved last year.* Fresh-cut stone, face still sharp: closed eyes, slack jaw, expression of reception. The carving follows the stone's natural planes. In three hundred years the features will blur to suggestion, joining thousands of weathered faces covering the cliffs.

*Example: A wealthy Tsatsan's dress.* Undyed linen in natural cream, simple cuts following body structure. No jewelry except a geometric silver pin marking family affiliation. Quality shows in weave fineness, tailoring precision, durability—not applied decoration. The choice not to display is the statement.
