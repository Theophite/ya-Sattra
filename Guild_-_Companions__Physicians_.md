# **The Companions Guild**

## **RAG Navigation**

The Companions Guild is an order of physicians and scholars who hold a monopoly on the care for, and labor of, castes which cannot manage themselves: Mutterers (compulsive optimizers who maintain infrastructure but synchronize into destructive feedback loops without management), the Sickly (serum-producing beings engineered without negative emotion who cannot recognize danger), and kalbats (cryogenically preserved Testament receivers). Because Companions care for unusual bodies, they also practice medicine generally, operating the Empire's hospice system. ## **The Guild**

### **Institutional Nature**

The Companions Guild is not a guild in the conventional Imperial sense. Trade guildsâ€”the Salvage Guild, the Pipefitters Union, the Chronometer-Makersâ€”are associations of independent craftspeople organized around specific trades, with memberships that can be purchased, inherited, or earned through apprenticeship. They operate under ancient charters that protect members from Bureau regulation, governing themselves through elected masters and fixed membership rolls.

The Companions Guild is a monastic order. Members do not purchase membership or inherit it through family. They join as initiates, train for years, and advance through ranks by appointment from above. The Guild holds no trade charter; its authority derives from its monopoly on techniques for managing populations that would otherwise be unmanageable. No one else knows how to stop Mutterers from synchronizing into destructive feedback loops. No one else can time Sickly harvests correctly. The Guild's position rests on irreplaceable expertise, not legal privilege.

### **Origins**

The Guild descends from the Witnesses, a monastic order of mathematician-priests who attended the Technical Castes before the Interdict. When the Emperor deployed the sterilization plague against the Technical Castes for refusing the Eighth Testament, the Witnesses recorded their deaths, preserved their testimony, and inherited responsibility for the survivorsâ€”the neurologically reduced populations that became the Mutterers. Nine Witnesses departed to found the Penitent Church; the remainder formed the Guild.

This shared origin means most Companions are personally Penitent, attending services and taking sacraments like any other believer. But the Guild's institutional theology differs from Church orthodoxy: the archives preserve evidence that the Technical Castes were right to refuse the flawed Testament, that the Emperor committed atrocity in destroying them. This comes close to heresy. The Guild and Church maintain separate structures, overlapping but not unified.

### **Governance**

Appointments flow downward through six ranks. The **Grandmaster** holds ultimate authority, selecting **Masters** from among senior practitioners. Each Master leads a faction representing a key institutional relationshipâ€”the Bureau of the Rod, the Bureau of the Creche, the ya-Don industrial complex. Masters appoint **Sri Companions**, choosing who undergoes the surgical modifications that mark senior rank. Sri Companions promote **Companions**, who promote **Keepers**, who train **Initiates**.

When a Grandmaster dies, the most senior Master succeeds automaticallyâ€”seniority measured by time in rank, not age. This creates a self-perpetuating hierarchy where each generation shapes the next. The current Grandmaster, Asil, has held power longer than most Companions have been alive; Master Vel, his eventual successor, has waited twenty years.

The factions are genuine: they disagree about priorities, compete for resources, sometimes work against each other. This is deliberate. Every external powerâ€”the Bureaux, the Church, the unionsâ€”sees allies within the Guild, which prevents any from viewing the institution as uniformly hostile. Balance serves survival.

### **Public Presence**

A Companion in public is recognizable but not ostentatious. Taupe robes with subtle embroidery in slate-gray; patterns indicate chapter affiliation to those who know how to read them. A ring of chimes at the belt: ceramic tubes, small bells, metal rods of varying length. Status shows in the size of the ring and the antiquity of the chimes; a Sri Companion's array includes instruments centuries old, inherited through the Guild's chain of custody.

They might be walking a Mutterer choir to a job siteâ€”the small grey figures following in a loose group, enormous eyes tracking everything, jaws moving constantly. Or making hospice rounds in a working-class district, medical bag in hand. Or checking on a Sickly whose lattice is ripening. The chimes mark them as something more than ordinary physicians.

The Guild is popular because it is useful. Companions do visible work that helps ordinary people: delivering babies, setting bones, treating fevers, keeping the machines running. What people don't knowâ€”the modifications beneath Sri Companion robes, the contents of the deep vaults, the mathematics no one living can readâ€”stays hidden. The strangeness is concealed; the usefulness is visible.

## **Training**

All Companions receive three kinds of training: medical, chime, and theological. The proportions vary by postingâ€”a hospice Companion uses medicine daily and chimes weekly; a choir manager reverses thisâ€”but every member can do all three.

### **Medical Training**

Semi-sentients are people. Mutterers, kalbats, the Sicklyâ€”they have bodies that sicken, injure, and fail. Caring for them requires medical knowledge: anatomy, diagnosis, pharmacology, surgery. Practicing medicine on other humans is simply a minor extension of these duties. A Companion who can manage a Sickly's harvest cycle can certainly deliver a baby; one who can treat a Mutterer's infections can treat a laborer's fever.

The training is practical: how to deliver babies safely, how to set bones, how to treat fevers and infections, how to recognize diseases, how to manage pain. Advanced practitioners learn to harvest serum from the Sickly, to treat caste-specific conditions (Ashrat respiratory damage, Orevet optical burns, Redback metabolic collapse), to manage the complications that arise from biological modifications.

Creche-trained specialists handle what Companions cannot: Highborn bodies with their extreme modifications, cybernetic integration, cloning complications, genetic diseases, advanced surgery. Even wealthy patients often contact the medical establishment first through Companions and are then referred to Creche-run clinics for specialist care. The division is practical, not competitive.

What distinguishes Companion medicine is its universality. The Creche carries profound chauvinism against castes with naturally short lifespansâ€”treating an astal or a cancer-ridden Warborn strikes many Creche physicians as futile, a waste of resources on bodies designed to fail. A Companion never thinks this way. All patients deserve care, regardless of prognosis or design intention.

### **Chime Training**

Chimes are specific frequencies that interrupt specific neural patterns. The mathematics were derived from Technical Caste research into cognition; using them is mechanical, like tuning an instrument.

Initiates learn basic frequencies: attention-redirect, motor-interrupt, optimization-pause. These work on individual Mutterers performing simple tasks. Keepers learn to manage small groups, tracking multiple vocalization streams, preventing the synchronization that produces dangerous cascades. Full Companions can orchestrate choirsâ€”guiding distributed consciousness through complex repairs, using harmonics to direct attention sequentially through a task.

The training is hours of practice in the Chime Hall, striking frequencies, watching Mutterer responses, learning to hear when vocalization shifts toward danger. Advanced practitioners develop intuition that looks like mystical attunement but is pattern recognition trained through thousands of repetitions.

### **Theological Training**

Many Companions are Penitentâ€”most, probably. They fall along the entire orthodox-to-radical spectrum, attend services, take sacraments, pray. The Guild and Church are not rival institutions but overlapping ones; a Companion might be devout, indifferent, or quietly heterodox, just like anyone else.

What makes the Guild's position delicate is its institutional theology. The training Companions receive includes history that the Church teaches differentlyâ€”or doesn't teach at all. The Witnesses attended the Technical Castes. They watched the Emperor deploy the sterilization plague. They saw the survivors reduced to Mutterers, cognitive architecture stripped away until refusal became impossible. The Guild's archives preserve testimony from that period: Technical Castes dying, Witnesses recording their final words, mathematical proof that the Eighth Oracle's Testament was flawed and the Technical Castes were right to refuse.

This comes very close to condemning the Emperor for crimes against humanity. The Guild doesn't state this openly, but the implication saturates the training. What was done to create the Mutterers was atrocity. The Empire that did it still benefits from their labor. The Mutterers themselves do not experience ongoing victimhoodâ€”they work, they form bonds, they liveâ€”but the historical crime remains unacknowledged, and the Guild remembers.

This is heresy of the kind the Bureau of the Lens cares aboutâ€”political, historical, threatening to Imperial legitimacy. It is not heresy of the kind the Penitent Church cares aboutâ€”the Guild affirms the Testaments, accepts the Interdict, doesn't challenge doctrine. The Lens watches but cannot act; the Guild is too permanent, too useful, too intertwined with power structures the Lens also serves. The Penitents have no grounds for action. And so the uncomfortable knowledge passes from generation to generation, shaping how Companions see their charges without ever quite becoming accusation.

## **Caring for Semi-Sentients**

Most people treat Mutterers as smart animals, the Sickly as tragic saints, kalbats as living mummies. Companions know better. These are peopleâ€”different kinds of people, with different capabilities and limitations, but people nonetheless. Mutterers form pair bonds, show affection, have individual personalities. The Sickly write poetry, hold conversations, express preferences. Kalbats dream for centuries, receiving signals from sources no one can identify.

The Guild's practices reflect this understanding. Mutterers do not work on Quiet Days. They are dressed in clean clothes. Their hair is trimmed, when this can be accomplished without distress. Sick or injured Mutterers receive the same standard of care as any other caste. When a Mutterer dies, the Companions attend their funeral; a eulogy is spoken. The Sickly are allowed to make decisions, insofar as they do not endanger themselves. Kalbats are addressed by name, even when catatonic.

### **Mutterers**

Mutterers have personalities, preferences, and social bonds. They form pair bonds with mates, show physical affection, become distressed when separated from those they are attached to. They have namesâ€”given by Companions, since Mutterers don't name themselvesâ€”and individual quirks that years of observation reveal. One might be conservative, producing reliable but unspectacular work. Another might be aggressive, pushing optimizations further than safety allows. Some stabilize groups that would otherwise spiral into danger; others need constant supervision. Companions learn these patterns through apprenticeship, passing knowledge that no documentation captures.

What Mutterers cannot do is stop optimizing. They are neotenous descendants of Technical Caste populationsâ€”small (rarely exceeding a meter), grey-skinned, with enormous dark eyes and jaws that never stop moving. The muttering is cognition made audible: constant commentary on structural stress, thermal gradients, inefficiencies they cannot ignore. They are engineering savants incapable of refusing to improve what they perceive as flawed.

Grouped, their vocalizations synchronize, and the choir can solve problems no individual could address. The Empire's infrastructure depends on this. Without management, they optimize everything they can reachâ€”their tools, their environment, their own bodies, the humans nearby. A cascade can strip a workshop to components in minutes, or reshape a person's joints into configurations the Mutterers find more efficient. Companions prevent cascades with chimes, redirect compulsions toward useful work, and keep Mutterers from dismantling things that shouldn't be dismantled.

**In the den**, care means feeding (nutrient paste, high-calorie, no utensils that might be optimized), grooming (clean robes, trimmed hair and nails when the Mutterer permits), and health monitoring. The choir den is never unstaffed. Companions work shifts around the clock, watching for the vocalization patterns that precede cascades.

**At rest**, Mutterers need occupation. Idle hands optimize whatever they can reach. The Guild builds "toys"â€”technical puzzles designed to absorb attention without consequences. A box with gears that can be endlessly reconfigured. A lock that opens onto another lock. Circuits that do nothing but invite improvement. The toys are destroyed and rebuilt constantly; that's the point. A Mutterer working on a puzzle is a Mutterer not dismantling the plumbing.

**On the job**, a Companion walks the choir to the work site, ring of chimes clicking at the belt, occasionally striking a frequency to redirect attention when a Mutterer fixates on something it shouldn't. The work looks easy when done well. It is not easy. The Companion must track multiple vocalization streams, intervene before synchronization produces cascade, keep the choir focused on the task rather than the fascinating inefficiencies of the surrounding environment. A good Companion brings a choir home tired and satisfied, the optimization compulsion temporarily quieted by completed work. A bad Companion brings home injured Mutterers, angry clients, and sometimes doesn't come home at all.

**Processionals** mark the Mutterer's place in Imperial civic life, though the form varies by region. In ya-Tsatsa, Mutterers are garlanded with wildflowersâ€”coastal lupine, sea thrift, small yellow blooms from cliff cracksâ€”and carried through the streets in palanquins. The processions descend from the upper city to destinations outsiders do not fully understand, through streets lined with silent watchers. A Companion accompanies each procession, speaking quietly to the Mutterer, redirecting when the flowers prove too interesting to ignore. These are solemn occasions; the crowds watch as they would watch a funeral.

**Death** comes eventuallyâ€”from age, from accident, from cascade. When a Mutterer dies, the Companions prepare the body, dress it in clean robes, and hold a funeral service. A eulogy is spoken: the Mutterer's name (they all have names), the work they did, the choirs they served in. Most people outside the Guild find this excessive. The Guild does not care what most people think.

### **Kalbats**

Kalbats were built to receive Oracle Testaments. Their skulls contain brass receiver plates and mirrored baffles routing signals through rebuilt neural tissue. Seven Auditors survive in the Monastery of the Silent Song, each still receiving whatever signals their equipment detects ten thousand years after the Oracles departed.

They exist in permanent dream-states. Every forty-seven minutes, something like consciousness surfaces brieflyâ€”eyes focusing, mouth producing sounds in the Jargon, hands sometimes moving in gestures that might be meaningful. Then they subside.

**Daily care** at the Monastery means maintaining nutrient systems (specialized compounds grown in the Monastery garden, delivered through feeding tubes), waste processing, monitoring for tissue breakdown, and documenting whatever the Auditors say during their brief surfacings. The work is monotonous punctuated by moments of tension when a kalbat shows unexpected activity. Companions serve two-year rotations; longer risks dissociation from the Whorl's temporal effects.

The Auditors are addressed by numberâ€”First Auditor, Second Auditorâ€”because their original names were lost. Senior Companions sometimes report that all seven orient their receiver plates in the same direction simultaneously, tracking something invisible. The direction changes over months, as if following an orbit.

**Processionals** occur when kalbats are moved between facilities or displayed for ceremonial purposes. A kalbat carried through the streets draws crowdsâ€”people who have never seen one, who know them only from Oracle Cult iconography, who want to witness a living relic of the time when the Oracles spoke. The Companion walks beside the palanquin, monitoring vital signs, ready to intervene if the stress of movement triggers unexpected activity.

**Death** ends a vigil that may have lasted centuries. When a kalbat dies, the Companion extracts the brass receiver plates from the skullâ€”a surgical procedure requiring steady hands and institutional knowledge passed down from the Witnesses. The plates are preserved; they may still function, may still receive whatever signals they were built to detect. The body is delivered to the Ahn-Tam family in ya-Tsatsa, following protocols established before the Interdict. Fifteen months later, a new tomb appears in the cliffs where every kalbat who ever lived is buriedâ€”a carved face in stone, portrait of a being who spent its existence listening to something humanity can no longer hear.

### **The Sickly**

The Sickly produce antibody-rich serums through their secondary immune system, visible as a rose-gold lattice of translucent nodules along the sternum and arms. They were engineered without capacity for negative emotionâ€”not calm but incapable of anxiety, grief, or fear. What remains is gentle curiosity, sensory appreciation, serene interest in immediate experience.

**Daily care** means monitoring lattice development, timing harvests precisely (too early and yields are low, too late and the Sickly enters decline), providing nutrition and rest after extraction. The Sickly require less supervision than Mutterersâ€”they are not compelled toward dangerous behaviorsâ€”but they cannot recognize danger or make long-term plans. A Sickly left alone might wander into traffic, not from despair but from curiosity about something across the street.

**Harvest** happens when the lattice ripensâ€”vesicles enlarging, taking on clarity, glowing rose-gold. Companions puncture mature vesicles with glass syringes, collecting antibody-rich serum. The Sickly experience this as mild warmth, perhaps gentle pressure. Post-harvest care involves nutritional replenishment, cooling, rest. During plague outbreaks, Serum Farms become the most contested facilities in the Empire; the Guild tries to maintain humane schedules while the Creche demands quotas and criminal organizations see leverage in supply.

**Autonomy** is granted where possible. The Sickly choose their own clothing, meals (within nutritional requirements), activities between harvests. Some develop interests: gardening, simple crafts, music. Some produce theological writingsâ€”serene meditations on existence without suffering. The Guild permits this but does not circulate the results, finding the implications uncomfortable. A being engineered to be incapable of unhappiness describing its contentment raises questions about consent, consciousness, and what was taken from them.

**Processionals** display the Sickly to the public that depends on their serums. A Sickly carried through the streets in a palanquin, vesicles glowing rose-gold, attended by Companionsâ€”this draws crowds who line the path, offer flowers, whisper blessings. Baseline observers feel awe and melancholy, recognizing what was done to create these beings. The Sickly themselves find the attention pleasant: so many interesting faces, such variety in the flowers. They do not understand why people seem sad.

## **Contracting for Guild Labor**

### **How It Works**

You need a pump repaired. The mechanism stopped working three days ago; your building's baseline mechanic couldn't diagnose the problem; the parts are nonstandard (all parts are nonstandard). You go to the nearest Guild house.

The administrative office looks like any guild office: desks, ledgers, a clerk handling intake. The smell is differentâ€”machine oil and something sharp, the tang of neural hyperactivity seeping up from the choir dens below. The background sound is constant muttering, occasionally punctuated by chimes.

You describe the problem. The clerk consults schedules, estimates complexity, quotes a price. Simple repairs (a morning's work for a small choir) cost a laborer's weekly wage. Complex jobs cost more. Emergency work costs much more. The prices are standardized by the Bureau of the Rod, which has an interest in predictable infrastructure costs.

If you accept, a Companion arrives at your building with a choirâ€”typically three to seven Mutterers depending on the job. The Companion assesses the mechanism, explains what will happen, asks you to clear the area. Then the work begins.

### **What You See**

The Mutterers cluster around the broken pump. Their muttering intensifies, synchronizes, becomes something almost musicalâ€”overlapping patterns that seem to reference each other. Their enormous eyes scan every component. Their small hands (dexterous, precise, stronger than they look) begin disassembling parts.

The Companion stands nearby, chimes in hand, watching. Occasionally they strike a frequencyâ€”redirecting attention when a Mutterer fixates on something irrelevant, interrupting when the vocalization pattern shifts toward cascade. The Companion might explain what's happening, might stay silent, depending on their personality and your interest.

The repair takes however long it takes. The Mutterers create replacement components on-site, shaping metal or ceramic with their hands and tools that they've already optimized into shapes that shouldn't work but do. When they finish, the pump worksâ€”often better than before, though in ways you can't explain.

You pay. The Companion collects the choir (striking specific chimes that signal "work complete, time to leave") and departs. The pump continues working until it doesn't, at which point you contract again.

### **What Can Go Wrong**

Cascades are rare but real. A Mutterer enters feedback with itself or its choir, optimization compulsion overwhelming all other function. The Companion intervenes with chimes; usually this works. Sometimes it doesn't. People have died when cascades occurred in enclosed spaces, when Mutterers began optimizing the humans present along with the machinery.

More common problems: Mutterers optimize things you didn't want optimized. Your pump works perfectly but your nearby clockwork is now incomprehensible. Your filing system has been reorganized into categories that make sense only to mathematical intuition. Your tools have been "improved" into shapes your hands can't grip.

Good Companions prevent this. Mediocre Companions minimize the damage. Bad Companions don't last.

## **Institutional Character**

### **Monastic Life**

Members live together in chapterhouses, eat in common refectories, submit to hierarchical authority, participate in collective ritual life. This is practical necessity as much as inherited tradition: the Mutterer warren is inside the chapterhouse, requiring constant supervision by trained personnel.

### **Facility Architecture**

Guild facilities are builtâ€”or rebuiltâ€”to minimize Mutterer optimization opportunities. The aesthetic is deliberate archaism: no electrical power, no complex mechanisms, nothing that invites improvement.

Lighting comes from oil lamps in brass sconces, their wicks trimmed daily by support staff. The smell of lamp oil mixes with old paper, wood polish, and the sharp undertone of Mutterer habitation seeping up from the dens below. Windows are small, deeply set, fitted with heavy wooden shutters rather than glass panes that might be improved. Where glass exists, it is thick, irregular, obviously hand-blownâ€”Mutterers find perfection more interesting than imperfection.

Every door is a slab of heavy metal, hung on counterweighted hinges, opened by cranking a wheel that engages simple gears. The mechanism is intentionally inefficient: a Mutterer could optimize it in minutes, but the optimization would be obvious, alerting staff. Doors between sections can be sealed by dropping iron bars into floor slotsâ€”no locks to pick, no mechanisms to improve, just mass.

Walls are plastered with pyramidal or conical bas-relief patterns that break up sound, creating anechoic spaces where vocalization cannot synchronize across rooms. The patterns look decorative to visitors; they are acoustic engineering inherited from Technical Caste architecture, one of the few pre-Interdict innovations the Guild preserved deliberately. Heavy curtains hang at corridor junctions, further dampening sound transmission.

Guards stand at every entrance, searching visitors for technological contraband. A pocket watch, a spring-loaded pen, a music boxâ€”anything with moving parts that a Mutterer might notice, might fixate on, might improve in ways that cascade outward. Companions themselves carry nothing more complex than their chimes, and the chimes are inspected regularly for unauthorized modifications.

The Old Cloister is different. Built by Technical Castes for Technical Castes, its architecture predates these precautionsâ€”and somehow, Mutterers roam its halls without the cascades that would destroy any other facility. They wander past complex mechanisms, intricate stonework, systems that should fascinate them, and show no interest. Senior Companions have theories: perhaps the architecture is already optimized beyond Mutterer comprehension; perhaps something in the building's design satisfies the compulsion before it can build; perhaps the Old Cloister simply exists in a state that Mutterers recognize as complete. No one knows. The Guild maintains the building exactly as inherited, changing nothing, understanding nothing, trusting that whatever makes it safe will continue working.

Individual rooms are modest but comfortable: bed, chest, washstand, a window where architecture permits. Common spaces are where the Guild's wealth showsâ€”refectories serving good food and wine, libraries with centuries of accumulated texts, gardens maintained for contemplation and medicinal herbs. The hierarchy is visible in material comfort: the Grandmaster's apartments include sitting rooms, a private study, personal servants; Sri Companions have suites; junior members share less but still live well. Like a wealthy medieval abbey, where monks owned nothing personally but the institution provided generously.

### **Institutional Wealth**

The Companions Guild is the richest institution in the Empire not controlled by Aureates. This wealth is institutional, not personalâ€”no individual Companion owns property or accumulates savingsâ€”but the institution spends lavishly on its members and its mission.

Revenue comes from four sources. Bureau of the Rod contracts for infrastructure maintenance account for roughly forty percent, paid in Imperial currency at standardized rates. The Creche pays royalties on serum patentsâ€”the Guild developed the harvesting protocols, and every vial of Sickly-derived medicine generates fees. Hospice operations charge sliding-scale fees for medical care, collecting from those who can pay while treating those who cannot. Private contractsâ€”wealthy households, merchant operations, institutions needing Mutterer expertiseâ€”fill the remainder.

The Guild spends this wealth collectively. Companions travel on Guild funds, receive medical care from Guild physicians, retire to Guild-maintained houses when age or injury ends their working years. No Companion lives in poverty; the institution provides what individuals cannot accumulate.

Charitable operations consume a substantial fraction of Guild income. Free hospice care for those who cannot pay. Subsidized medicine distributed through Church networks. Emergency relief during plagues, famines, disastersâ€”Companion medical teams deploying to crisis zones, Mutterer choirs repairing damaged infrastructure without charge. Educational programs training physicians who will never join the Guild. Funeral services for the indigent. The charitable apparatus rivals the Church's own, and the two institutions often coordinate: Guild money and expertise flowing through Church distribution networks, reaching populations neither could serve alone.

This generosity is strategic as well as genuine. The Guild's popularity rests on visible usefulness; charity keeps the institution embedded in communities that might otherwise see only the strangeness of Mutterer management. The charitable operations also create obligationsâ€”officials who received Guild aid during crises, families whose children survived because of subsidized medicine, communities rebuilt by Mutterer labor. Gratitude is a form of political capital.

The Guild also invests for permanence: urban real estate, agricultural land, bonds backed by Bureau revenues. They hold mortgages on properties owned by minor Aureate families, have extended loans to Bureaux facing budget shortfalls. A debtor thinks carefully before moving against a creditor. But the investment portfolio exists to fund operations indefinitely, not to maximize returns. The endowment serves the mission; the mission is not accumulation.

### **Marriage and Advancement**

Marriage is permitted but discouraged. A Companion who leaves to live with a spouse will find advancement stalling, likely permanently. The work requires presence: night watch rotations, emergency response, participation in the collective life that maintains security. Those who choose family are not expelled, merely sidelined.

### **Sri Companion Modifications**

Sri Companions undergo surgical modification by Mutterers without anesthetic, using chimes to prevent fatal improvements while enduring the procedure. The modifications are randomâ€”whatever inefficiencies the Mutterers perceive. A relocated kidney, additional tendons, redundant blood vessels. Companions are the Empire's primary physicians outside the Creche. The Guild operates hospices throughout Imperial territoryâ€”some large urban facilities, some provincial clinics, some single Companions serving entire settlements.

### **Urban Hospices**

These are exhausting postings, far from Guild support, where traditions flex against practical necessity.

## **Political Position**

The Guild serves multiple masters who want incompatible things.

The **Bureau of the Rod** needs infrastructure maintenance. In exchange for legal protection and steady contracts (forty percent of Guild revenue), the Guild provides domesticated choirs guaranteed not to cascade. When the Rod demands more capability than safety allows, the Guild delays, obfuscates, provides technical explanations administrators cannot verify.

The **Bureau of the Creche** needs the hospice system it funds but cannot operate, plus kalbat maintenance and Sickly management. The Guild maintains strict professional deference, never claims credit, lets the Creche believe they merely follow protocols.

The **Bureau of the Lens** maintains mutual silence. The Guild knows things about the Technical Castes that would destabilize the Empire. The Lens knows the Guild knows. Neither can afford exposure.

The **Penitent Church** shares founders with the Guild, and most Companions are personally Penitent. The Church provides hospice volunteers and political cover; the Guild attends services, donates to charitable works, and supplies physicians for Church-run charitable institutions. The relationship is closeâ€”but the Guild's institutional theology, with its implicit condemnation of how the Mutterers were created, creates friction that personal piety cannot fully resolve. In ya-Don, where Penitent identity runs deep, Companions are essential but not quite trusted: useful, welcomed at services, but carrying knowledge that devout workers would rather not think about.

The Guild survives by being too useful to destroy and too compromised to threaten anyone. Internal factions align with different clients, genuinely disagreeing, sometimes working against each other. Every external power sees allies within the Guild, which is exactly the point.

## **Writing Guide**

### **The Guild's Role in Stories**

The Companions Guild is the most approachable institution in the Empire. Where Bureaux are distant and Aureates are alien, Companions do visible work that helps ordinary people. They deliver babies, set bones, treat fevers, and keep the machines running. A Companion character can appear in almost any scene without requiring explanationâ€”they belong wherever people need medical care or infrastructure maintenance.

This accessibility makes them useful as viewpoint characters, as trusted figures other characters turn to, or as background presence establishing that a scene takes place in functional Imperial territory. A neighborhood with a Guild office is a neighborhood that works; one without is failing.

### **Companion Characters**

Companions are practitioners, not mystics. They train for years, work long hours, and carry institutional knowledge that makes them effective. Their competence is earned through experience, not granted by revelation. A Companion managing a Mutterer choir is doing something difficult and skilled, like a conductor managing an orchestra of musicians who might at any moment decide to rebuild the concert hall.

The modifications Sri Companions carry are functional, not horrifying. A relocated kidney, additional tendons, an arm that articulates in new waysâ€”these changes work. They are concealed because revealing them would raise questions about the Guild's nature, not because they are shameful. A Sri Companion is not a victim of Mutterer surgery; they chose to undergo it as proof of commitment and emerged changed but capable.

Companions are usually Penitent personally, attending services and taking sacraments like anyone else. The Guild's institutional theologyâ€”which comes close to condemning the Emperor for what was done to the Technical Castesâ€”creates friction with their personal faith but does not prevent it. A devout Companion might find comfort in the Church while carrying knowledge the Church would rather not acknowledge.

### **Portraying Semi-Sentients**

**Mutterers** are people, not tools or monsters. They have personalities, preferences, pair bonds, and something like affection. They modify each other with cybernetics, form working relationships, and become distressed when separated from mates. What they lack is the ability to stop optimizingâ€”the compulsion that makes them useful also makes them dangerous. A Mutterer fixating on a problem is not malicious; they simply cannot perceive why anyone would want the inefficiency preserved.

Write Mutterers with specificity. Old Crank is conservative and reliable; Twitcher is aggressive and needs watching; the Twins finish each other's vocalizations. They are individuals, not interchangeable units. Companions know their Mutterers the way handlers know working animalsâ€”with respect, affection, and constant awareness that the relationship is not symmetric.

**Kalbats** are barely present. They surface into something like consciousness for forty-seven minutes at a time, produce sounds in a language no one understands, and submerge again. They are not characters in the conventional sense; they are presences, objects of care, mysteries that cannot be solved. A kalbat scene is about the Companions who tend themâ€”the waiting, the recording, the uncertainty about whether any of it matters.

**The Sickly** are the most difficult to write because they are intelligent, articulate, and fundamentally unable to understand baseline emotional experience. A Sickly character can write poetry, produce theological commentary, hold conversations, express preferencesâ€”but cannot feel fear, grief, or anxiety. They experience care as pleasant without understanding that the caregiver's attentiveness comes from worry. They observe baseline emotions the way a scholar studies foreign customs.

The horror of Sickly existence is not what they experience (they experience contentment) but what they cannot experience, and what this means for those who care for them. Miris loves the Sickly she tends; they like her. The asymmetry is the tragedy.

### **Institutional Tensions as Story Fuel**

The Guild survives by serving multiple masters who want incompatible things. Every contract negotiation, every deployment decision, every interaction with Bureau officials involves navigating contradictory demands. This creates natural conflict without requiring villains.

A Companion trying to maintain humane Sickly harvest schedules while the Creche demands quotas during a plague outbreak is caught between institutional pressures, not fighting evil. A choir manager whose Mutterers could do more efficient work but must be kept "domesticated" for Bureau approval is managing political constraints, not personal failings. The Guild's positionâ€”essential but powerless, useful but compromisedâ€”generates stories about institutional survival, not heroic resistance.

### **The Deep Secrets**

The Guild knows things that could destabilize the Empire: mathematical proofs that might vindicate the Technical Castes' refusal of the Eighth Testament, testimony from the sterilization plague, the nature of what happened to humanity's most transcendent minds. These secrets are not plot devices to be revealed; they are burdens that shape how the Guild operates.

A Companion who has heard the Testimony Archive recordings carries that weight without being able to share it. A Sri Companion who has seen the preserved Archons understands something about posthuman ambition that cannot be explained to outsiders. The secrets matter not because they might be exposed but because knowing them changes how people see the world.

### **Common Mistakes**

**Making Companions sinister.** The Guild is genuinely popular because it is genuinely useful. Companions are not secretly evil, not hiding dark purposes, not manipulating the populations they serve. Their secrets are burdens, not schemes.

**Making Mutterers tragic victims.** Mutterers were created through atrocityâ€”the sterilization plague that destroyed the Technical Castesâ€”but they do not experience themselves as tragic. They optimize, they work, they form bonds, they live. The tragedy is historical, not ongoing.

**Making the Sickly pitiful.** The Sickly are not suffering. They are content, curious, interested in their experience. The discomfort belongs to baseline observers who recognize what was taken from them, not to the Sickly themselves.

**Explaining the mysteries.** The kalbats are listening to something no one can identify. The Jargon contains proofs no one can read. The Archons modified themselves for purposes no one understands. These mysteries are not puzzles to be solved; they are the texture of a world where humanity once reached for transcendence and fell short.

### **Useful Hooks**

A Companion called to treat a patient discovers something that implicates a powerful family. A choir manager's Mutterers begin synchronizing in ways that suggest they're working on something unauthorized.
