## **I. Phonology**

Ganati phonology blends features from three linguistic traditions: Ethiopian Semitic (ejectives, gemination, uvular fricatives), Coptic (the "ou" vowel, softer consonant clusters), and Navajo (voiceless laterals, glottal stops, certain affricates). The result is distinctive without requiring special characters beyond the apostrophe.

### **Consonants**

| Type | Sounds | Notes |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Plain stops | p, t, k, b, d, g | Standard |
| Ejectives | k', t' | Write with apostrophe after consonant. Emphatic; appear in older or formal names. Pronounced with a "popped" quality. |
| Fricatives | f, s, sh, kh, gh, h | "kh" is a uvular fricative (like German "Bach"). "gh" is its voiced counterpart (like French "r"). |
| Affricates | ts, ch | "ts" is distinctive to Ganati and appears frequently. |
| Laterals | l, lh | "lh" is a voiceless lateral fricative (Navajo influence). Pronounced by positioning tongue for "l" and blowing air past it without voicing. |
| Nasals | m, n | Standard. |
| Liquids | r | Single tap or light roll, not heavily trilled. |
| Glides | w, y | Standard. |
| Glottal | ' | Glottal stop. Appears between vowels or after certain consonants. A brief catch in the throat. |

**Gemination:** Doubled consonants indicate emphasis or formality. They are pronounced longer than single consonants. Write as: tt, kk, mm, ss, ll, nn, etc.

### **Vowels**

| Vowel | Notes |
| ----- | ----- |
| a, e, i, o, u | Clear, full vowels. Ganati does not reduce unstressed vowels the way English does. Each vowel is pronounced distinctly. |
| ou | A single vowel sound (Coptic influence), not a diphthong. Pronounced like "oo" in "food" but with lips less rounded. |
| ai, ei | Occur in older or formal names. Pronounced as written, with both vowels audible but forming a single syllable. |

### **Syllable Structure**

* **Preferred:** CV (consonant-vowel) or CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant)  
* **Allowed:** CVCC in compounds or formal names  
* **Initial clusters:** Permitted in kilit markers (Dhal-, Khen-, Tsel-)  
* **Final clusters:** Rare. Acceptable finals include \-kh, \-lh, \-ts

### **Stress**

Default stress falls on the penultimate (second-to-last) syllable. In compound names, each element receives its own stress.

## **II. Name Structure**

### **The Historical Four-Part Introduction (Archaic)**

Under the Sultanate, formal introductions located a person completely:

1. **Current kilit** — the economic unit where you work  
2. **Position** — your function within that kilit  
3. **Birth kilit** — the kilit your family originated from  
4. **Lineage name** — your clan

*Example:* "Yula of the Textile Enterprise, Factor, of Khen, of Masot"

The combination of birth kilit and lineage name (rendered as a hyphenated compound like "Khen-Masot") was the marker of kilit-zem-kilitam membership—the "kilit of kilits," an exclusive body that controlled inter-kilit arbitration, trade standards, and access to the Sultan's court. If you could give a compound name, you belonged to the old aristocracy.

Post-revolution, this structure became politically toxic. Using a compound name announces: *my ancestors were aristocrats*.

### **Modern Usage**

#### **Unmarked Names (Revolutionary Default)**

**Structure:** \[Given Name\] \[Lineage Name\]

**Examples:**

* Yem Tokan (Hereditary Debtor Senator)  
* Kemal Torin (Copper Road Syndicate)  
* Tewodo Khamun (Arch-Curate)  
* Temma Ghesam  
* Tsedam Toukham  
* Shenou Lhodam

**Social Signal:** "I am not claiming aristocratic heritage." This form is used by veterans, hereditary debtors, new settlers, frontier communities, and anyone wanting revolutionary credibility. Even people who possess compound names sometimes drop the marker in social contexts to avoid seeming elitist.

**Political Lean:** Dawn Party or neutral.

#### **Compound Names (Old Merchant Families)**

**Structure:** \[Given Name\] \[Kilit Marker\]-\[Clan Name\]

**Examples:**

* Rasma Dhal-Setil (Dawn Consul)  
* Yula Khen-Masot (Textile Factor)  
* Sekh Tal-Omir (Captain)  
* Senator Ul-Kemari (Hollow Seat holder)  
* Doram Khen-Toukh  
* Tolha Ghar-Lhoud

**Social Signal:** "My family has ancient kilit affiliation." This indicates established lineage, connection to pre-revolutionary economic structures, and probable wealth. The compound form persists among old merchant families, those whose commercial position requires signaling credit-worthiness, and the stubbornly proud.

**Political Lean:** Tends toward Dusk Party, though exceptions exist. Rasma Dhal-Setil serves as Dawn Consul despite her compound surname—she chose revolution over heritage, which some find admirable and others find suspicious.

#### **Imperial Descent Names**

**Structure:** \[Given Name\] \[Kilit Marker\] el-\[Clan Name\]

**Examples:**

* Datho Kel el-Marwen (Dusk Consul)  
* Ghetou Kel el-Gheram  
* Oukam Shem el-Youlam

**Social Signal:** "My family has traceable Imperial heritage predating the Sultanate." The el- particle derives from Old Imperial and marks near-baseline lineage with ancient Imperial roots. This is the most fraught name form—it announces not just kilit-zem-kilitam membership but a heritage that goes back to when this territory was still Imperial.

**Political Lean:** Strong Dusk affiliation. This name form is useful for certain Dusk constituencies but poison for Dawn voters.

**Frequency:** Less common than standard compound forms. Most Ganatis are Serrulata, and even among near-baseline populations (approximately 25% of the Republic), not all possess Imperial lineage ancient enough to preserve the particle.

## **III. Name Components**

### **Given Names**

Ganati given names are generally short, typically two syllables, though monosyllabic names occur. They favor hard consonants (k, t, d) balanced by liquid or nasal sounds (l, m, n, r), with clear vowels throughout.

**Canonical Examples:**

* Rasma, Datho, Yem, Kemal, Sekh, Tewodo, Yula, Kaleb, Arsakh

**Extended List (Organized by Phonological Feature):**

*Standard Pattern:* Rashod, Doram, Sefka, Yemkal, Talim, Kemra, Darsha, Bekla, Selam, Desta

*With Ethiopian Features (ejectives, gemination, kh/gh):* Temma, Ghedam, Tekla, K'eram, Mekdes, Gheram, Sekkam, Temmad

*With Coptic Features (ou vowel, softer feel):* Touma, Shenou, Boutam, Morkou, Yousam, Kirou, Oukam, Ghetou

*With Navajo Features (lh, ts, glottal stop):* Tolha, Tsedam, Atlha, Kelha, Da'am, Se'od, Tsekha, Lhekam

**Gender:** Given names are not grammatically gendered. Gender is marked through titles or context, not name form. However, certain names have acquired gender associations through usage patterns.

## **IV. Near-Baseline and Mixed Heritage Names**

Approximately 25% of Ganat's population is near-baseline human rather than Serrulata. This population's relationship to Imperial heritage is ancient, not recent—Ganat was independent under the Sultanate for centuries before the Revolution, and the Sultanate's independence from the Empire predates that by longer still. Near-baselines with "Imperial heritage" are not immigrants or defectors; they are families whose Imperial roots predate Ganati independence entirely, sometimes by millennia.

### **The el- Particle: Fossil of Ancient Heritage**

The el- connector in names like "Datho Kel el-Marwen" is not a sign of recent Imperial connection. It is a fossil—a linguistic remnant preserved across countless generations, marking families who were Imperial before this territory became the Sultanate, before the Sultanate became the Republic. These families have been Ganati (or proto-Ganati) for so long that everything about them is local except this single particle, preserved out of pride or habit or both.

**Structure:** \[Ganati Given Name\] \[Ganati Marker\] el-\[Ganati Clan Name\]

**Examples:**

* Datho Kel el-Marwen  
* Ghetou Kel el-Gheram  
* Touma Shem el-Youlam  
* Tsedam Ash el-Temmir

The el- particle is the only Imperial element. The given name, the marker, the clan name—all follow Ganati phonology entirely. The particle signals: "Our ancestors were here when this was Imperial territory, and we remember."

This is distinct prestige from mere kilit-zem-kilitam membership. A compound name like "Khen-Masot" says your family was Sultanate aristocracy. An el- name says your family predates the Sultanate.

### **Imperial Factors in Taho**

The Imperial factors who operate seasonally in Taho's Factor's Row are not Aureates. Aureates would never emigrate—their entire identity, wealth, and status depends on the Imperial system of authenticated naming and Lector-verified delegation. An Aureate outside the Empire is nothing; the es- particle that defines them cannot authenticate beyond Imperial territory.

The factors are instead:

* Merchants authorized to trade on behalf of Aureate families or Bureau enterprises  
* Minor Bureau functionaries assigned to commercial liaison  
* Non-Aureate Imperial subjects seeking opportunity in border trade

Their names follow non-Aureate Imperial patterns:

**Examples:**

* Varan Keloreth (patronymic pattern)  
* Sera Tavsaren (Bureau-adjacent family)  
* Miral Doshan (merchant family)

Such characters are immediately identifiable as outsiders. They do not typically adopt Ganati names—they are seasonal residents who return to Imperial territory, not immigrants. Their foreignness is part of their commercial function; Ganati merchants know exactly who they are dealing with.

### **Assimilating Imperials (Rare)**

Occasionally, an Imperial subject remains in Ganat permanently—a factor who married locally, a deserter, a political exile, someone who simply found life in the Republic preferable. This is uncommon. The Empire does not encourage emigration, the Republic views Imperial arrivals with suspicion, and most Imperials find Ganati society alien and uncomfortable.

Those who do assimilate typically adapt their names to Ganati phonology while preserving some Imperial structure:

**Pattern:** Imperial roots with Ganati sound changes applied.

* Final syllables acquire Ganati endings (-am, \-ou, \-kh)  
* Stress shifts to penultimate syllable  
* Vowels may shift toward Ganati inventory (ou for o, etc.)

**Examples:**

* "Varan Doshan" → "Varou Dosham"  
* "Sera Tavsaren" → "Sera Tafsaram"  
* "Miral Keloreth" → "Mirou Keloram"

These names mark their bearers as permanent outsiders—not seasonal factors, but not quite Ganati either. Their children, raised in the Republic, typically adopt fully Ganati names and may not preserve their parents' Imperial heritage at all.

This pattern is rare enough that encountering it prompts questions. Why did they stay? What did they leave behind? The answers are usually personal rather than political, but suspicion lingers regardless.

### **Near-Baseline Ganatis Without Imperial Markers**

Most near-baseline Ganatis have no el- particle because their families either:

* Were never Imperial (arrived after independence, or from non-Imperial populations)  
* Lost the marker through intermarriage with Serrulata families  
* Deliberately dropped it during the Revolution or earlier  
* Were low-status Imperial subjects who never had prestigious names to begin with

These near-baselines use standard Ganati naming conventions—compound names if they have kilit-zem-kilitam heritage, unmarked names if they don't. Their near-baseline appearance carries no automatic assumption of Imperial connection.

A near-baseline character named "Bekla Ghesam" with no compound surname is simply a Ganati whose family history doesn't include aristocratic or Imperial prestige. This is the majority case.

### **Revolutionary Name Changes**

Some near-baseline families with el- names deliberately dropped the particle after the Revolution, adopting standard compound or unmarked forms to signal political alignment:

**Pattern:** Preserved structure minus the Imperial marker.

* "Kel el-Marwen" → "Kel-Marwen" (standard compound)  
* "Shem el-Youlam" → "Youlam" (unmarked, dropped marker entirely)

This signals revolutionary commitment at the cost of ancient prestige. Children raised with the new form may not even know their family once used the particle.

### **Code-Switching (Limited)**

Unlike contexts where characters might shift between entirely different naming systems, Ganati code-switching is subtle:

* **Formal contexts:** Full name with all elements  
* **Casual contexts:** Given name plus lineage only, dropping marker  
* **Revolutionary contexts:** May drop el- particle even if legally still using it

A character might be "Ghetou Kel el-Gheram" in Senate records and "Ghetou Gheram" at a Dawn Party meeting.

## **V. Social Reading of Names**

### **Quick Reference Table**

| Name Form | Probable Background | Political Lean |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| \[Given\] \[Unmarked\] | Veteran, debtor, settler, worker, or deliberately de-classed | Dawn or neutral |
| \[Given\] \[Marker\]-\[Clan\] | Old merchant family, kilit-zem-kilitam descent | Likely Dusk |
| \[Given\] \[Marker\] el-\[Clan\] | Ancient pre-Sultanate heritage, near-baseline | Strong Dusk |
| Non-Aureate Imperial name | Seasonal factor, not permanent resident | Outsider (not immigrant) |
| Imperial name with Ganati adaptation | Rare permanent immigrant, assimilated | Suspect, variable |

### **Complications and Exceptions**

**Compound name, Dawn politics:** Rasma Dhal-Setil serves as Dawn Consul despite her elite surname. This can read as principled (chose revolution over privilege) or suspicious (infiltrator maintaining family interests). Her name is always slightly incongruous in Dawn contexts.

**Dropping the marker:** Someone introducing themselves as "Yula Masot" rather than "Yula Khen-Masot" is signaling something—revolutionary sympathies, desire to obscure background, or simple informality. Context matters.

**Worker with compound name:** A dock worker named "Tal-Omir" serving on the Dockers' Fraternal represents either a fallen family or is lying about their background. Either way, it raises questions.

**Near-baseline with unmarked name:** Either deliberate political statement (rejected heritage) or family was never prestigious to begin with. Cannot distinguish without further information.

## **VI. Generating New Names**

### **Process**

1. **Determine social position:** Revolutionary credibility? Old merchant family? Imperial heritage?  
2. **Select name form:** Unmarked, compound, or Imperial descent  
3. **Choose given name:** Two syllables, hard consonants balanced by liquids/nasals  
4. **If compound, choose marker:** Monosyllabic or disyllabic, often ending in consonant  
5. **If compound, choose clan name:** Two syllables, often ending in consonant or \-i  
6. **If unmarked, choose lineage name:** Two syllables, often ending in \-an, \-in, \-un, \-am  
7. **Consider phonological features:** Add ejectives, gemination, ou vowels, lh sounds, etc. for distinctiveness

## **VII. Pronunciation Guide**

| Spelling | Pronunciation | Example |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| kh | Like "ch" in German "Bach" | Khamun \= "KHA-moon" |
| gh | Voiced version of kh, like French "r" | Ghedam \= "GHEH-dam" |
| lh | Voiceless "l" — position for "l", blow without voice | Tolha \= "TOL-ha" (breathy l) |
| ts | Like "ts" in "cats" | Tsedam \= "TSEH-dam" |
| k' | "k" with a popped quality | K'eram \= "K'EH-ram" |
| t' | "t" with a popped quality | T'ak \= "T'AK" |
| ou | Like "oo" in "food" but less rounded | Touma \= "TOO-ma" |
| ' (mid-word) | Brief catch in throat | Da'am \= "DA-am" (glottal stop) |
| Double consonant | Held longer | Temma \= "TEM-ma" (long m) |

Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable unless otherwise marked.
