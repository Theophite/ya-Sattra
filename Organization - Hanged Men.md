# The Hanged Men

> **Type:** Criminal organization controlling water infrastructure
> **Territory:** Middens district (ya-Sattra)
> **Structure:** The Knot (7 seats, 6 filled) leading three crews
> **Origin:** Revolutionary war veterans who refused to disarm after the Malpais War

The Hanged Men evolved from militia to protection racket over three centuries. Their power derives from the Lockworks—control water distribution, control everything.

## The Knot: Seven Seats of Power

### 1. The Chairman: Luka Fen

**Age:** 42 | **Territory:** The Gallows, overall authority | **Bloodline:** Seventh-generation direct descent from Garren Fen I

At 2.40 meters, Luka's height comes from Springheel elongation—double-jointed in every direction. Face abandons baseline humanity: no exterior ears, nose reduced to flattened vents, mouth a lipless seam. Pale Warborn nodules ridge his brow. Black waxed-canvas coat bears dozens of brass and ceramic buttons, each taken from a defeated rival.

Luka occupies the seat through hereditary succession but maintains it through careful balance:
- Springheel-dominant genetics grant physical capability to outmaneuver Garren
- Genuine Penitent faith provides moral legitimacy that older Valvehands respect
- Understands infrastructure through practical knowledge—morning inspections with Riva aren't performance but actual technical work
- Protects Curate Aram not from sentiment but calculation about martyrdom's consequences

### 2. The Iron Yards Commander: Jarek Fen

**Age:** 39 | **Territory:** Iron Yards salvage operations | **Bloodline:** First cousin to Luka, competing branch

2.50 meters tall but lean as a drawn blade. Face even more inhuman than Luka's—nose a flat ridge, ears just depressions, mouth a lipless slash. Chain-link scarification from cheekbone to jaw, each ridge representing a year of command.

Controls the Hanged Men's primary revenue stream through systematic extraction from salvage. Bottomless cynicism—sees all complexity as fraud, all expertise as gatekeeping. Can eat dinner while Chain-Men pull fingers off debtors because suffering is just noise unless it affects revenue. Relies on Garren's violence while positioning himself as the rational alternative to both Luka's restraint and Garren's chaos.

### 3. The Chain-Men Commander: Garren Fen XI

**Age:** 37 | **Territory:** Bright-Spire, enforcement | **Bloodline:** Warborn-Dominant, second cousin to Luka

At 2.85 meters and 160 kilograms, Garren represents the Ironbone variant's terminal expression—overlapping pale bone plates cover brow, cheeks, jaw, shoulders, forearms, and knuckles like natural armor grown wrong. Face has abandoned human proportions: no ears at all, nose reduced to ventilation slits, mouth locked half-open by oversized jaw.

**Terminal generation of Ironbone breeding.** Will be dead from cancer by forty. Experiences Warborn rage as divine inspiration rather than neurological dysfunction. Has killed three wives—the Knot covered it up. His Chain-Men follow him because he's the biggest monster in a band of monsters. Jarek points him at problems; Garren breaks them.

### 4. The Lockworks Captain: Riva Fen-Cho

**Age:** 36 | **Territory:** Water infrastructure | **Bloodline:** Half-Fen, half-sewer-fisher, probably infertile

At 1.97 meters, appears stretched and compressed simultaneously—elongated limbs attached to a torso that seems folded in on itself. Graphite-grey skin with petroleum sheen. Eyes full black sclera with gun-metal grey limbal rings. Blade-long ears pierced with ascending rows of copper rings.

**The lynchpin.** Unique hybrid genetics make her irreplaceable for maintaining infrastructure requiring both Springheel reach and sewer-fisher compression ability. Her arthritis worsens daily; she has no successor. Loyal to Luka alone because he's the only Knot member who understands that when she fails, everything fails.

### 5. The Master of Counting House: Ygren Fen

**Age:** 65 | **Territory:** Financial operations | **Bloodline:** Springheel/Ironbone, distant cousin

2.05 meters, showing softening of age overlaying a frame built for loading cargo. Diluted Fen modifications: nose flattened but not severely, small ears still present. Drab ledgersman's coat, brass abacus bracelet projecting respectability.

Operates as the Knot's institutional memory and accountant. His son Derren's rise in the Salvage Guild represents his only hope for legitimacy. Drinks alone at night, reconciling what he does against the revenue which buys his son's escape.

### 6. The Night Watch Captain: Sera Fen-Galen

**Age:** 31 | **Territory:** Intelligence, surveillance | **Bloodline:** No Fen blood—married to Ruk Fen (Luka's younger brother)

1.78 meters, baseline human among genetic extremes. Face achieves perfect forgettability. Only pale blue irises with faint reflective rings suggest variance. Charcoal coat conceals hidden pockets for two Lectors; silver cipher-wheel bracelet is Lens-issued encoding device.

**Triple agent:** Galen family connection to Hanged Men, Bureau of the Lens Asset LS-4-113, primary loyalty to continued operation. Receives weekly Grey Sheet briefings from the Lens, filtering intelligence to serve multiple agendas. Lack of genetic stake makes her paradoxically trustworthy—she'll betray everyone equally based on rational calculation. Takes contraceptives from three suppliers.

### 7. The Empty Seat

**Vacant:** 12 years since Korvain Fen died in a "construction accident"

The seventh seat remains empty because filling it would require consensus the Knot cannot achieve:
- Luka wants someone to balance Jarek's aggression
- Jarek wants an ally to tip votes
- Garren doesn't understand why they need seven when six works fine

**The empty seat has become a useful fiction:** a position to promise potential allies, a threat to existing captains, a convenient excuse for deadlock.

## The Three Crews

### Valvehands
Water infrastructure maintenance. Operate the Lockworks under Riva's technical direction. Real work, not just enforcement—they understand the systems that keep the district alive.

### Iron Yards Boys
Jarek's primary profit center. Extract percentages from salvage operations. When enforcement requires more muscle than the Boys provide, Chain-Men come down from Bright-Spire.

### Chain-Men
Ironbone Warborn enforcers occupying Bright-Spire. Terminal variant approaching 3 meters, bone plates covering face and body. Chosen violence, not compelled by biology—they could be anything, but they choose to be monsters. Pure enforcement without service provision rationale.

## Origin: The Malpais War

### The Conscription

During the war against the Child of the Malpais, desperate Imperial conscription pulled in every able-bodied human, including mixed-caste Middens laborers. The original Garren Fen commanded a unit of Ironback dock workers and Springheel scouts—assigned to hold the outer defensive arrays of the City of Glass. Essentially sacrificial positions.

**Instead, they held for three days.** The mixed unit's caste advantages proved unexpectedly effective. They were part of the desperate defense that lasted long enough for the sewer-fishers to activate something within the City of Glass itself.

### The Refusal to Disarm

When standard demobilization orders came, Garren Fen's veterans understood what compliance meant—they'd proven mixed-caste units could be militarily effective. They would be seen as threats.

The unit simply didn't report. They kept their weapons and fortified the industrial district. The "revolt" lasted less than a week with no actual combat. The exhausted Empire chose to negotiate.

### The City-of-Glass Charter

The compromise: veterans could keep weapons and form local militias, but Warborn production would be limited, weapons manufacture regulated, and restrictions placed on Warborn genetics.

Over three centuries, the militia became protection racket. Revolutionary credentials became hereditary privilege. Technical knowledge became power through water control.

## Current Power Dynamics

**Luka vs Jarek:** The fundamental tension. Luka favors stability and careful extraction; Jarek wants expansion and maximization. Neither can remove the other without destroying the balance.

**Garren as weapon:** Jarek points him at problems. Garren breaks them. Neither understands why this might be unsustainable.

**Riva as linchpin:** Her failing health creates succession crisis. No one can replace her unique biology. When she dies, the Lockworks maintenance depends on... who? The Chos will demand a higher price.

**Sera as wild card:** She serves everyone and no one. Her continued usefulness depends on being useful to multiple parties. The day she becomes more liability than asset to any one of them is the day everything shifts.
